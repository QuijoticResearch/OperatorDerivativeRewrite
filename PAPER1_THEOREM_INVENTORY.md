## Explicit adversarial checks supporting the inventory

1. **Main F:** F=B_T^H, s=0. Its proposed coefficient is zero, whereas R_(0,t)=E[B_T^H given F_t^H] has second moment at least R_H(T,t)²/t^(2H). This is of order t^(2H) for H<1/2 and t^(2−2H) for H>1/2, contradicting the stated rates. The example is first chaos and lies in every finite-order Malliavin Sobolev class. A separate diagnostic of the claimed uniform mechanism is H=1/2: P_s D(W_T)=1_[0,s] is orthogonal to k_t−k_s=1_(s,t], while the conditional-expectation increment has variance t−s, not O((t−s)²). This Brownian diagnostic is outside Main F's expressly excluded H=1/2 case; the preceding fBM counterexample is the direct refutation.
2. **Itô projection step:** for Brownian W and φ=sin, E[cos(W_t) given F_s]=exp(−(t−s)/2)cos(W_s), not cos(W_s). Replacing the Clark–Ocone integrand as in869 loses the genuine drift term.
3. **Trace correction:** in two independent Gaussian coordinates, u=(X₂,−X₁) has δu=0 but E‖u‖²+E‖Du‖²_HS=4. The correct trace correction is −2.
4. **L² products:** under uniform measure on (0,1), f=g=x^(−1/3) belong to L² but fg does not. This invalidates `rem:domain` and any global pointwise-L² algebra realization.
5. **Meyer claim:** a compactly supported f=χ abs(x)^(2/3), with χ=1 near zero, has Gaussian weak derivative in L² but not L⁴, while f is bounded. Thus Gaussian D¹,²∩L⁴ is strictly larger than D¹,⁴.
6. **Predictable versus full derivative:** for Brownian terminal W_T, J*(W_T²)_t=2W_t while D^Mall(W_T²)_t=2W_T. The predictable adjoint's Leibniz defect for F=G=W_T is 2(W_t−W_T), already nonzero in the standard Brownian setting.
