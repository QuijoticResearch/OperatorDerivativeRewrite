\begin{abstract}
For independent Brownian motions $B,W$, let $Z_t=B(|W_t|)$ and
$A_T=\int_0^T Z_s\,ds$. Although $Z_T$ is outside the original clock
Gaussian Sobolev domain at every positive time, $A_T$ belongs to the
intersection of the two original Gaussian derivative domains. A folded
Gaussian transition bound gives a uniform finite clock energy for spatially
mollified averages, and closedness gives the actual average-domain result.
Bounded smooth functions of averages form a dense derivative domain in
each fixed raw observed past. For the resulting closed derivative and its
maximal Hilbert adjoint, the embedded gradient of $A_T$ is differentiable
in adjoint-graph-dual norm, including left differentiation at the past
endpoint. Its derivative pairs with actual $Z_T$ but has no native field
representative. Along every vanishing positive or negative time-step
sequence, the native gradient-slope norms diverge. At a fixed horizon,
contractions with each fixed clock direction have actual spatial occupation
kernels whose mollifications converge strongly; full-vector strong convergence for those linear approximants is not inferred. Separately, the nonlinear sine time average belongs to the same original joint domain, and both its native gradient channels converge strongly in full Hilbert norm. A compact spatial operator bound, summable occupation trace and uniform cutoff tail prove the vector limit. These results give
precise source-valued operator identities, not an observed-filtration
It\^o or Clark--Ocone formula. They reconstruct frozen formal results;
novelty remains unresolved.
\end{abstract}

\section{The process, operator and statement}
Let $B$ be a two-sided standard Brownian scenery and $W$ an independent
one-sided standard Brownian motion, on their product Gaussian probability
space $(\Omega,\mathcal F,P)$. Put
\[
 Z_t=B(|W_t|),\qquad A_T=\int_0^T Z_s\,ds\quad(t,T\geq0).
\]
Only the positive scenery branch is used by $Z$. Extending it independently
to negative arguments permits spatial convolution. This is the simple
Brownian-time Brownian model of~\cite{AZ}; an excursion does not select a
fresh scenery, and the clock is reflected. The signed-clock process
$B(W_t)$ is a different model.

Let $D_B,D_W$ be the original closed Gaussian gradients on the product
space, acting respectively in the scenery and clock factors. Their
coefficient Hilbert spaces can be realized as $H_B=L^2(\R)$ and
$H_W=L^2(\R_+)$, using white noise. Equivalently one can use the frozen
library's isometric separable Gaussian coordinates. Additional unused
independent coordinates tensor the graph with constants; averaging over
them gives the reverse domain implication. All fields below retain the
ambient Gaussian codomain
\[
 \G=L^2(P;H_B)\oplus L^2(P;H_W).
\]
They are not assumed observable or predictable in physical time.

Set $E=L^2(\sigma(Z_s:s\geq0),P)$, understood as the closed subspace of
$L^2(P)$ with measurable representatives, and define the \emph{native}
operator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:domain}
 \D f=(D_Bf,D_Wf),\qquad
 D(\D)=E\cap D(D_B)\cap D(D_W).
\end{equation}
The domain is the entire stated intersection. For fixed $H\geq0$ let
\[
 \mathcal F_H^Z=\sigma(Z_s:0\leq s\leq H),\qquad
 E_H=L^2(\mathcal F_H^Z,P),\qquad \D_H=\D|_{E_H}.
\]
This is the raw observed past; $L^2$ always identifies almost-everywhere
equal variables. Taking that quotient does not assert right continuity
of the filtration.

For a closed densely defined operator $T:E_0\supset D(T)\to\G$, write
\[
 \mathcal T_T=D(T^*),\quad
 \|v\|_{\mathcal T_T}^2=\|v\|_{\G}^2+\|T^*v\|_{E_0}^2,
\]
and define continuous linear functionals
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual}
 (j_TV)(v)=\langle V,v\rangle,\qquad
 (d_Tf)(v)=\langle f,T^*v\rangle.
\end{equation}
The adjoint domain here is maximal: $v\in D(T^*)$ exactly when
$f\mapsto\langle v,Tf\rangle$ is bounded in the $E_0$ norm on $D(T)$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
The operators $\D$ and $\D_H$ are closed and densely defined. For every
$T\geq0$, $A_T\in D(\D)$; if $T\leq H$, also $A_T\in D(\D_H)$ and
$\D_H A_T=\D A_T$. Write $V_T=\D A_T$. For every $T>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fulltime}
 \frac{d}{dT}j_{\D}V_T=d_{\D}Z_T
 \quad\hbox{in }\mathcal T_{\D}'\hbox{ norm}.
\end{equation}
For $0<T\leq H$ and every $h_n<0$ with $h_n\to0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:left}
 j_{\D_H}\frac{V_{T+h_n}-V_T}{h_n}
 \longrightarrow d_{\D_H}Z_T
 \quad\hbox{in }\mathcal T_{\D_H}'\hbox{ norm},
\end{equation}
where the finitely many terms with $T+h_n<0$ may be omitted. The limit is
nonzero and has no representative $j_{\D_H}V$ with $V\in\G$. Moreover
\begin{equation}\label{eq:escape}
 \left\|\frac{V_{T+h_n}-V_T}{h_n}\right\|_{\G}\longrightarrow\infty.
\end{equation}
The full operator has the same nonrepresentability, and
\eqref{eq:escape} also holds for every positive vanishing step sequence.
For each $v\in D(\D_H^*)$ the fixed-past derivative has the actual pairing
\begin{equation}\label{eq:actual}
 (d_{\D_H}Z_T)(v)=\E[Z_T\,\D_H^*v].
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The proof separates average-domain membership, density on the observed
past, and the change of topology needed for time differentiation. None
of these steps assumes a Gaussian law for $Z$ or a predictable
representation property for its observed filtration.

\section{Time continuity and scenery differentiation}
Let $e_r=\mathbf1_{(0,r]}\in H_B$ for $r\geq0$. The scenery white-noise
isometry sends $e_r$ to $B(r)$, and
\[
 \|B(r)-B(q)\|_{L^2(B)}^2=|r-q|\quad(r,q\geq0).
\]
Conditioning on the entire clock gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:continuity}
 \|Z_t-Z_s\|_2^2
 =\E\bigl||W_t|-|W_s|\bigr|
 \leq\E|W_t-W_s|
 =\sqrt{2/\pi}\,|t-s|^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
Consequently $Z$ is continuous as an $L^2(P)$-valued curve. It is Bochner
integrable on compact time intervals, and the integral agrees almost
surely with the integral of its continuous paths, by Fubini. The
fundamental theorem of calculus gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:FTC}
 A'_T=Z_T\quad\hbox{in }L^2(P),\qquad T>0.
\end{equation}
The same derivative holds in the closed subspaces $E$ and, from the
left for $T\leq H$, $E_H$.

For fixed clock sample, $B(|W_t|)$ is first Gaussian chaos in $B$, with
scenery gradient $e_{|W_t|}$. This gives the product closed graph and
\[
 \|D_BZ_t\|_{L^2(P;H_B)}^2=\E|W_t|=\sqrt{2t/\pi}.
\]
Here graph membership is genuine: approximate $e_r$ by finite orthogonal
projections in the scenery coefficient space, and use dominated
convergence with majorant $|W_t|$ for the squared errors. The finite
linear Gaussian graphs converge strongly to the stated product graph.
Integrating that graph yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sourceaverage}
 A_T\in D(D_B),\qquad
 D_BA_T=\int_0^T e_{|W_s|}\,ds.
\end{equation}
Indeed both value and gradient are Bochner integrable: the gradient norm
is a constant times $s^{1/4}$, which is integrable on $[0,T]$.
A closed linear graph contains the integral of any integrable curve
in that graph, by approximation with simple graph-valued functions.

\section{The folded transition estimate and average clock domain}
Choose the frozen nonnegative smooth probability mollifiers $\rho_n$,
supported in $[-\epsilon_n,\epsilon_n]$, $\epsilon_n=2/(n+1)$, and let
\[
 B_n(r)=\int\rho_n(u)B(r-u)\,du,\quad
 Z_t^{(n)}=B_n(|W_t|),\quad A_T^{(n)}=\int_0^T Z_s^{(n)}\,ds.
\]
The Brownian increment metric and Minkowski's inequality give, uniformly
in $r\in\R$,
\[
 \|B_n(r)-B(r)\|_{L^2(B)}
 \leq\int\rho_n(u)\sqrt{|u|}\,du\leq\sqrt{\epsilon_n}.
\]
Independence therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:averageerror}
 \|A_T^{(n)}-A_T\|_2\leq T\sqrt{\epsilon_n}.
\end{equation}
These mollified approximants need not be observed functions of $Z$;
they are used only in the original ambient clock graph.

Put $q_s=\mathbf1_{(0,s]}\in H_W$ and $\operatorname{sgn}(0)=0$.
In the original product Gaussian clock domain,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clockgraph}
 D_W Z_s^{(n)}=B_n'(|W_s|)\operatorname{sgn}(W_s)q_s,
 \qquad
 G_T^{(n)}=D_W A_T^{(n)}=\int_0^T D_WZ_s^{(n)}\,ds.
\end{equation}
To justify the point graph without assuming the scenery path has a
classical derivative before mollification, expand $B_n(r)$ in a scenery
orthonormal basis. Its scalar coefficients have derivative in the
original one-dimensional Gaussian graph after $r=|W_s|$. The absolute
value has weak derivative $\operatorname{sgn}$; approximating it by
$\sqrt{x^2+\delta^2}$ and then taking the Gaussian closed graph proves
that assertion. For the scenery sums, the square norms of the derivative
coefficients sum to $\|\rho_n\|_2^2$, uniformly in $r$. Thus the value
and derivative sums converge in the product $L^2$ graph. At $s=0$ the
random value is clock independent and the derivative is zero. On $[0,T]$
the derivative norms are bounded by $\sqrt{s}\|\rho_n\|_2$, so graph
integration gives the second equality in \eqref{eq:clockgraph}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:folded}
For every $n$ and $T\geq0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:energy}
 \|G_T^{(n)}\|_{L^2(P;H_W)}^2
 \leq\frac8{\sqrt{2\pi}}T^{5/2}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By the scenery white-noise isometry,
\[
 \E_B[B_n'(r)B_n'(q)]
 =k_n(r,q):=\int_{\R}\rho_n(r-x)\rho_n(q-x)\,dx\geq0.
\]
For fixed $r$, $\int_{\R}k_n(r,q)\,dq=1$ by Tonelli and normalization.
For $0\leq s<t$, conditional on $W_s=y$, the density of $|W_t|$ on
$q>0$ is
\[
 p_{t-s}(q-y)+p_{t-s}(-q-y)
 \leq\frac2{\sqrt{2\pi(t-s)}}.
\]
Hence $\E k_n(|W_s|,|W_t|)\leq2/\sqrt{2\pi(t-s)}$.
In the double integral for the squared norm of \eqref{eq:clockgraph},
the coefficient inner product is $\langle q_s,q_t\rangle=\min(s,t)$.
After scenery averaging, the signs have absolute value at most one and
$k_n\geq0$. Thus off the time diagonal the absolute integrand is bounded
by
\[
 \frac{2T}{\sqrt{2\pi}}|t-s|^{-1/2}.
\]
For each fixed mollifier, its actual diagonal covariance is finite and
is not assigned zero. The diagonal is a product-Lebesgue null set, so it
does not affect integration. The bound is integrable, since
\[
 \int_0^T|t-s|^{-1/2}\,dt
 =2\sqrt{s}+2\sqrt{T-s}\leq4\sqrt T.
\]
Integrating over $s\in[0,T]$ proves \eqref{eq:energy}. The same domination
justifies the double integrals and conditioning. The constant is a
convenient uniform estimate, not a sharp energy formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:average}
The actual average $A_T$ belongs to $D(\D)$ for every $T\geq0$. Its
original clock gradient satisfies
$\|D_WA_T\|_2^2\leq8T^{5/2}/\sqrt{2\pi}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $T=0$ the result is immediate. Otherwise \eqref{eq:averageerror}
gives strong value convergence, and Lemma~\ref{lem:folded} bounds the
actual clock gradients. A bounded sequence in this Hilbert field space
has a weakly convergent subsequence, say $G_T^{(n_k)}\rightharpoonup G$.
The graph of the closed linear operator $D_W$ is a norm-closed subspace,
and hence is weakly closed: a point outside it can be separated by its
orthogonal projection. Therefore
$(A_T^{(n_k)},G_T^{(n_k)})\rightharpoonup(A_T,G)$ lies in that graph.
It follows that $A_T\in D(D_W)$, and lower semicontinuity of the norm
gives the estimate. Equation~\eqref{eq:sourceaverage} gives scenery-domain
membership. Finally $A_T$ is observable from $Z$ and is an element of $E$,
which proves \eqref{eq:domain} membership.
\end{proof}

Uniform derivative bounds alone have been used for membership, not to
assert strong gradient convergence. The library has a further strong
directional occupation-kernel convergence theorem, proved separately below;
it is unnecessary for the time-differentiation result and does not assert
full-vector strong convergence.

\section{Density in the fixed raw observed past}
Closedness of $\D$ follows from closedness of both partial graphs and of
$E$. Its restriction $\D_H$ is closed because $E_H$ is closed. Density
requires proof; it does not follow merely from restricting a dense
ambient domain.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:chain}
Finite linear combinations of $A_t$, $0\leq t\leq H$, belong to
$D(\D_H)$. Their sine and cosine also belong to that domain.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Linearity and Proposition~\ref{prop:average} prove the first assertion;
all averages up to $H$ are $\mathcal F_H^Z$-measurable. For either
Gaussian factor, the closed gradient satisfies the bounded $C^1$ chain
rule $D\phi(f)=\phi'(f)Df$ when $\phi,\phi'$ are bounded and $\phi'$
is continuous. To see this from the original closure, take smooth
cylindrical graph approximants $f_n\to f$, $Df_n\to Df$. Lipschitz
continuity gives $\phi(f_n)\to\phi(f)$ in $L^2$. The derivative
products converge: the part containing $Df_n-Df$ is bounded by
$\|\phi'\|_\infty\|Df_n-Df\|_2$, and the part containing
$(\phi'(f_n)-\phi'(f))Df$ tends to zero by convergence in probability
and bounded dominated convergence. A subsequence argument gives the
same limit for the whole sequence. Smooth compositions in finitely many
Gaussian coordinates belong to the original closure: cut off the
coordinates, identify the weak partial derivatives by Gaussian
integration by parts, and expand in tensor Hermite polynomials. The
derivative coefficient identities and Parseval show that polynomial
partial sums converge with all first derivatives in $L^2$. Cutoff errors
vanish by Gaussian integrability and the bounded derivative. Thus the
finite-dimensional chain identities hold in the original closed graph,
and closedness proves the asserted chain rule. Apply it in each factor
to sine and cosine. Their
compositions remain observable in $\mathcal F_H^Z$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:dense}
$D(\D_H)$ is dense in $E_H$, including at $H=0$, and $D(\D)$ is dense
in $E$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $0<t\leq H$ and $0<\epsilon<t$, put
$Q_{t,\epsilon}=(A_t-A_{t-\epsilon})/\epsilon$. By
\eqref{eq:continuity}, $Q_{t,\epsilon}\to Z_t$ in $L^2$ as
$\epsilon\downarrow0$. Thus Lemma~\ref{lem:chain} and Lipschitz
continuity show that the sine and cosine of every finite linear
combination of points $Z_{t_1},\ldots,Z_{t_m}$ lie in the $L^2$ closure
of $D(\D_H)$. Zero-time points cause no difficulty since $Z_0=0$.
Constants are in the domain with zero gradient.

Suppose $g\in E_H$ is orthogonal to $D(\D_H)$. It is integrable by
Cauchy--Schwarz. For each finite observation vector
$X=(Z_{t_1},\ldots,Z_{t_m})$, the finite signed measure
\[
 \lambda(S)=\E[g\mathbf1_{\{X\in S\}}]
\]
has all Fourier transforms zero, by the preceding sine/cosine
approximation. Fourier uniqueness implies $\lambda=0$. One can verify
uniqueness by convolving $\lambda$ with a centered Gaussian density:
Fourier inversion for the Gaussian and Fubini make each such convolution
zero; Gaussian approximate identities then recover the integrals of
compactly supported continuous functions, and hence the finite measure.
Therefore $\E[g\mathbf1_A]=0$ for every finite observation cylinder $A$.

These cylinders form a pi-system generating the raw past. The signed
measure $A\mapsto\E[g\mathbf1_A]$ has total mass zero; the pi-lambda
theorem makes it zero on the whole generated sigma-algebra. Since $g$
has a past-measurable representative, taking its positive and negative
sets gives $\E|g|=0$. Hence $g=0$. The orthogonal complement is zero,
which proves density. When $H=0$, $E_0$ consists of constants and the
conclusion is immediate. The same argument with arbitrary finitely
many nonnegative times proves density in $E$.
\end{proof}

This proof uses the actual finite observation laws; it does not replace
them with Gaussian laws or identify conditional expectation with a
source-chaos projection.

\section{Why positive-time points remain outside the domain}
For clarity we recall the clock obstruction underlying the time result,
with its common-field requirement. In the scenery-valued Hilbert space
$K=L^2(B)$, for $c>0$ consider $F_c(x)=B(c|x|)$.
For $0\leq a<x$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:metric}
 \|F_c(x)-F_c(a)\|_K^2=c(x-a).
\end{equation}
If $F_c$ had a locally square-integrable Bochner weak derivative $g$,
its continuous representative would satisfy
$F_c(x)-F_c(a)=\int_a^x g(u)\,du$. Cauchy--Schwarz and
\eqref{eq:metric} would give
\[
 c\leq\int_a^x\|g(u)\|_K^2\,du.
\]
The right side tends to zero as $x\downarrow a$, a contradiction.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:point}
For every $T>0$, $Z_T\notin D(D_W)$ and therefore $Z_T\notin D(\D)$.
If $T\leq H$, also $Z_T\notin D(\D_H)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Set $G=W_T/\sqrt T$, a standard Gaussian coordinate, and
$h=\mathbf1_{(0,T]}/\sqrt T\in H_W$. Decompose the remaining clock
Gaussian coordinates orthogonally to $h$. If a full original clock
gradient $V$ existed, contract it with $h$ and conditionally average over
those remaining clock coordinates. Jensen gives a common field
$g\in L^2(\gamma;K)$, where $\gamma$ is standard Gaussian measure.
The product Gaussian integration-by-parts identity, tested against a
scenery variable $e\in K$ and a compactly supported smooth scalar
$p(G)$, gives
\[
 \int\langle F_{\sqrt T}(x),e\rangle(p'(x)-xp(x))\,d\gamma(x)
 =-\int\langle g(x),e\rangle p(x)\,d\gamma(x).
\]
This identity follows first on the Gaussian core and passes to the
closed graph by Cauchy--Schwarz. The recovered $g$ is the same field
for every $e$, a fact essential to the conclusion.

Let $\gamma(x)$ also denote the strictly positive Gaussian density.
Substitute $p=\psi/\gamma$ on any compact interval. Since
$\gamma'=-x\gamma$, one has $(p'-xp)\gamma=\psi'$. The displayed
identity becomes the distributional weak derivative identity relative
to Lebesgue measure. On that interval $g$ is square integrable because
the Gaussian density is bounded below. Testing a countable dense set
of scenery vectors shows that
$F_{\sqrt T}(x)-\int_a^xg(u)\,du$ is one constant $K$-valued vector
almost everywhere. Indeed every scalar projection has zero weak
derivative; separability and a common full-measure set identify that
vector. Both sides have continuous representatives, so the equality
holds throughout the interval. This contradicts \eqref{eq:metric}.
The two native exclusions follow from their domain inclusions.
\end{proof}

This is the point obstruction from the companion~\cite{clock}. The
scenery gradient in Section 2 remains valid; a clock chain rule is not
applied to a point outside its domain.

\section{Adjoint-graph duals and time-slope escape}
We give the closed-operator facts explicitly to fix the test space.
For $T:E_0\supset D(T)\to\G$ closed and densely defined, the maps in
\eqref{eq:dual} satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounds}
 \|d_Tf\|_{\mathcal T_T'}\leq\|f\|_{E_0},\quad
 j_T(Tu)=d_Tu,\quad
 \|j_T(Tu)-d_Tf\|_{\mathcal T_T'}\leq\|u-f\|_{E_0}.
\end{equation}
The first bound is Cauchy--Schwarz and $\|T^*v\|\leq\|v\|_{\mathcal T_T}$;
adjointness gives the equality, and applying the first bound to $u-f$
gives the last estimate.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:representation}
The map $j_T$ is injective, and $d_Tf=j_TV$ for some $V\in\G$ if and
only if $f\in D(T)$, in which case $V=Tf$. If
$u_n\in D(T)$ converges to $f\notin D(T)$ in $E_0$, then
$\|Tu_n\|_{\G}\to\infty$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The orthogonal complement of the graph is
\[
 \Gamma(T)^\perp=\{(-T^*v,v):v\in D(T^*)\}.
\]
This follows directly by testing
$\langle x,u\rangle+\langle y,Tu\rangle=0$ on $u\in D(T)$;
density makes $x=-T^*y$ unique. The relation $d_Tf=j_TV$ says precisely
that $(f,V)$ is orthogonal to this complement. Since the graph is
closed, $(f,V)\in\Gamma(T)$. The converse is adjointness. Taking
$f=0$ proves injectivity of $j_T$.

If $\|Tu_n\|$ did not tend to infinity, a subsequence would have bounded
outputs and hence a further weakly convergent subsequence, say to $V$.
Then $(u_n,Tu_n)$ along that subsequence converges weakly to $(f,V)$.
The closed linear graph is weakly closed, so $f\in D(T)$,
a contradiction. This proves full norm escape, rather than mere
unboundedness of one subsequence.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Propositions~\ref{prop:average} and \ref{prop:dense}, together with
closedness already noted, give the operator assertions. Put
$V_t=\D A_t$. Adjointness gives $j_{\D}V_t=d_{\D}A_t$. The bounded
linear map $d_{\D}$ commutes with the derivative \eqref{eq:FTC}, proving
\eqref{eq:fulltime}. For fixed $H$, the same equality holds with
$\D_H$ for $0\leq t\leq H$. The left value quotients converge in
$E_H$ to $Z_T$ even when $T=H$. Applying the bound in
\eqref{eq:bounds} proves \eqref{eq:left}; more precisely, its error is
at most
\[
 \left\|\frac{A_{T+h_n}-A_T}{h_n}-Z_T\right\|_2\longrightarrow0.
\]
By Lemmas~\ref{lem:point} and \ref{lem:representation},
$d_{\D_H}Z_T$ is not represented by any $j_{\D_H}V$. It cannot be
zero, because zero has representative $V=0$. The full-operator
conclusion is identical.

The value quotients
$u_n=(A_{T+h_n}-A_T)/h_n$ lie in $D(\D)$ and converge to
$Z_T\notin D(\D)$. Their gradients are
$(V_{T+h_n}-V_T)/h_n$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:representation} proves
\eqref{eq:escape}. The proof applies to positive steps as well.
In particular $V_t$ has no strong ordinary-time derivative at any
positive $T$: such a derivative would force these slope norms to remain
bounded. Finally \eqref{eq:actual} is the definition of $d_{\D_H}$
as an $L^2$ pairing. Its integrand is integrable by Cauchy--Schwarz.
\end{proof}

No tests or past spaces change with the step index in
\eqref{eq:left}. This is differentiation on each \emph{fixed} raw
observed past. It does not resolve convergence of fields with a changing
past, and it is not a temporal stochastic integral identification.


\section{Actual directional occupation kernels and their strong limit}
The average-domain proof requires only bounded clock gradients. There is
also a stronger statement for each fixed clock direction. We prove it
without replacing a physical spatial kernel by a pointwise white-noise
value. Fix $T\geq0$ and $h\in H_W$, and put
\[
 a_h(s)=\langle h,q_s\rangle,\qquad
 X_{n,h}=\langle G_T^{(n)},h\rangle_{H_W},\qquad
 X_h=\langle D_WA_T,h\rangle_{H_W}.
\]
Here $a_h$ is continuous and $|a_h(s)|\leq\|h\|\sqrt T$ on $[0,T]$.
Let $I:L^2(\R_x)\to L^2(P_B)$ denote the original scenery white-noise
isometry, determined by the oriented Brownian interval indicators.
Its range is closed. It is applied only to ordinary spatial $L^2$
inputs, never to a Dirac distribution. Independence gives the isometric
Bochner lift
\[
 \mathcal I:L^2(P_W;L^2(\R_x))\longrightarrow L^2(P_W\otimes P_B),
 \qquad(\mathcal I k)(W)=I(k(W)).
\]
Fubini transports this to the original factor order. The equation is
an $L^2(P_B)$ equality for almost every clock sample.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:occupationstrong}
For every fixed $h,T$ there is a unique
$K_{h,T}\in L^2(P_W;L^2(\R_x))$ such that $X_h=\mathcal I K_{h,T}$.
The finite-width kernels
\begin{equation}\label{eq:occupationkernel}
 k_{n,h,T}(W,x)=\int_0^T a_h(s)\operatorname{sgn}(W_s)
                      \rho_n(|W_s|-x)\,ds
\end{equation}
are genuine members of that Bochner space, satisfy
$X_{n,h}=\mathcal I k_{n,h,T}$, and obey
\begin{equation}\label{eq:occupationstrong}
 k_{n,h,T}\longrightarrow K_{h,T}
 \quad\hbox{in }L^2(P_W;L^2(\R_x)),\qquad
 X_{n,h}\longrightarrow X_h\quad\hbox{in }L^2(P).
\end{equation}
For each fixed $\phi\in C_c(\R)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:occupationtest}
 \int_\R K_{h,T}(W,x)\phi(x)\,dx
 =\int_0^T a_h(s)\operatorname{sgn}(W_s)\phi(|W_s|)\,ds
 \quad\hbox{for almost every }W.
\end{equation}
A specified countable test family has a common probability-one event.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each fixed width, the spatial profile in
\eqref{eq:occupationkernel} is an $L^2(\R_x)$ vector with norm
$\|\rho_n\|_2$. Its coefficient is bounded by $\|h\|\sqrt T$.
Translations of a fixed smooth compactly supported profile are
continuous in spatial $L^2$, so the time/clock field is strongly
measurable. Bochner integration and Minkowski show
\[
 \|k_{n,h,T}(W,\cdot)\|_2
 \leq T\|h\|\sqrt T\,\|\rho_n\|_2.
\]
Thus it defines the asserted clock-Bochner element. Moving the bounded
isometry through the time integral in \eqref{eq:clockgraph} proves
$X_{n,h}=\mathcal I k_{n,h,T}$.

The full gradients $G_T^{(n)}$ converge weakly to $D_WA_T$.
Indeed strong value convergence and uniform gradient bounds show that
all weak subsequential limits belong to that closed graph and equal its
unique gradient. Equivalently, adjoint-domain pairings converge directly
and extend to all field tests by their density and the uniform bound.
Consequently $X_{n,h}\rightharpoonup X_h$. The range of $\mathcal I$
is a closed linear subspace, hence weakly closed. It follows that
$X_h=\mathcal I K_{h,T}$ for exactly one $K_{h,T}$, by isometry.
Applying the adjoint of $\mathcal I$ also proves
$k_{n,h,T}\rightharpoonup K_{h,T}$ in the physical Bochner space.

Test this weak limit against $\zeta(W)\phi(x)$, with
$\zeta\in L^2(P_W)$ and $\phi\in C_c(\R)$. Fubini in
\eqref{eq:occupationkernel} gives
\[
 \langle k_{n,h,T},\zeta\otimes\phi\rangle
 =\mathbb E_W\!\left[\zeta\int_0^T a_h(s)\operatorname{sgn}(W_s)
                 (\rho_n*\phi)(|W_s|)\,ds\right].
\]
The frozen mollifiers are even, nonnegative and have mass one.
Their convolutions tend to $\phi$ pointwise and are bounded by
$\|\phi\|_\infty$. The integrand is dominated by
$|\zeta|\,\|h\|\sqrt T\|\phi\|_\infty$ on the finite time interval;
$\mathbb E|\zeta|<\infty$. Dominated convergence proves the corresponding
identity with $K_{h,T}$ and $\phi$. Both random scalar sides belong to
$L^2(P_W)$: the spatial side by Cauchy--Schwarz, the time side by its
deterministic bound. Equality against all $\zeta$ proves
\eqref{eq:occupationtest}. Countable intersection gives the stated
family version, with no uncountable intersection of null sets.

The crucial additional identification is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:occupationconvolution}
 k_{n,h,T}(W,\cdot)=\rho_n*K_{h,T}(W,\cdot)
 \quad\hbox{in spatial }L^2,\quad\hbox{for almost every }W.
\end{equation}
Here are the representative details. For each fixed spatial $x$, apply
\eqref{eq:occupationtest} with $\phi(r)=\rho_n(x-r)$.
Its spatial side is the continuous pairing of the Bochner kernel with a
continuously translated $L^2$ profile. Its time side is the integral of
a jointly measurable time/space/clock function. Both sides are jointly
measurable in $(x,W)$. Product-measure Fubini therefore converts the
fixed-$x$ almost-sure identities into an identity for almost every
$(x,W)$, then for almost every $W$ and almost every $x$.

For the finite-width time integral its actual spatial $L^2$
representative is \eqref{eq:occupationkernel}: for a continuous clock
sample set $M=\sup_{s\leq T}|W_s|<\infty$. The scalar integrand
vanishes outside $[-\epsilon_n,M+\epsilon_n]$ and is bounded by
$\|h\|\sqrt T\|\rho_n\|_\infty$. This compact spatial majorant is
square integrable, so scalar Fubini agrees with the spatial Bochner
integral. Lebesgue measure on the whole line has not been treated as
finite. These observations prove \eqref{eq:occupationconvolution} as an
identity of actual stored $L^2$ classes, not merely of formal pairings.

Positive unit-mass convolution is an $L^2$ contraction, even for signed
inputs: Jensen gives
\[
 |(\rho_n*u)(x)|^2\leq\int\rho_n(x-r)|u(r)|^2\,dr,
 \qquad \|\rho_n*u\|_2^2\leq\|u\|_2^2
\]
by Tonelli and translation invariance. Consequently
$\|k_{n,h,T}\|\leq\|K_{h,T}\|$ in the full clock/spatial norm.
Using the already proved weak convergence,
\[
 \|k_{n,h,T}-K_{h,T}\|^2
 \leq2\|K_{h,T}\|^2-2\langle k_{n,h,T},K_{h,T}\rangle
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
This proves the first strong limit; applying the isometry proves the
second. Norms converge as well. At $T=0$ all kernels and fields vanish.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{A signed occupation density and the exact quantifiers}
A further \emph{classical human deduction} identifies a measure after
fixing $h,T$. For each continuous clock path define the finite signed
measure
\[
 \nu_{h,T,W}(A)=\int_0^T a_h(s)\operatorname{sgn}(W_s)
                                      \mathbf1_A(|W_s|)\,ds.
\]
Its total variation is at most $T\|h\|\sqrt T$, and its support is in
$[0,M]$. Choose, for every positive integer $m$, a countable uniformly
dense family of continuous functions supported in $[-m,m]$ (rational
piecewise linear functions suffice). Apply the countable-family identity
\eqref{eq:occupationtest} once to their union. On that common event,
spatial Cauchy--Schwarz bounds the kernel pairing error for a uniform
test approximation by $\sqrt{2m}\|K_{h,T}(W)\|_2$ times its uniform
error. The time-side error is at most $T\|h\|\sqrt T$ times that error.
Thus the identity holds for every compactly supported continuous test
on the same event. Uniqueness of locally finite signed Radon measures
gives $\nu_{h,T,W}(dx)=K_{h,T}(W,x)\,dx$ locally. It also forces the
density to vanish outside $[0,M]$. Spatial $L^2$ on this bounded support
implies $L^1$, so this is equality of finite signed measures globally.
It asserts no common event for uncountably many $h$ or $T$, and no
positivity or jointly continuous local-time field. Classical Brownian
occupation density is already established; the primary exposition
of Pitman--Yor~\cite{PY} supplies that context. The present proof instead
identifies the particular density recovered from the original clock graph.

For completeness, two more \emph{human consequences} make the topology
precise. For any fixed finite orthonormal family $h_1,\ldots,h_N$,
\[
 \mathbb E\left\|P_N(G_T^{(n)}-D_WA_T)\right\|_{H_W}^2
 =\sum_{j=1}^N\|X_{n,h_j}-X_{h_j}\|_2^2\longrightarrow0.
\]
Moreover convergence is uniform over $h$ in any fixed compact subset
of $H_W$. The maps $h\mapsto X_{n,h}-X_h$ have uniformly bounded
operator norms by Lemma~\ref{lem:folded} and the average-domain bound.
Cover the compact set by finitely many sufficiently small balls; use
the fixed-direction limit at their centers and this common bound on
the remaining errors. This is the usual finite-net proof, not an
operator-norm convergence assertion on the whole unit ball.

Neither conclusion implies full-vector strong convergence: if $(e_n)$
is an orthonormal family in an infinite-dimensional coefficient space
and $\xi$ is any fixed scalar random variable of $L^2$ norm one, the
fields $\xi e_n$ have norm one while every fixed contraction tends to
zero in scalar $L^2$. This is a logical counterexample, not a claimed
nonconvergence theorem for the actual IBM gradients. The strong
directional spatial limit also concerns mollifier width at fixed $T$;
it coexists with native time-slope norm escape in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
It supplies neither a point clock gradient nor physical-time observed
stochastic integration.


\section{A nonlinear average with strong native gradient approximation}
There is a separate, stronger vector statement for
\[
 U_T=\int_0^T\sin(Z_t)\,dt.
\]
This is not $\sin(A_T)$. Its proof needs more than the fixed-direction
argument of the preceding section. Write $v_r=1_{(0,r]}$ for $r\geq0$
in the physical scenery realization and $q_t=1_{(0,t]}\in H_W$.
Use the original two-sided oriented extension of $v_r$ at negative
$r$. The mollified scenery $B_n$ is the one fixed in Section 3.
Define the full clock fields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sine-native-clock}
 V_n(T)=\int_0^T\operatorname{sgn}(W_t)
       \cos(B_n(|W_t|))B_n'(|W_t|)q_t\,dt
       \ \in L^2(P;H_W).
\end{equation}
Each fixed-width integral is an ordinary Bochner integral in this
Hilbert field space. Its actual representatives use the original
scenery and clock, with their factor-order isometries retained.
For the source channel put $t_{j,n}=jT/(n+1)$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sine-native-source}
 S_n(T)=\frac{T}{n+1}\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}
       \cos(Z_{t_{j,n}})v_{|W_{t_{j,n}}|}
       \ \in L^2(P;H_B).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:sine-strong}
For every fixed $T\geq0$, $U_T\in D(\D)$, and
\[
 S_n(T)\longrightarrow D_BU_T,\qquad
 V_n(T)\longrightarrow D_WU_T
\]
strongly in the original respective Hilbert field norms. In particular
$(S_n(T),V_n(T))\to\D U_T$ in $\G$. Its clock energy satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sine-energy}
 \|D_WU_T\|_2^2\leq C_T,
 \qquad C_T=8p_1T^2\sqrt T+4T^3,\quad p_1=(2\pi)^{-1/2}.
\end{equation}
For every $v\in D(\D^*)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sine-uniform-test}
 \left|\langle(S_n(T),V_n(T)),v\rangle
       -\E[U_T\D^*v]\right|
 \leq\|(S_n(T),V_n(T))-\D U_T\|_{\G}\,\|v\|_{\G}.
\end{equation}
Thus one index works for all such tests in the field unit ball,
without a common bound on their divergence norms.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The original source channel}
For each fixed clock value, the bounded Gaussian chain rule gives
$D_B\sin(B(|W_t|))=\cos(Z_t)v_{|W_t|}$ in the original source graph.
One proves this by approximating the Brownian indicator with finite
source projections and applying the closed Gaussian chain rule;
$|\cos|\leq1$ bounds the derivatives, and finite Gaussian moments
justify the limit. Product Fubini gives the same graph on the joint
space. The field
$g_t=\cos(Z_t)v_{|W_t|}$ has continuous paths in $H_B$: the scenery
and clock paths are continuous and $r\mapsto v_r$ is continuous in
$L^2(\R)$. Moreover
\[
 \|g_t\|_{H_B}^4\leq|W_t|^2,\qquad
 \E\|g_t\|_{H_B}^4\leq t.
\]
For any convergent time sequence in a compact interval, these fourth
moments are uniformly bounded. They imply uniform integrability of
the squared field differences: their fourth moments are bounded by
$8(\E\|g_{t_n}\|^4+\E\|g_t\|^4)$, and the squared-difference tail
above level $R$ is at most this bound divided by $R$. Almost-sure
continuity, truncation at $R$, and then $R\to\infty$ prove
$g_{t_n}\to g_t$ in $L^2(P;H_B)$. This supplies strong time continuity,
not an assumed pointwise evaluation map on $L^2$ classes.

Consequently the right Riemann sums \eqref{eq:sine-native-source}
converge to the Bochner integral of $g_t$. The corresponding scalar
Riemann sums converge in $L^2$ to $U_T$, since $\sin$ is Lipschitz
and $Z$ is mean-square continuous. Every finite pair is in the
original source graph, and that graph is closed. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sine-source-formula}
 D_BU_T=\int_0^T\cos(Z_t)v_{|W_t|}\,dt,
 \qquad S_n(T)\to D_BU_T.
\end{equation}
The bounded continuous-path observable $U_T$ is measurable in the
raw observed past at $T$, by its scalar Riemann sums.

\subsection{The nonlinear covariance correction and a rough spatial operator}
For the clock channel, set
\[
 Q_n(r)=\cos(B_n(r))B_n'(r),\qquad
 O_{nm}(r,z)=\int_\R\rho_n(r-x)\rho_m(z-x)\,dx.
\]
The decisive bound is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sine-covariance}
 |\E_B[Q_n(r)Q_m(z)]|\leq O_{nm}(r,z)+4.
\end{equation}
It bounds the absolute expectation, not the expectation of the
absolute product. In particular it does not assert independence
between the values and derivatives of the same Gaussian scenery.
Here is a direct derivation retaining those correlations. For any
original Gaussian directions $u,a,b$, differentiation of their joint
characteristic function gives
\[
 \E[X(a)X(b)\cos X(u)]
 =\bigl(\langle a,b\rangle-\langle u,a\rangle\langle u,b\rangle\bigr)
       e^{-\|u\|^2/2}.
\]
Finite Gaussian second moments dominate the differentiated
integrands, so this identity also includes degenerate directions.
Use $\cos X(w)\cos X(v)=\tfrac12[\cos X(w+v)+\cos X(w-v)]$.
The covariance of the derivative directions is $O_{nm}(r,z)$.
Every pairing of one mollified value direction with one derivative
direction has absolute value at most one: the Brownian indicator
pairing is an integral of a positive unit-mass mollifier over an
oriented interval; averaging it against the other positive unit-mass
mollifier preserves the bound. Thus the pairings with $w+v$ or
$w-v$ are at most two, and each correction product at most four.
The exponential factor is at most one. This proves
\eqref{eq:sine-covariance} without a divergent derivative variance
on the right.

Fix a finite interval $I=(a,b]$ of length $\ell=b-a\geq0$ and define
\[
 J_n^I\phi=\int_I\phi(r)Q_n(r)\,dr
 \quad\hbox{as a Bochner integral in }L^2(P_B).
\]
For fixed $n$ the columns $Q_n$ are strongly measurable and uniformly
bounded in source $L^2$ by $\|\rho_n\|_2$. Hence every
$\phi\in L^2(I)$ gives a well-defined integral. Since $O_{nn}$ is
symmetric, nonnegative and has whole-line row mass one,
\eqref{eq:sine-covariance} and $2|\phi(r)\phi(z)|\leq\phi(r)^2+\phi(z)^2$
give, with all integrals on the finite product interval integrable,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sine-spatial-bound}
 \|J_n^I\phi\|_{L^2(B)}^2
 \leq(1+4\ell)\|\phi\|_{L^2(I)}^2.
\end{equation}
Indeed the row integral of $O_{nn}+4$ is at most $1+4\ell$;
expansion of the squared Bochner norm and Fubini prove the estimate.

These operators converge strongly at every rough $L^2(I)$ input.
To prove it, first take a smooth test $\phi$. Ordinary pathwise
integration by parts for the smooth $B_n$ gives
\[
 J_n^I\phi=\phi(b)\sin B_n(b)-\phi(a)\sin B_n(a)
             -\int_a^b\phi'(r)\sin B_n(r)\,dr.
\]
Canonical scenery convolution converges uniformly on compact spatial
sets on the continuous-path event. The original Gaussian
mollifier fields represent those convolutions on one event for all
$n,r$: fixed rational-coordinate identities and continuity give
this common event, without an uncountable null intersection.
The displayed expression converges almost surely to the same formula
with $B$ in place of $B_n$, and is bounded by the deterministic
constant $|\phi(a)|+|\phi(b)|+\int_a^b|\phi'|$. Dominated convergence
therefore gives convergence in source $L^2$, including both endpoint
terms. Smooth restrictions are dense in $L^2(I)$. The common bound
\eqref{eq:sine-spatial-bound} and a three-term approximation show
$J_n^I\phi$ is Cauchy for every rough $\phi\in L^2(I)$.
Its limit $J^I$ is linear and bounded by $\sqrt{1+4\ell}$.
This is pointwise strong operator convergence, not operator-norm
convergence or a pointwise white-noise evaluation.

\subsection{The summable occupation trace supplies full-vector convergence}
For the actual linear occupation kernels of
Theorem~\ref{thm:occupationstrong}, an orthonormal basis $(e_i)$
of the original clock coefficient space satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:occupation-trace}
 \sum_i\|K_{e_i,T}\|_{L^2(W;L^2_x)}^2
 =\sum_i\|\langle D_WA_T,e_i\rangle\|_{L^2(P)}^2
 =\|D_WA_T\|_2^2<\infty.
\end{equation}
The first equality uses the original lifted scenery isometry; the
second is Parseval followed by Tonelli. Extra unused source directions
have zero contraction and cause no change.

Choose $0\leq\chi\leq1$ continuous with compact support inside $I$,
and insert $\chi(|W_t|)$ in \eqref{eq:sine-native-clock}, obtaining
$V_n^\chi(T)$. For every fixed clock direction $h$, the actual
occupation identity gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonlinear-occupation}
 \langle V_n^\chi(T),h\rangle
   =J_n^I(\chi K_{h,T})
 \quad\hbox{in }L^2(P_W;L^2(P_B)).
\end{equation}
For precision, the right side applies the deterministic bounded
operator to each clock-section input. To verify the identity, the
$L^2(P_B)$-valued function $r\mapsto\chi(r)Q_n(r)$ is continuous
and compactly supported. Test its spatial and clock-time integrals
against any source Hilbert vector and apply the scalar occupation
identity already proved. A countable dense set of such tests, plus
Bochner integrability and separability, yields equality of the actual
source-Hilbert vectors for almost every clock sample. Boundedness
of the compactly supported column then gives their clock $L^2$
membership and product Fubini. This proves
\eqref{eq:nonlinear-occupation} on the original product law.

The strong convergence of $J_n^I$ lifts through $L^2(P_W)$.
For a fixed input $k(W)$, pointwise source convergence holds for
almost every $W$, while the squared difference is bounded by
$4(1+4\ell)\|k(W)\|^2$. Dominated convergence proves the lifted
strong limit. In particular every contraction in
\eqref{eq:nonlinear-occupation} is Cauchy, and
\[
 \|\langle V_n^\chi(T),e_i\rangle\|_2^2
 \leq(1+4\ell)\|K_{e_i,T}\|_{L^2(W;L^2_x)}^2.
\]
The bound is summable in $i$, uniformly in $n$, by
\eqref{eq:occupation-trace}. Parseval applied to
$V_n^\chi-V_m^\chi$ now proves full-vector Cauchy convergence:
each fixed coefficient difference tends to zero, its squared norm
is bounded by four times this summable majorant, and a finite head
plus summably small tail proves the sum tends to zero. This is the
extra step that a collection of directional limits alone would lack.

\subsection{Uniform spatial tails remove the cutoff}
The cutoff error is uniform in the smoothing index. If
$\chi=1$ on $[-R,R]$, $R>0$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonlinear-cutoff-tail}
 \|V_n(T)-V_n^\chi(T)\|_2^2\leq\frac{T}{R^2}C_T.
\end{equation}
We prove the dependence between the earlier clock and its tail is
retained. Conditional on $W_s=x$, $s<t$, the folded later clock has
density bounded by $2p_1(t-s)^{-1/2}$. Since the overlap row mass is
one, any measurable $0\leq w\leq1$ gives
\[
 \E[w(W_s)O_{nm}(|W_s|,|W_t|)]
 \leq2p_1(t-s)^{-1/2}\E[w(W_s)].
\]
This integrates the proved independent future Brownian increment,
not an independent tail event. For $w(x)=1_{|x|>R}$, Chebyshev and
$\E W_s^2=s$ give $\E w(W_s)\leq s/R^2$. The nonlinear $+4$
term contributes at most $4s/R^2$ as well.

Expand the squared norm of the cutoff-error time integral.
The coefficient inner product is $\langle q_s,q_t\rangle=\min(s,t)$;
the signs have absolute value at most one. For $s<t$, the two
nonnegative complementary masks have product at most
$1_{|W_s|>R}$. Combining the preceding weighted bound with
\eqref{eq:sine-covariance}, and using $s\min(s,t)\leq T^2$, bounds
the absolute time covariance by
\[
 \frac{T^2}{R^2}\bigl(2p_1|t-s|^{-1/2}+4\bigr).
\]
The case $t<s$ follows by symmetry. The diagonal has two-dimensional
Lebesgue measure zero, and
$\int_0^T\int_0^T|t-s|^{-1/2}\,ds\,dt\leq4T\sqrt T$.
Integration proves \eqref{eq:nonlinear-cutoff-tail}. With both masks
replaced by one the same computation, using $\min(s,t)\leq T$,
gives $\|V_n(T)\|_2^2\leq C_T$.
Choose compact cutoffs with radii tending to infinity. For any
prescribed error, first make their uniform tail small; at that fixed
cutoff use the full-vector Cauchy result. The triangle inequality
therefore makes $V_n(T)$ Cauchy without cutoff. Completeness gives
a strong limit $V(T)$ and \eqref{eq:sine-energy} for that limit.

\subsection{Identification in the original graph and the adjoint identity}
At each width the original clock Gaussian chain rule gives
$D_W\sin(B_n(|W_t|))$
equal to the integrand of \eqref{eq:sine-native-clock}.
The kink at $W_t=0$ causes no extra first weak derivative:
$|\cdot|$ has almost-everywhere derivative $\operatorname{sgn}$;
smooth approximations give the closed-graph identity. More explicitly, condition on clock directions orthogonal to $q_t$ and use the scalar coordinate $W_t/\sqrt t$ for $t>0$. The mollified value has source second moment bounded by $(\sqrt{|r|}+\sqrt{\epsilon_n})^2$, and its spatial derivative has source second moment $\|\rho_n\|_2^2$, uniformly in $r$. These bounds are integrable against the scalar Gaussian clock density. Ordinary weak differentiation on bounded scalar intervals, followed by cutoff removal with these integrable bounds, proves the original Gaussian $W^{1,2}$ graph formula. Multiplying by the bounded cosine preserves the derivative bound.
The graph, a closed linear subspace of value times field, is closed
under Bochner integration of graph pairs. This follows, for example,
by applying its bounded quotient map to the integral.
Consequently
\[
 D_W\int_0^T\sin(B_n(|W_t|))dt=V_n(T).
\]
The value error is at most $T\sqrt{\epsilon_n}$ by the sine
Lipschitz bound and the original mollifier estimate. Its value tends
to $U_T$ and its field tends to $V(T)$ strongly. Closedness proves
$U_T\in D(D_W)$ and $V(T)=D_WU_T$. Combined with
\eqref{eq:sine-source-formula} and observed measurability, this gives
$U_T\in D(\D)$ and both strong limits of the theorem. No weak
convergence is promoted without the preceding summable-trace and
tail proof.

The two channels in the approximating pair need not share one
finite-stage graph input: the source channel is an unmollified
finite-time quadrature, and the clock channel is a spatially
mollified time integral. Their full-field limit is the actual joint
gradient. The separate ramp-weighted cosine Simpson sums for $U_T$
have their established value and adjoint-graph-dual limits; no
strong native joint derivatives of those particular sums are
inferred here. Finally the original adjoint identity gives
$\langle\D U_T,v\rangle=\E[U_T\D^*v]$; its product is integrable
by $L^2$ Cauchy--Schwarz. Subtracting and applying field
Cauchy--Schwarz proves \eqref{eq:sine-uniform-test}, completing
the proof. This also permits tests depending on $n$ with uniformly
bounded field norms, even if their divergence norms grow.
The horizon is fixed throughout. This nonlinear result supplies
neither a positive-time point clock gradient nor an observed
physical-time It\^o or prescribed-driver representation theorem.
