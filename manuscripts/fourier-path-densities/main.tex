\begin{abstract}
Fix a countable family of dyadic path characters and complete their coefficient space using an $\ell^1$ quotient. Higher-even-order heat multipliers define normalized continuous expectation functionals and contraction semigroups on this realized Banach space. The full infinitesimal generator has an exact weighted-coefficient quotient graph, a bounded unit resolvent and a finite-character graph core. For every positive smoothing horizon, the orbit under bounded time controls is genuinely Fr\'echet smooth. Every derivative has a unique Banach-valued Bochner time density, rather than merely a formal distributional kernel. We reconstruct the finite signed-sampler limit, the descent through representation relations, all-order density uniqueness and the scalar realization theorem. An elementary Fourier estimate gives a separately labelled human bound $(n/(epH))^{n/p}$ for the order-$n$ density when the heat order is $p$. Conjugate-exponent controls have canonical $L^p$ time densities and an explicit uniform exponent boundary. A jointly measurable time/path representative justifies weighted Fubini and strong $L^p$ convergence after path averaging. Deterministic stopping has an explicit dictionary obstruction and a contractive transport remedy. A summable cosine example fails to be differentiable without smoothing, and one fixed continuous rough direction excludes a heat expectation from its actual transpose domain. A jointly continuous backward heat character has no uniform-path Bochner time representative, separating scalar Fubini from Banach-valued integration. The entire terminal completion also has an actual signed backward-kernel pairing and exactly centered finite gains, with a partition-independent terminal telescope. The general Fourier-functional mechanism is classical; comparison with higher-order pseudoprocess and Fresnel literature is explicit. No finite-variation path measure or arbitrary adapted stochastic integral is constructed.
\end{abstract}

\section{The realized domain and the main statement}
Throughout, $p=2m$ with an integer $m\ge1$. The smoothing parameter $H$ is nonnegative and is not the physical coordinate of a path. Set
\[
 \X=\{\omega\in C(\R,\R):\omega(0)=0\},
\]
with the compact-open topology. Choose any countable list of dyadic levels $r_i\ge0$ and vectors $\xi_i\in\R^{2^{r_i}}$. Adjoin the zero vector as index zero. With $N_i=2^{r_i}$ and $t_{ij}=(j+1)/N_i$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:char}
 \chi_i(\omega)=\exp\left(-2\pi i\sum_{j=0}^{N_i-1}\xi_{ij}\omega(t_{ij})\right).
\end{equation}
Repeated frequencies and characters are permitted. All characters have modulus one and are continuous. Their coefficient realization is
\[
 J:\ell^1(\mathbb N,\C)\longrightarrow C_b(\X,\C),\qquad
 Ja=\sum_i a_i\chi_i.
\]
The series converges uniformly, and $\norm{Ja}_\infty\le\norm a_1$. Put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quotient}
 K=\ker J,\quad \A=\ell^1/K,\quad Q:\ell^1\to\A,\qquad
 \norm{Qa}_{\A}=\inf_{b:\,Jb=Ja}\norm b_1.
\end{equation}
Thus $\A$ is complete, and its induced realization $\mathcal J:\A\to C_b(\X)$ is injective and contractive. The quotient norm is part of the theorem: it is not replaced by the uniform norm. An arbitrary selected list need not be closed under frequency addition or negation, so no algebra or conjugation closure is assumed.

Let $U=L^\infty(\R,ds;\R)$, and define the control direction
\[
 h_u(t)=\int_0^t u(s)\,ds\quad(t\in\R).
\]
The integral has the usual orientation for negative $t$. It depends only on the almost-everywhere class of $u$, is globally Lipschitz, and vanishes at zero. Translation is denoted by $\tau_h$, with realization $\mathcal J(\tau_h q)(\omega)=\mathcal Jq(\omega+h)$.

Define the step functions, extended by zero outside $[0,1]$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eta}
 \eta_i(s)=2\pi\sum_j\xi_{ij}\mathbf1_{(0,t_{ij}]}(s),
 \qquad \lambda_i=\int_0^1|\eta_i(s)|^p\,ds.
\end{equation}
Endpoints of the intervals do not affect these classes. The heat evolution will be
\begin{equation}\label{eq:heat}
 S_H Qa=Q\big((a_i e^{-H\lambda_i})_i\big).
\end{equation}
Its well-definedness requires proof; arbitrary coefficient multipliers do not automatically preserve $K$.

For a real Banach space $B$, write $B_0=B$ and $B_{n+1}=L^1(\R;B_n)$, with Bochner integrability. Complex spaces are regarded as real when taking control derivatives. For $u_1,\ldots,u_n\in U$, let $\langle u_1\otimes\cdots\otimes u_n,F\rangle$ denote successive Bochner pairings, starting with the outer variable. Its norm is bounded by $\norm F\prod_j\norm{u_j}_\infty$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
The formula \eqref{eq:heat} defines a strongly continuous contraction semigroup on $\A$. For $H>0$ and $q\in\A$, the map
\[
 F_q(u)=\tau_{h_u}S_Hq\quad(U\to\A)
\]
is $C^\infty$ over $\R$. For every integer $n\ge1$, there is a unique $D_{n,H}q\in\A_n$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density}
 D^nF_q(0)[u_1,\ldots,u_n]
 =\langle u_1\otimes\cdots\otimes u_n,D_{n,H}q\rangle.
\end{equation}
These densities are supported in $[0,1]^n$, in the iterated $L^1$ sense. The frozen formal construction gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:formalbound}
 \norm{D_{n,H}q}\le(1+n/H)^n\norm q_{\A}.
\end{equation}
The direct human Fourier estimate proved below gives the stronger bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:humanbound}
 \norm{D_{n,H}q}\le C_{n,p,H}\norm q_{\A},\qquad
 C_{n,p,H}=\left(\frac{n}{epH}\right)^{n/p}.
\end{equation}
For every bounded real-linear $L:\A\to\C$, application of $L$ at every Bochner level gives the unique scalar density of $D^n(LF_q)(0)$, of norm at most $\norm L C_{n,p,H}\norm q_{\A}$.
\end{theorem}

The weighted-sampler interpretation is proved in Section~\ref{sec:weighted}. This statement concerns every element of the specified completion. It does not identify that completion with all bounded continuous path functions or with a space of predictable integrands. The sharper constant in \eqref{eq:humanbound} is not asserted to be a named Lean theorem or the optimal operator norm for every selected spectrum.

\section{Actual finite signed samplers and the expectation functional}
For $t>0$, let $k_{p,t}$ be the real Schwartz inverse Fourier transform of $e^{-t(2\pi\xi)^p}$, with Fourier convention $\widehat f(\xi)=\int e^{-2\pi i\xi x}f(x)\,dx$. Then
\[
 \int k_{p,t}=1,\qquad
 k_{p,t}(x)=t^{-1/p}k_{p,1}(t^{-1/p}x).
\]
Reality follows from the real even multiplier. Schwartz regularity follows because every derivative of the multiplier is a polynomial times a rapidly decreasing function. In particular its inverse transform is integrable. Write $M_p=\int|k_{p,1}|$, a finite number at least one, independent of $t$ after scaling.

At dyadic level $r$, take $N=2^r$ signed independent increments with density $\prod_{j<N}k_{p,H/N}(x_j)$, and take the unique Lagrange polynomial path through zero at time zero and the cumulative sums at the dyadic times. This is a continuous path on the whole real line, depending linearly on the increments. This produces a finite signed Borel measure $\nu_{r,H}$ on $\X$, of mass one and total variation $M_p^N$; the interpolation map is injective, since its increments can be recovered from dyadic evaluations.

The positive sampler $P_{r,H}$ is the pushforward of the normalized absolute increment density. If $\rho(x)=\prod_j k_{p,H/N}(x_j)$, use
\[
 w_{r,H}(\omega)=M_p^N\operatorname{sgn}_*(\rho(x(\omega))),
\]
where $x(\omega)$ recovers the increments, and $\operatorname{sgn}_*$ agrees with the sign off zero and has modulus one at zero. The convention at zeros does not affect the sampler. Recovery outside the interpolated-path support may be defined by the same evaluations. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weights}
 d\nu_{r,H}=w_{r,H}\,dP_{r,H},\quad
 |w_{r,H}|=M_p^N,\quad
 \int w_{r,H}\,dP_{r,H}=1,
\end{equation}
and $\int|w_{r,H}|=M_p^N$, $\int w_{r,H}^2=M_p^{2N}$. This is an actual finite positive sampling law with a signed weight. The positive sampler is generally not a Gaussian proposal law.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:finitechar}
For every $i$ and $r$, $|\int\chi_i\,d\nu_{r,H}|\le1$. For $r\ge r_i$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exactchar}
 \int\chi_i\,d\nu_{r,H}=e^{-H\lambda_i}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
At any level, every interpolated evaluation is a real linear combination of the independent increments. Substitution into \eqref{eq:char} therefore gives a product of their Fourier transforms, each of the form $e^{-(H/N)(2\pi b)^p}\in(0,1]$. This proves the bound even when some character times are finer than the sampler.

If $r\ge r_i$, all the character times are grid points. In the coarse $i$-grid, write $\omega(t_{ij})=\sum_{k\le j}\Delta_k$. Collecting coefficients gives $\sum_j\xi_{ij}\omega(t_{ij})=\sum_k(\sum_{j\ge k}\xi_{ij})\Delta_k$. Independence and convolution of the finer increments yield
\[
 \prod_{k<N_i}\exp\left[-\frac{H}{N_i}
 \left(2\pi\sum_{j\ge k}\xi_{ij}\right)^p\right].
\]
This is \eqref{eq:exactchar}, since $\eta_i$ equals the corresponding tail sum on each interval of length $1/N_i$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:expect}
For every $a\in\ell^1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:expectlimit}
 \int Ja\,d\nu_{r,H}\longrightarrow\sum_i a_i e^{-H\lambda_i}.
\end{equation}
The limit depends only on $Ja$ and defines a functional $\E_H:\A\to\C$ of norm exactly one.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For each fixed $r$, uniform convergence of the character series allows integration term by term against the finite variation measure $\nu_{r,H}$. Although its variation depends on $r$, Lemma~\ref{lem:finitechar} bounds the resulting $i$th scalar term by $|a_i|$, uniformly in $r$. Dominated convergence for this counting-measure series proves \eqref{eq:expectlimit}. This uses a character-specific bound, not a nonexistent uniform variation estimate.

If $Ja=0$, every finite integral on the left vanishes. The limiting scalar series is consequently zero. It annihilates $K$, so descends to $\A$. Its coefficient bound is $\norm a_1$; taking the infimum over all representatives gives $|\E_H q|\le\norm q_{\A}$. The constant character represents $\mathbf1\in\A$ with norm one: its coefficient norm is one and its realized supremum norm is one. Its expectation is one, proving the reverse norm inequality. Finite character combinations are dense in the quotient, since finitely supported sequences are dense in $\ell^1$.
\end{proof}

\section{Translations, heat evolution and the quotient relations}\label{sec:semigroup}
On coefficients define $T_h a=(a_i\chi_i(h))_i$. It is an invertible isometry with inverse $T_{-h}$, and
\[
 J(T_h a)(\omega)=Ja(\omega+h).
\]
Thus $T_hK=K$, and $\tau_h Qa=Q(T_h a)$ is an isometry of $\A$. Dominated convergence in $\ell^1$ proves continuity in $h$ for the compact-open topology: each coordinate $\chi_i(h)$ is continuous, and the coefficient difference is bounded by $2|a_i|$. The same argument, using the finitely many times of each character, proves continuity of $u\mapsto\tau_{h_u}q$ in the $L^\infty$ norm.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:heat}
The multiplier in \eqref{eq:heat} preserves $K$. The induced $S_H$ is a contraction, $S_0=I$, $S_{H+G}=S_HS_G$, and $S_H\tau_h=\tau_hS_H$. Moreover
\begin{equation}\label{eq:translatedexpect}
 \mathcal J(S_Hq)(\omega)=\E_H(\tau_\omega q),\qquad H>0.
\end{equation}
The map $H\mapsto S_Hq$ is norm continuous on $[0,\infty)$ for every $q\in\A$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For coefficients the right side of \eqref{eq:translatedexpect} is $\sum_i a_i\chi_i(\omega)e^{-H\lambda_i}$, the realization of the proposed evolved coefficients. If $a\in K$, then $T_\omega a\in K$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:expect} makes this scalar zero for every $\omega$. Hence the evolved coefficients belong to $K$. At $H=0$ preservation is immediate.

The coefficient multiplier has norm at most one. Its quotient operator is therefore contractive. The remaining algebraic identities follow from multiplication of the diagonal factors, including $e^{-(H+G)\lambda_i}=e^{-H\lambda_i}e^{-G\lambda_i}$, and descend because $K$ is preserved. Finally, if $H_j\to H\ge0$, the coefficient norm difference tends to zero by domination by $2|a_i|$ and coordinate continuity. Its quotient norm is no larger. This proves strong continuity, without requiring uniform continuity in operator norm.
\end{proof}

There is no positivity assertion in this proposition. The existence of a normalized contraction functional in a coefficient quotient is weaker than existence of a probability path law. Likewise the boundedness of $\mathcal J$ does not give a bounded inverse on its range equipped with the uniform norm.

\section{Bounded controls separate genuine Bochner time fields}\label{sec:bochner}
For a real Banach space $B$, the map
\[
 P_u:L^1(\R;B)\to B,\qquad P_uf=\int u(s)f(s)\,ds
\]
has norm at most $\norm u_\infty$. Multiplication by $u$ preserves strong measurability and has integrable norm, so this is an ordinary Bochner integral. Bounded linear maps commute with it. Iterating gives the pairing and norm bound used in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:separate}
If $F\in B_n$ and all bounded-control pairings of $F$ vanish, then $F=0$ in $B_n$. In particular a density satisfying \eqref{eq:density}, if it exists, is unique as an iterated $L^1$ class.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First let $n=1$. Taking $u=\mathbf1_E$ for every measurable set $E$ of finite measure shows $\int_E F=0$. A Bochner integrable function is essentially valued in a separable closed subspace $B'\subset B$. There is a countable family $(\ell_j)$ of norm-one bounded real-linear functionals on $B$ that norms $B'$: choose a countable dense subset of its unit sphere, choose a supporting functional for each point by Hahn--Banach, and use density to obtain $\norm x=\sup_j|\ell_j(x)|$ for $x\in B'$. The scalar functions $\ell_j(F)$ have integral zero on every finite-measure set, so each vanishes almost everywhere. For example, apply the integral identity on bounded subsets of $\{\ell_j(F)>1/k\}$ and of $\{\ell_j(F)<-1/k\}$, then let their bounds and $k$ increase. The countable norming family now yields $F=0$ almost everywhere.

For the induction step, fix the first control $u_1$. Pairing the outer variable gives an element of $B_{n-1}$ whose every remaining control pairing is zero. It vanishes by induction. Thus all first-control pairings vanish as elements of $B_{n-1}$. The case $n=1$, now with Banach target $B_{n-1}$, gives $F=0$. Apply this to a difference to obtain uniqueness.
\end{proof}

This proof avoids an uncountable intersection of exceptional sets indexed by all functionals. No reflexivity or Radon--Nikodym property of $B$ is needed. When desired, the iterated spaces identify isometrically with Bochner $L^1$ on $\R^n$ by the Bochner Fubini theorem. The iterated formulation already defines every contraction and is the one used in the source construction.

\section{Native Fr\'echet derivatives and direct density bounds}\label{sec:derivatives}
Fubini for the finite sum in \eqref{eq:eta} gives the exact control phase
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phase}
 \chi_i(h_u)=e^{-i b_i(u)},\qquad
 b_i(u)=\int\eta_i(s)u(s)\,ds.
\end{equation}
The sign and the factor $2\pi$ come from \eqref{eq:char}; neither can be discarded. Since the support has length one, H\"older's inequality gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:coercive}
 \norm{\eta_i}_1\le\norm{\eta_i}_p=\lambda_i^{1/p},\qquad
 |b_i(u)|\le\lambda_i^{1/p}\norm u_\infty.
\end{equation}
For $n\ge1$ and $H>0$, differentiation of a real scalar exponential suggests the coefficient tensor
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tensorcoeff}
 \Big(a_i e^{-H\lambda_i}e^{-i b_i(u)}
       (-i)^n\prod_{j=1}^n b_i(v_j)\Big)_i.
\end{equation}
We prove both summability and genuine Fr\'echet differentiability before passing to the quotient.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:multiplier}
For $n\ge1$,
\[
 \sup_{x\ge0}x^{n/p}e^{-Hx}
 =\left(\frac{n}{epH}\right)^{n/p}=C_{n,p,H}.
\]
The tensor \eqref{eq:tensorcoeff} has $\ell^1$ norm at most $C_{n,p,H}\norm a_1\prod_j\norm{v_j}_\infty$. For $n=0$, use $C_{0,p,H}=1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $x>0$, the logarithmic derivative of $x^{n/p}e^{-Hx}$ is $n/(px)-H$. The function increases up to $x=n/(pH)$, then decreases to zero, and vanishes at $x=0$. Substitution yields the maximum. Apply \eqref{eq:coercive} to every factor in \eqref{eq:tensorcoeff}, sum $|a_i|$, and use this scalar bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:smooth}
The coefficient orbit $u\mapsto(a_i e^{-H\lambda_i}e^{-ib_i(u)})_i$ is $C^\infty$ from $U$ to $\ell^1$. Its $n$th derivative is \eqref{eq:tensorcoeff}. The quotient orbit $F_q$ is also $C^\infty$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For real $x,y$, the integral Taylor formula gives
\[
 |e^{-i(x+y)}-e^{-ix}+iy e^{-ix}|\le |y|^2/2,
 \qquad |e^{-i(x+y)}-e^{-ix}|\le |y|.
\]
At derivative order $n$, multiply the first inequality by $|a_i|e^{-H\lambda_i}\prod_{j\le n}|b_i(v_j)|$, substitute $y=b_i(w)$, and sum. The remainder in the space of bounded $n$-linear maps is at most
\[
 \tfrac12 C_{n+2,p,H}\norm a_1\norm w_\infty^2
        \prod_{j\le n}\norm{v_j}_\infty.
\]
The candidate derivative is the $(n+1)$st tensor. The second inequality similarly bounds the difference of the $n$th tensors at $u+w$ and $u$ by $C_{n+1,p,H}\norm a_1\norm w_\infty\prod_j\norm{v_j}_\infty$. This proves differentiability and continuity in operator norm, inductively at every order, including order zero. Composition with the bounded quotient map proves the last assertion. In particular the tensors annihilate $K$ after applying $Q$: if $a\in K$, the entire quotient orbit is identically zero by Proposition~\ref{prop:heat} and translation invariance of $K$.
\end{proof}

For a representative $a$, define a field in the iterated $\ell^1$-valued $L^1$ space by the absolutely convergent sum
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fieldcoeff}
 \widetilde D_{n,H}a(s_1,\ldots,s_n)
 =\sum_i a_i e^{-H\lambda_i}(-i)^n
       \eta_i(s_1)\cdots\eta_i(s_n)e_i.
\end{equation}
Each summand is strongly measurable, supported in $[0,1]^n$, and has integral norm $|a_i|e^{-H\lambda_i}\norm{\eta_i}_1^n$. Lemma~\ref{lem:multiplier} bounds the sum of these norms by $C_{n,p,H}\norm a_1$. Thus the sum converges in the complete Bochner space; no pointwise convergence on a common exceptional set is required. Its contraction against controls is exactly the coefficient tensor at $u=0$, by continuity of the pairings.

\begin{proof}[Proof of the density assertions in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Apply $Q$ at each Bochner level to \eqref{eq:fieldcoeff}; call the resulting field $\mathcal D a$. If $a\in K$, every control pairing of $\mathcal D a$ is zero by Proposition~\ref{prop:smooth}. Lemma~\ref{lem:separate} implies $\mathcal D a=0$. Hence $\mathcal D$ descends to a bounded linear map $D_{n,H}:\A\to\A_n$. Taking the infimum of the coefficient bound over representatives proves \eqref{eq:humanbound}. Its control pairing is the derivative of the quotient orbit, establishing \eqref{eq:density}; uniqueness follows from the same separation lemma. Support is preserved by summation, quotient maps and norm limits.

For a bounded real-linear $L:\A\to\C$, apply its Bochner lift at every depth. Each lift has norm at most $\norm L$, rather than a new factor at each depth: induction lifts the same previously constructed map, so its norm never increases. Pairing commutes with every lift, yielding $L$ of the tensor in \eqref{eq:density}. The bounded linear chain rule identifies this with $D^n(LF_q)(0)$. The norm bound and scalar uniqueness follow.
\end{proof}

At a general control $u$, replace $q$ by $\tau_{h_u}q$ in the zero-control density; its norm is unchanged. Thus these are native Banach-space derivatives at every control. Their multilinear symmetry follows directly from the product in \eqref{eq:tensorcoeff}, and the density is invariant under permutation of the time variables in its Bochner product-space realization. These are consequences of the explicit formula and uniqueness, not assumptions about formal functional differentiation.

\section{The source construction and the total heat horizon}
The formal source takes a compositional route. Its first time-gradient map at horizon $T>0$ is a bounded map
\[
 G_T:\A\to L^1(\R;\A),\qquad \norm{G_T}\le1+T^{-1}.
\]
It is constructed from the step-function character atoms and descended through the same kernel using bounded-control separation. Its contraction is the genuine first derivative of $u\mapsto\tau_{h_u}S_Tq$ at zero. On a generator the representative is $-i\eta_i(s)e^{-T\lambda_i}Qe_i$, agreeing with \eqref{eq:fieldcoeff} at $n=1$.

Iterate $G_T$ using its Bochner lifts. The order-$n$ map has norm at most $(1+T^{-1})^n$ and contracts to the ordered composition of $n$ smoothed directional derivatives. Each step contributes a new heat factor. On finite generators their product is $e^{-nT\lambda_i}(-i)^n\prod_jb_i(u_j)$. Both the maps and contractions are bounded, so finite-generator density extends this identity to every $q\in\A$. Proposition~\ref{prop:smooth}, or the source's heat-splitting induction, identifies it with the native $n$th derivative at total horizon $nT$.

To obtain the derivative at a prescribed $H$, one must therefore take $T=H/n$. This yields \eqref{eq:formalbound}. Taking all $n$ factors at horizon $H$ would instead describe total smoothing $nH$; it would not prove the stated fixed-horizon identity. The normalized formal density and the direct field of Section~\ref{sec:derivatives} have identical contractions, so Lemma~\ref{lem:separate} identifies them. Consequently the human estimate \eqref{eq:humanbound} bounds that same canonical density; no new domain or alternative completed space is being substituted.

For $n=2$, the source also allows two unequal positive horizons $S,T$. The nested Hessian field has bound $(1+S^{-1})(1+T^{-1})\norm q$ and contracts to the Hessian at total horizon $S+T$. Pairing it against $u,v$ is a genuine iterated Bochner integral. In the present notation its generator formula is
\[
 -e^{-(S+T)\lambda_i}\eta_i(s)\eta_i(t)Qe_i.
\]
This checks the two minus-imaginary phase factors and the sum, rather than product, of the heat horizons.

\section{Realized derivatives and weighted expectation limits}\label{sec:weighted}
Path evaluation $L_\omega q=\mathcal Jq(\omega)$ has norm at most one. Its scalar lifted field realizes the $n$th derivative of
\[
 u\longmapsto\mathcal J(S_Hq)(\omega+h_u).
\]
This follows from the translation identity and the bounded linear chain rule. It is a derivative of an actual bounded continuous path function in the specified Banach domain.

An expectation functional may have a different even heat order $2k$ and a different positive horizon $G$. Denote it by $\E^{(2k)}_G$ and use the samplers $P^{(2k)}_{r,G},w^{(2k)}_{r,G}$. The domain $\A$ depends on the chosen characters, not on the heat order, so this comparison uses the same space. The smoothing densities above still use order $p$ and horizon $H$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:weighted}
Fix $H,G>0$, $m,k\ge1$, $q\in\A$, $n\ge1$ and bounded controls $u_1,\ldots,u_n$. At every dyadic sampling level, the weighted realized native derivative is integrable. Moreover
\begin{align*}
 &\lim_{r\to\infty}\int w^{(2k)}_{r,G}(\omega)
 D^n\big[v\mapsto\mathcal J(S_Hq)(\omega+h_v)\big](0)
 [u_1,\ldots,u_n],dP^{(2k)}_{r,G}(\omega)\\
 &\hspace{12mm}=D^n\big[v\mapsto\E^{(2k)}_G(\tau_{h_v}S_Hq)\big](0)
 [u_1,\ldots,u_n]\\
 &\hspace{12mm}=\langle u_1\otimes\cdots\otimes u_n,
        (\E^{(2k)}_G)_n D_{n,H}q\rangle.
\end{align*}
Here $(\E^{(2k)}_G)_n$ is the iterated Bochner lift, not an integral over an unspecified infinite-dimensional signed law.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $a_*=D^nF_q(0)[u_1,\ldots,u_n]\in\A$. The realized tensor is exactly $\mathcal J a_*(\omega)$ by the bounded evaluation chain rule. Its absolute value is at most $\norm{a_*}_{\A}$. Equation~\eqref{eq:weights} now bounds the absolute weighted integrand by $M_{2k}^{2^r}\norm{a_*}_{\A}$, a finite constant at every fixed $r$. This proves integrability.

Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:expect}, with order $2k$ and horizon $G$, to the single completed test $a_*$. Its finite weighted expectations converge to $\E^{(2k)}_G a_*$. The bounded linear chain rule gives the first identity on the right; commutation with the lifted density pairing gives the second. No derivative is interchanged with a limit using a uniform bound on the weights. Instead the derivative is first established in the completed test space, and the existing continuous expectation-limit theorem is then applied to that test.
\end{proof}

The theorem is pointwise in the chosen controls and derivative order. Its scalar limiting multilinear map has the bound from Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, since $\norm{\E^{(2k)}_G}=1$. It makes no claim that the weighted densities themselves converge in $L^1$ under one common probability measure as $r$ varies. The finite sampler laws change with $r$.

\section{Conjugate-exponent controls and a sharp uniform boundary}\label{sec:dual}
There is a natural larger local class of controls. Put $q_p=p/(p-1)$ and
$V=L^{q_p}(\R;\R)$. This notation distinguishes the control exponent
$q_p$ from the test element $q\in\A$. Every $u\in V$ is locally
integrable and defines the same primitive $h_u(t)=\int_0^tu$. H\"older
gives the explicit estimate
\[
 |h_u(t)-h_u(s)|\le\norm u_{q_p}|t-s|^{1/p},\qquad s,t\in\R.
\]
Thus $h_u$ is a continuous path. Local absolute continuity also gives
$h'_u=u$ almost everywhere, so this direction map is injective on the
whole real-line control space. These paths need not be Lipschitz. For
example $u(t)=t^{-a}\mathbf1_{(0,1]}(t)$ belongs to $V$ whenever
$0<a<1/q_p$, but is not essentially bounded; its primitive on $[0,1]$
is $t^{1-a}/(1-a)$ and is not Lipschitz at zero.

For a Banach space $B$, now define $B^{[0]}=B$ and
$B^{[n+1]}=L^p(\R;B^{[n]})$. These are different spaces from the
iterated $L^1$ towers of the main theorem. H\"older defines bounded
pairings by controls in $V$ at every level, with the same product
norm bound. They separate the towers: all finite-measure indicator
controls belong to $V$, and the local Bochner separation proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:separate} applies. At its first step an $L^p$ field is
integrable on every finite-measure set by H\"older; global $L^1$
integrability is not required for that argument.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:dual}
For every $H>0$ and $q\in\A$, the orbit $u\mapsto\tau_{h_u}S_Hq$ is
$C^\infty$ on $V$. Its order-$n$ derivative has a unique time density
$D^{[p]}_{n,H}q\in\A^{[n]}$, with
\[
 \norm{D^{[p]}_{n,H}q}\le C_{n,p,H}\norm q_{\A}.
\]
The explicit coefficient formula is \eqref{eq:fieldcoeff}, now summed
in the iterated $L^p$ space. The native first derivative and its $L^p$
density are formalized in the frozen sources. This all-order $L^p$
tower statement and the sharper constant are human deductions.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The phase \eqref{eq:phase} remains valid for locally integrable controls.
Its key bound is now the exact H\"older inequality
\[
 |b_i(u)|\le\norm{\eta_i}_p\norm u_{q_p}
          =\lambda_i^{1/p}\norm u_{q_p}.
\]
This substitutes directly for \eqref{eq:coercive} in the Taylor proof
of Proposition~\ref{prop:smooth}. The remainder bounds at orders
$n$ and $n+1$ are identical with the $V$ norm in place of the
$L^\infty$ norm. They prove actual all-order Fr\'echet differentiability
in this Banach topology, not merely directional differentiability.

The iterated $L^p$ norm of the $i$th simple tensor in
\eqref{eq:fieldcoeff} is exactly
$|a_i|e^{-H\lambda_i}\norm{\eta_i}_p^n$.
The sum of these norms is at most $C_{n,p,H}\norm a_1$.
Completeness therefore gives a genuine Bochner field. Contracting it
gives the native tensor; for a coefficient relation in $K$ the tensor
is zero in $\A$ because the quotient orbit is identically zero.
Separation of these $L^p$ towers annihilates the relation at the field
level. Hence the field descends, and taking the infimum over
representatives proves the bound. The same separation proves
uniqueness. Scalar realization and application of every bounded
linear functional follow by Bochner lifting as before.
\end{proof}

The source's first-gradient norm estimate is $1+H^{-1}$. Its actual
closed directional graph, native Fr\'echet identity and uniquely
representing $L^p$ field occur in \code{FourierPathDualFrechet} and
\code{FourierPathDualTimeGradient}. Proposition~\ref{prop:dual}
identifies the latter field with the direct formula by uniqueness.
It does not attribute an all-order $L^p$ tower theorem to those
first-order declarations.

The bounded-control and conjugate-exponent densities are compatible.
They vanish off $[0,1]^n$, and there the continuous embedding of
iterated $L^p$ into iterated $L^1$ has norm at most one, by H\"older on
a set of measure one at every level. Truncating any $L^\infty$ control
to $[0,1]$ puts it in $V$ without changing any character phase.
The embedded field and $D_{n,H}q$ have the same bounded-control
pairings; uniqueness in the $L^1$ tower makes them equal. This uses
compact time support, not an invalid global inclusion between
$L^\infty(\R)$ and $L^{q_p}(\R)$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:exponentboundary}
The exponent $q_p$ is a sharp lower boundary for a uniform
first-gradient bound over arbitrary allowed spectra. More precisely,
there is a single admissible countable spectrum such that, for every
$1\le r<q_p$ and every fixed $H>0$, no finite constant $B$ can bound
\[
 \norm{D[\,u\mapsto\tau_{h_u}S_Hq\,](0)[u]}
       \le B\norm q_{\A}\norm u_r
\]
even just for its single-character tests and bounded step controls.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $N=2^j$, include the character with its only nonzero observation
coefficient at $1/N$, chosen so that its tail field is
$\eta_N=N^{1/p}\mathbf1_{(0,1/N]}$. This is an allowed dyadic
character: take the coefficient $N^{1/p}/(2\pi)$ at that first node
and zero at all the other nodes. It has $\lambda_N=1$. Its generator
$q_N=Qe_N$ has quotient norm exactly one, by the same realized-supremum and one-coefficient argument used below for
terminal characters. Take
$u_N=N^{1/r}\mathbf1_{(0,1/N]}$, which has $L^r$ norm one and is a
bounded step control. The generator derivative equals
\[
 -i e^{-H}b_N(u_N)q_N,
 \qquad b_N(u_N)=N^{1/p+1/r-1}.
\]
Its norm tends to infinity because $r<q_p$ makes the exponent
positive. This contradicts any proposed $B$.
\end{proof}

Here the single spectrum is fixed and contains all the dyadic spikes.
The result does not say that every individual spectrum or test fails
at smaller control exponents, or that every such orbit lacks a
derivative. It rules out the stated bounded uniform theorem and is a
human sharpness deduction from the character formula. All controls
used in the obstruction are bounded, so their individual derivatives
already exist by Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{Joint time/path representatives and justified Fubini}\label{sec:joint}
An almost-everywhere time field evaluated separately at each path does
not by itself establish joint measurability. The source supplies this
missing bridge for the first $L^p$ density. Let
$g=D^{[p]}_{1,H}q\in L^p(\R;\A)$ and choose a strongly measurable
representative $\widetilde g:\R\to\A$, equal to $g$ almost
everywhere. It can be chosen zero off $[0,1]$. Define
\[
 j_q(s,\omega)=\mathcal J(\widetilde g(s))(\omega).
\]
The quotient $\A$ is separable, since it is a quotient of countable
$\ell^1$. The path space $\X$ is Polish for the compact-open
topology: $C(\R,\R)$ is complete and separable for a usual uniform-on-
compacts metric, and $\omega(0)=0$ is a closed condition.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:joint}
The function $j_q$ is jointly measurable for the product Borel
structure, continuous in $\omega$ at every fixed $s$, and satisfies
\[
 |j_q(s,\omega)|\le\norm{\widetilde g(s)}_{\A}.
\]
For every $\omega\in\X$ and $u\in V$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jointcontract}
 \int u(s)j_q(s,\omega)\,ds
 =\mathcal J\big(DF_q(0)[u]\big)(\omega).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The evaluation map $(a,\omega)\mapsto\mathcal Ja(\omega)$ is jointly
continuous. Indeed, at $(a_0,\omega_0)$ its difference from
$\mathcal Ja_0(\omega_0)$ is bounded by
$\norm{a-a_0}_{\A}+|\mathcal Ja_0(\omega)-\mathcal Ja_0(\omega_0)|$.
Composing this continuous map with the measurable map
$(s,\omega)\mapsto(\widetilde g(s),\omega)$ proves joint
measurability. The stated spaces have their Borel structures; a
strongly measurable representative is Borel measurable, and their
second-countable structures ensure the product Borel identification.
The pointwise bound and path continuity follow from contractivity of
$\mathcal J$ and its values in $C_b(\X)$.

For fixed $\omega$, bounded evaluation commutes with the Bochner
integral of $u\widetilde g$, which is integrable by H\"older.
The defining density contraction is $DF_q(0)[u]$, proving
\eqref{eq:jointcontract}. The exceptional time set is that of a single
$\A$-valued representative. In particular the argument does not take
an uncountable intersection of path-specific null sets.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:fubini}
Let $\mu$ be an s-finite Borel measure on $\X$ and let real $w\in
L^1(\mu)$. For every $u\in V$, the function
$w(\omega)u(s)j_q(s,\omega)$ is absolutely integrable under
$ds\,d\mu(\omega)$, and
\begin{align*}
 &\int\!\int w(\omega)u(s)j_q(s,\omega)\,d\mu(\omega)\,ds\\
 &=\int w(\omega)\mathcal J(DF_q(0)[u])(\omega)\,d\mu(\omega).
\end{align*}
Its product $L^1$ norm is at most
$\norm w_{L^1(\mu)}\norm u_{q_p}\norm g_{L^p(\R;\A)}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:joint} provides joint measurability and the product
majorant
$|w(\omega)||u(s)|\norm{\widetilde g(s)}_{\A}$.
Its integral is at most the asserted bound by Tonelli and H\"older.
Fubini therefore applies to the actual integrable function.
Exchange the two integrals and apply \eqref{eq:jointcontract} for each
path to obtain the identity. This establishes the necessary product
integrability before any exchange.
\end{proof}

Take $\mu=P^{(2k)}_{r,G}$ and $w=w^{(2k)}_{r,G}$. The bound is finite
at every level, although its weight factor $M_{2k}^{2^r}$ can diverge.
The proposition identifies the double time/path integral with the
weighted expectation of the single completed derivative test.
Proposition~\ref{prop:expect} then gives the limit
\[
 \lim_{r\to\infty}\int\!\int
 w^{(2k)}_{r,G}(\omega)u(s)j_q(s,\omega)
 \,dP^{(2k)}_{r,G}(\omega)\,ds
 =\int u(s)\, (\E^{(2k)}_G g)(s)\,ds.
\]
The field on the right is the Bochner $L^p$ lift of the expectation
functional applied to $g$. Its pairing equals
$\E^{(2k)}_G(DF_q(0)[u])$. The source establishes exactly this first-
density sampler limit, not convergence of the unpaired joint fields
under a common probability measure. Arbitrary random controls
$u(s,\omega)$ are not admitted merely because deterministic controls
can be paired. Their products and limits require additional domains
and estimates.

\subsection{Strong convergence of the averaged time densities}
The paired limit has a stronger form once the path variable has
been integrated out. This is different from convergence of the raw
joint time/path fields under changing sampler laws. Fix the order
$2k$ and horizon $G$ of the sampling functional, and write
\[
 A_rq=\int w^{(2k)}_{r,G}(\omega)\mathcal Jq(\omega)
                 \,dP^{(2k)}_{r,G}(\omega),\qquad A=\E^{(2k)}_G.
\]
These are bounded functionals on the same $\A$, with
$\norm{A_r}\le1$, $\norm A=1$ and $A_rq\to Aq$ for every $q\in\A$.
The uniform finite-level contraction follows already from
Lemma~\ref{lem:finitechar}: integrate a coefficient representative,
bound its character expectations by one and take the quotient
infimum. A coefficient relation has realized value zero, so each
finite functional is well defined on the quotient before taking
its limit. Large variation of the signed sampler does not enter
this stronger coefficient-norm bound.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:strongaveraged}
For $g=D^{[p]}_{1,H}q$, let $f_r$ and $f$ be the $L^p(\R;\C)$
classes obtained by the Bochner lifts of $A_r$ and $A$. Then
\[
 \norm{f_r}_p\le\norm g_p\le C_{1,p,H}\norm q_{\A},\qquad
 \norm{f_r-f}_p\longrightarrow0.
\]
One version of $f_r$ is
$s\mapsto\int w^{(2k)}_{r,G}(\omega)j_q(s,\omega)
\,dP^{(2k)}_{r,G}(\omega)$, and the corresponding version of $f$
is $s\mapsto A\widetilde g(s)$. For every fixed $s$ these versions
converge. Moreover
\[
 \sup_{\norm u_{q_p}\le1}
 \left|\int u(s)(f_r(s)-f(s))\,ds\right|\longrightarrow0.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The bounds for $A_r$ give the first inequality by the norm bound for
Bochner lifting; the second is Proposition~\ref{prop:dual}.
For every fixed $s$, $A_r\widetilde g(s)\to A\widetilde g(s)$ by
pointwise convergence of the functionals on $\A$. Also
\[
 |A_r\widetilde g(s)-A\widetilde g(s)|^p
       \le2^p\norm{\widetilde g(s)}_{\A}^p.
\]
The right side is integrable. Dominated convergence therefore gives
the $p$th power of the asserted norm convergence. The finite integral
formula follows by applying the actual sampler functional to
$\widetilde g(s)$; it is integrable in the path variable for every
$s$, because the test is bounded and the finite weight is integrable.
Its equality with the lifted class is the usual almost-everywhere
representative identity for a bounded map on $L^p$. Finally H\"older
bounds the displayed supremum by $\norm{f_r-f}_p$.
\end{proof}

The native first-field convergence and the operator-norm convergence
of its control contractions are both formalized in
\code{FourierPathDensityStrongLimit}, using the genuine uniform
finite expectation operators in \code{FourierPathSamplerOperators}.
The common $L^p$ space here is time only, after path averaging. The
argument supplies neither convergence of $w_{r,G}$ under one fixed
probability law nor operator-norm convergence of $A_r$ to $A$ on
the entire unit ball of $\A$. No quantitative rate follows from
dominated convergence alone.

\section{Two concrete domain and normalization checks}
\subsection{A two-time character and an invisible constant control}
Take the times $1/2,1$ with frequencies $1,-1/2$. Then $\eta$ is $\pi$ on $(0,1/2]$ and $-\pi$ on $(1/2,1]$, and $\lambda=\pi^p$. For the constant control $u=\mathbf1_{(0,1]}$, $b(u)=0$. This character is unchanged by the linear direction $h_u(t)=t$ on $[0,1]$, although its heat multiplier is strictly less than one for $H>0$.

Let $v=\mathbf1_{(0,1/2]}$ and $z=\mathbf1_{(1/2,1]}$. Then $b(v)=\pi/2$ and $b(z)=-\pi/2$. At zero path the first $v$-derivative is $-i(\pi/2)e^{-H\pi^p}$ and the mixed $(v,z)$ derivative is $+(\pi^2/4)e^{-H\pi^p}$. Pairing the density \eqref{eq:fieldcoeff} gives these same signs and constants. This example shows why the frequency energy uses all tail sums, rather than just the derivative in a constant-control direction or the terminal frequency alone.

\subsection{An unsmoothed orbit that is not differentiable}
Choose a spectrum containing the terminal frequencies $j,-j$ for every $j\ge1$, and put
\[
 f(\omega)=\sum_{j=1}^\infty\frac{\cos(2\pi j\omega(1))}{j^2}.
\]
This is an element $q\in\A$: use coefficients $1/(2j^2)$ for the two characters. The coefficient sum is finite and the realization is exactly $f$. Along $u=\mathbf1_{(0,1]}$, write $g(x)=f(h_{xu})$. It is an even continuous real function. With $h_N=1/(4N)$, all terms in $g(0)-g(h_N)$ are nonnegative, and for $N\le j\le2N$ the angle $2\pi jh_N$ lies in $[\pi/2,\pi]$. Therefore
\[
 g(0)-g(h_N)\ge\sum_{j=N}^{2N}j^{-2}
 \ge\frac{N+1}{4N^2}>h_N.
\]
If $g$ were differentiable at zero, evenness would force $g'(0)=0$, contradicting these difference quotients. Evaluation at zero path is a bounded functional on $\A$, so the unsmoothed $\A$-valued control orbit is not Fr\'echet differentiable there either. This is a concrete element of the completed domain, not merely growth of an operator norm on a sequence of generators.

For a single terminal character of integer frequency $j$, its quotient norm is exactly one, since its realized supremum norm and its one-coefficient bound are both one. Its unsmoothed control derivative in direction $u$ has norm $2\pi|j|$. Thus no uniform bounded first derivative on all these generators can hold at $H=0$. Positive heat suppresses these frequencies by $e^{-H(2\pi j)^p}$, exactly the mechanism of Lemma~\ref{lem:multiplier}.

\section{A continuous rough direction outside expectation-transfer domains}\label{sec:rough}
The preceding sharpness result varies bounded controls inside one
spectrum. A distinct source example fixes one continuous direction
and still defeats heat-damped expectation transfer. The natural
directional operator must first be specified.

For any fixed $h\in\X$, the maps $T_t=\tau_{th}$, $t\in\R$, form a
strongly continuous isometric group on $\A$. Define
\[
 \operatorname{Dom}D_h=
 \{q\in\A:\lim_{t\to0}(T_tq-q)/t\text{ exists in }\A\},
 \qquad D_hq=\lim_{t\to0}(T_tq-q)/t.
\]
Finite character combinations lie in this domain and are dense.
The source identifies this native domain with its actual quotient
coefficient graph, rather than postulating derivative values on a
larger completion.

For completeness, $D_h$ is closed. For $q\in\operatorname{Dom}D_h$,
the group law gives $\frac d{dt}T_tq=T_tD_hq$ and hence
\[
 T_tq-q=\int_0^t T_sD_hq\,ds.
\]
The derivative follows by applying $T_t$ to the defining difference
quotient; its continuity follows from strong continuity on $D_hq$.
The displayed identity is then the Banach-space fundamental theorem
of calculus. If $q_j\to q$ and $D_hq_j\to v$, isometry permits passage
to the limit under this finite-interval Bochner integral: the error
is at most $|t|\norm{D_hq_j-v}$. Thus $T_tq-q=\int_0^t T_sv\,ds$.
Dividing by $t$ and using strong continuity at zero gives $q\in
\operatorname{Dom}D_h$ and $D_hq=v$. Density and closedness therefore
refer to the original native differentiation graph.


\subsection*{The exact coefficient domain, including the reverse inclusion}
Here is the full quotient identification just used. For the fixed
continuous direction $h$, put
\[
 \beta_i=-2\pi\sum_j\xi_{ij}h(t_{ij}),\qquad d_i=i\beta_i,
 \qquad \chi_i(th)=e^{it\beta_i}.
\]
Thus $d_i$ is the directional eigenvalue; it is unrelated to the
nonnegative heat rate $\lambda_i$.
The precise statement is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:directional-full-graph}
 \operatorname{Graph}(D_h)=
 \{(Qa,Qb):a,b\in\ell^1,\ b_i=d_i a_i\ \text{for every }i\}.
\end{equation}
In particular domain membership requires the existence of a
representative $a$ with $Qa=q$ and $(\beta_i a_i)\in\ell^1$.
It does not require this condition for every representation of $q$,
nor does it identify arbitrary coefficients uniquely in the quotient.

One inclusion follows directly by dominated convergence in $\ell^1$:
if $b_i=d_i a_i$, then
\[
 \left|a_i\frac{e^{it\beta_i}-1}{t}\right|\leq|b_i|,
 \qquad
 a_i\frac{e^{it\beta_i}-1}{t}\longrightarrow b_i.
\]
For $t\ne0$, the first estimate is the elementary chord bound
$|e^{ix}-1|\leq|x|$. The difference from $b_i$ is dominated by
$2|b_i|$, summable independently of $t$. Applying the bounded
quotient map gives the native two-sided derivative with value $Qb$.

For the converse, the coefficient multiplier
\[
 (\mathcal R_h a)_i=\frac{a_i}{1-d_i}
\]
is bounded on $\ell^1$, with norm at most one since
$|1-i\beta_i|\geq1$. It also satisfies
$d_i(\mathcal R_h a)_i=(\mathcal R_h a)_i-a_i$.
Its descent through $K$ is essential. A human Laplace-integral
presentation gives that step without choosing an inverse of the
realization. Define
$\Delta_s a=(e^{is\beta_i}a_i)_i$. This is strongly continuous
in $\ell^1$ by dominated convergence and has constant norm
$\|a\|_1$. Therefore
\[
 \mathcal R_h a=\int_0^\infty e^{-s}\Delta_s a\,ds,
 \qquad
 Q\mathcal R_h a=\int_0^\infty e^{-s}T_sQa\,ds.
\]
The first integral is Bochner integrable with norm majorant
$e^{-s}\|a\|_1$. Evaluation of each coefficient reduces it to
$\int_0^\infty e^{-(1-i\beta_i)s}ds=(1-i\beta_i)^{-1}$.
The second equality follows because $Q$ is bounded and
$Q\Delta_s=T_sQ$. If $a\in K$, its right-hand side vanishes;
hence $\mathcal R_h a\in K$. We consequently obtain a
well-defined bounded operator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:directional-resolvent}
 R_hQa=Q\mathcal R_h a,\qquad \|R_h\|\leq1,
 \qquad (R_hq,R_hq-q)\in\operatorname{Graph}(D_h).
\end{equation}
The norm bound follows by taking the infimum over all coefficient
representatives, or directly by isometry of $T_s$ in the integral.

Now suppose $q$ has native derivative $v$. Put
$x=R_h(q-v)$ and $y=q-x$. The already proved inclusion and
\eqref{eq:directional-resolvent} give
$D_hx=x-(q-v)$, so $D_hy=y$.
The group law gives $\frac d{ds}T_sy=T_sD_hy=T_sy$.
Thus $e^{-s}T_sy$ has zero derivative and is constant for
$s\geq0$. It follows that $T_sy=e^s y$; isometry at any
$s>0$ forces $y=0$. Hence $q=R_h(q-v)$.
Choose a coefficient representative $a$ of $q-v$ and put
$c=\mathcal R_h a$. Then $Qc=q$ and
$Q((d_i c_i)_i)=Q(c-a)=q-(q-v)=v$.
Both coefficient sequences are in $\ell^1$, which proves the
reverse inclusion in \eqref{eq:directional-full-graph}.

This also gives the resolvent characterization of the complete
graph:
\[
 (q,v)\in\operatorname{Graph}(D_h)
       \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad q=R_h(q-v).
\]
It is a closed set by continuity of $R_h$, consistently with the
native closedness proof above. Finally, truncate both sequences in
any pair on the right of \eqref{eq:directional-full-graph} to the
same finite index sets. They converge in their respective
$\ell^1$ norms, so their quotient values converge in both graph
components. The finite-character span is therefore a genuine
graph core, not merely a dense value domain. This last graph-core
formulation is a human tail consequence; the source maps the
exact native graph and resolvent identities.

The Laplace-resolvent mechanism is classical, as in the
Engel--Nagel comparison below for the heat generator. The formal
directional proof obtains kernel descent and the reverse inclusion
through its dissipative graph bound; the displayed integral and
isometric-group argument reorganize that mechanism. No heat
smoothing, bounded-control restriction on $h$, or physical-time
path derivative is inferred from this purely directional graph.

Regard $\A$ as real and let $\A'_{\R}$ be its continuous real dual.
Its Banach transpose has the domain
\[
 \operatorname{Dom}D_h'=\{\phi\in\A'_{\R}:\exists\psi\in\A'_{\R},\

The value $\psi=D_h'\phi$ is unique by density. Domain membership is

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:rough}
There are a fixed countable dyadic spectrum and a fixed continuous
direction $h$ such that, for quartic heat and every $H>0$,
\begin{enumerate}
 \item $D_hS_H$ has no bounded extension agreeing with its values on
 the finite-character core;
 \item no continuous real-linear $L:\A\to\C$ satisfies
 $\E_H(D_hq)=Lq$ throughout $\operatorname{Dom}D_h$;
 \item the continuous real functional $\phi_H=\operatorname{Im}\E_H$
 is not in $\operatorname{Dom}D_h'$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take $h(t)=(\max(t,0))^{1/8}$. At level $8n$, put
$\delta_n=2^{-8n}$ and include the character
\[
 \chi_n(\omega)=\exp(-i2^{2n}\omega(\delta_n)),\qquad n\ge0.
\]
Its frequency vector has just the first nonzero entry
$2^{2n}/(2\pi)$. The associated tail function is
$\eta_n=2^{2n}\mathbf1_{(0,\delta_n]}$. For $p=4$,
\[
 \lambda_n=2^{8n}\delta_n=1,\qquad
 2^{2n}h(\delta_n)=2^n.
\]
Its generator $q_n$ has quotient norm one, and direct scalar
differentiation gives
\[
 D_hq_n=-i2^nq_n,\qquad S_Hq_n=e^{-H}q_n,\qquad
 \E_Hq_n=e^{-H}.
\]
Consequently $\norm{D_hS_Hq_n}=2^ne^{-H}\to\infty$, proving the first
claim. Also $\E_H(D_hq_n)=-i2^ne^{-H}$, whose modulus is unbounded
on these unit input tests. This rules out $L$ in the second claim.
Its imaginary part is $-2^ne^{-H}$, so it cannot be the value of one
bounded real functional $\psi$ on the $q_n$. The transpose-domain
criterion proves the third claim.
\end{proof}

Every generator and its heat evolution in this proof lies in the
native directional domain. Unboundedness on that core does not
assert that every smoothed test lies outside the domain. The formal
rough-transfer and rough-adjoint modules establish the actual second
and third exclusions, with precisely the displayed dyadic spectrum.
The first bounded-extension exclusion is an immediate human norm
deduction from the same exact source formulas.

This direction is continuous, but its derivative on $(0,1]$ is
$\frac18t^{-7/8}$, whose $4/3$ power has divergent integral at zero.
It therefore falls outside the local conjugate-exponent control
condition. The example is compatible with all the positive theorems
above. In particular strong continuity of translation in a continuous
direction does not imply that every normalized heat expectation
belongs to its transpose domain. A bounded functional and a densely
defined closed derivative still require their actual transfer
estimate.

\section{Deterministic stopping and a change of Fourier dictionary}\label{sec:stopping}
Another domain boundary is independent of differentiability. For
$t\ge0$ define $(\operatorname{stop}_t\omega)(s)=\omega(\min(s,t))$.
This is a continuous path vanishing at zero. Composition by the
fixed continuous clock $s\mapsto\min(s,t)$ is continuous for the
compact-open topology: the image of each compact set is compact.
Consequently $f\mapsto f\circ\operatorname{stop}_t$ is a contraction
on $C_b(\X)$. It need not preserve a selected realized quotient.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:stopping}
There is an admissible countable Fourier dictionary and a test
$q\in\A$ for which $\mathcal Jq\circ\operatorname{stop}_{1/2}$ is
not the realization of any element of the same $\A$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take every nonconstant listed character to be the same terminal
character $\chi(\omega)=e^{-2\pi i\omega(1)}$. Along with the
adjoined constant, every test in its quotient has a realization
depending only on $\omega(1)$; this follows term by term in its
absolutely convergent coefficient series. Let $q$ be its character
generator. Compare the zero path with
$b(s)=-2s(1-s)$, a continuous path on $\R$ with $b(0)=b(1)=0$.
They have the same endpoint, so every realized test in this
dictionary has equal values on them. But $b(1/2)=-1/2$, and hence
\[
 (\chi\circ\operatorname{stop}_{1/2})(0)=1,\qquad
 (\chi\circ\operatorname{stop}_{1/2})(b)=-1.
\]
No realized quotient test can equal that stopped function. This also
rules out any same-space stopping map with the proposed realization
property, even without linearity or continuity of that map.
\end{proof}

There is a precise positive repair using a different dictionary.
Suppose characters $(\widetilde\chi_i)$, indexed in the same way as
$(\chi_i)$ and including the constant, satisfy
\[
 \widetilde\chi_i(\omega)=\chi_i(\Phi(\omega))
       \quad\text{for every }i,\omega,
\]
for a specified path map $\Phi$. Write $\widetilde\A=\ell^1/
\widetilde K$ for the target realization kernel. Then
\[
 T:\A\to\widetilde\A,\qquad T(Qa)=\widetilde Q a,
 \qquad\norm T\le1,
 \qquad\widetilde{\mathcal J}(Tq)=\mathcal Jq\circ\Phi.
\]
Indeed, summing the character identity gives
$\widetilde Ja=(Ja)\circ\Phi$. Thus $K\subset\widetilde K$, which
proves well-definedness. The coefficient bound followed by the
source quotient infimum proves contractivity, and the same series
identity gives realization. No algebraic closure of the source
dictionary is needed.

For dyadic stopping this target can be explicit. If $t$ is a common
node in every selected observation grid, choose the node $k_i$ with
$t_{i,k_i}=t$. Stopping replaces $\omega(t_{ij})$ by
$\omega(t_{i,\min(j,k_i)})$. The new frequency at node $\ell$ is
\[
 \widetilde\xi_{i\ell}=
       \sum_{j:\,\min(j,k_i)=\ell}\xi_{ij}.
\]
Collecting the finite phase sum proves the character identity, and
the displayed contraction maps into that stopped-frequency
dictionary. The common-node condition is part of this exact source
specialization; it is not assumed for every dictionary and time.
The general transport statement also covers explicitly refined
target grids when their character identities have been proved.

The obstruction and contractive dictionary transport are formalized
in \code{FourierPathStopping} and
\code{FourierPathDictionaryTransport}. These are deterministic
pullbacks. No optional-stopping theorem, probabilistic past
conditional expectation, or arbitrary adapted integral is obtained
from dictionary transport alone.


\section{The full heat generator, unit resolvent and graph core}
The heat evolution has an exact full infinitesimal generator on the
same realized quotient $\A$. Fix the heat order $p=2m$, $m\geq1$,
and the dictionary of Section 1. Define the linear relation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:heat-generator-graph}
 \mathfrak G=\{(Qa,Qb):a,b\in\ell^1,
                      \ b_i=-\lambda_i a_i\text{ for every }i\}.
\end{equation}
Thus a graph input has at least one representative with
$\sum_i\lambda_i|a_i|<\infty$. This is an existence condition on a
representative in the original fixed dictionary, not a prescribed
choice of coordinates or an assumption that every representative
has finite weighted norm. Repeated characters and nontrivial
representation relations remain allowed.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:full-heat-generator}
The relation $\mathfrak G$ is the graph of a closed densely defined
operator $L:D(L)\subset\A\to\A$, and for every $f,g\in\A$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:heat-full-generator}
 \lim_{h\downarrow0}\frac{S_hf-f}{h}=g\text{ in }\A
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad (f,g)\in\mathfrak G.
\end{equation}
Its unit resolvent is well defined on every $f=Qa\in\A$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:heat-unit-resolvent}
 \mathcal R f=Q\left(\frac{a_i}{1+\lambda_i}\right)_i,
 \qquad \|\mathcal R f\|\leq\|f\|.
\end{equation}
It satisfies $(I-L)\mathcal R f=f$ and
$\mathcal R(I-L)x=x$ for $x\in D(L)$. The finite span of the
pairs $(Qe_i,-\lambda_iQe_i)$ is a graph core: its closure in
$\A\times\A$ is exactly $\mathfrak G$.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Weighted representatives and the contraction tangent}
For a coefficient pair in \eqref{eq:heat-generator-graph},
\[
 \frac{a_i(e^{-h\lambda_i}-1)}h\longrightarrow-\lambda_i a_i=b_i,
 \qquad
 \left|\frac{a_i(e^{-h\lambda_i}-1)}h\right|\leq|b_i|\quad(h>0).
\]
The bound follows from $0\leq1-e^{-r}\leq r$ for $r\geq0$.
Dominated convergence for the summable sequence $2|b_i|$ proves
convergence of the difference quotients in $\ell^1$. Applying $Q$
proves the forward graph-to-limit implication. A graph pair with
zero first entry consequently has zero second entry, since its
quotient difference quotients are identically zero. The relation is
therefore single valued and defines $L$. Each finite character
combination belongs to its domain, so the previously proved finite
character density makes $D(L)$ dense.

We will use a tangent estimate valid for any contraction orbit.
Regard the complex Banach space as a real one. Suppose
$F(h)=x+hy+o(h)$ for $h\downarrow0$ and $\|F(h)\|\leq\|x\|$.
A real Hahn--Banach supporting functional $\varphi$ satisfies
$\|\varphi\|\leq1$ and $\varphi(x)=\|x\|$. Hence
$\varphi(F(h))\leq\|x\|$, and division by $h>0$ gives
$\varphi(y)\leq0$. For every $q>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:heat-dissipative}
 q\|x\|\leq\varphi(qx-y)\leq\|qx-y\|.
\end{equation}
If $x=0$, the zero supporting functional gives the same inequality.
In particular every graph pair satisfies this bound, because $S_h$
is contractive and its graph tangent was just proved. The same
estimate applies to any other vector whose right tangent exists,
before it is known to lie in the coefficient graph.

\subsection{The resolvent descends through the actual relations}
The coefficient map $a\mapsto(a_i/(1+\lambda_i))$ is contractive
on $\ell^1$. To prove that it preserves $K$, one may use the
following classical Laplace presentation, here a labelled human
reorganization of the named coefficient-resolvent construction.
The coefficient semigroup $C_ta=(e^{-t\lambda_i}a_i)$ is strongly
continuous in $\ell^1$ and $\|C_ta\|_1\leq\|a\|_1$. Thus
$t\mapsto e^{-t}C_ta$ is Bochner integrable on $[0,\infty)$.
Each continuous coefficient evaluation commutes with its integral,
and ordinary scalar integration gives
\[
 \int_0^\infty e^{-t}C_ta\,dt
   =\left(\frac{a_i}{1+\lambda_i}\right)_i.
\]
If $a\in K$, every $C_ta$ is in the same closed kernel by the
already established multiplier descent. Applying $Q$ to the
Bochner integral shows its quotient is zero. This proves kernel
preservation and makes \eqref{eq:heat-unit-resolvent} independent
of the representative. Its coefficient bound, followed by the
infimum over all representatives of $f$, gives $\|\mathcal Rf\|\leq\|f\|$.
It also gives the actual strong integral formula
\begin{equation}\label{eq:heat-laplace-human}
 \mathcal Rf=\int_0^\infty e^{-t}S_tf\,dt.
\end{equation}
This is integration of a quotient-valued heat orbit, with integrable
norm at most $e^{-t}\|f\|$; it is not an integral over a signed
path measure.

For $a\in\ell^1$, put $c_i=a_i/(1+\lambda_i)$. Then
$c_i-a_i=-\lambda_i c_i$ and both $c$ and $c-a$ are summable,
since $\lambda_i/(1+\lambda_i)\leq1$. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:heat-resolvent-graph}
 (\mathcal Rf,\mathcal Rf-f)\in\mathfrak G
 \quad\hbox{for every }f\in\A.
\end{equation}
This already proves surjectivity of $I-L$ onto the entire quotient.
If $(x,y)\in\mathfrak G$, subtract the graph pair
$(\mathcal R(x-y),\mathcal R(x-y)-(x-y))$.
The difference pair has equal first and second entries.
Estimate \eqref{eq:heat-dissipative} with $q=1$ forces that entry
to be zero, yielding $x=\mathcal R(x-y)$. Conversely that equality
and \eqref{eq:heat-resolvent-graph} give $(x,y)\in\mathfrak G$.
Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:heat-graph-resolvent}
 (x,y)\in\mathfrak G\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 x=\mathcal R(x-y).
\end{equation}
The right side is a closed condition on $\A\times\A$ because
$\mathcal R$ is bounded. This proves closedness of the actual
image graph, and also the two inverse identities of the theorem.
No assumption that an image of a closed subspace is closed is used.

\subsection{Maximality of the generator and the finite graph core}
Suppose the right limit on the left of
\eqref{eq:heat-full-generator} exists and equals $g$. Set
$x=\mathcal R(f-g)$ and $y=x-(f-g)$. By
\eqref{eq:heat-resolvent-graph}, $(x,y)\in\mathfrak G$, so its
right tangent is $y$. Linearity of $S_h$ shows the orbit of $f-x$
has right tangent $g-y$. Contractivity and the tangent estimate,
applied to this difference at $q=1$, give
\[
 \|f-x\|\leq\|(f-x)-(g-y)\|=0,
\]
because $y=x-f+g$. Hence $f=x=\mathcal R(f-g)$ and
\eqref{eq:heat-graph-resolvent} puts $(f,g)$ in $\mathfrak G$.
This proves the converse implication on every vector with a genuine
norm derivative; the coefficient graph is the full generator,
rather than only a convenient subdomain.

Finally, for any represented graph pair $(Qa,Qb)$, truncate both
sequences at the same index $N$. Their weighted relation is retained,
and
\[
 \sum_{i<N}a_i(Qe_i,-\lambda_iQe_i)
     =(Qa^{(N)},Qb^{(N)})\longrightarrow(Qa,Qb)
       \quad\hbox{in }\A\times\A.
\]
Both convergences follow from ordinary $\ell^1$ tails. Thus every
graph pair is in the closure of the finite eigenpairs. Those
finite pairs are themselves in the graph, whose closedness proves
the opposite inclusion. This completes the theorem, including
its graph-core assertion.

The semigroup and resolvent mechanism is classical. Engel--Nagel
\cite[Theorem II.1.10, equation (1.13)]{EN} give the broader
Laplace representation for generators of exponentially bounded
strongly continuous semigroups. Our direct quotient proof records
the exact weighted representative domain and original relations;
\eqref{eq:heat-laplace-human} is the explicit human presentation,
while the named formal proof obtains kernel descent using the same
dissipative graph estimate. The parameter differentiated here is
heat evolution $H$, distinct from the time-control derivatives
elsewhere in this paper. No physical path generator, positive
higher-heat process or arbitrary adapted integration is inferred.


\section{Joint continuity does not imply uniform-path Bochner measurability}
The time densities above were built in their specified Banach spaces,
with strong measurability proved before integration. A moving-time
heat observable gives a concrete reason to retain this requirement.
Let $E=C_b(\X;\C)$ have its uniform norm and, for $0<t<1$, put
\[
 c_t(\omega)=e^{-2\pi i\omega(t)}.
\]
The scalar map $(t,\omega)\mapsto c_t(\omega)$ is jointly continuous:
evaluation is continuous for the compact-open topology on $\X$ and
locally compact time domain. Each $c_t$ is in $E$, with norm one.
Nevertheless $t\mapsto c_t$ is not almost-everywhere strongly
measurable as an $E$-valued map for Lebesgue measure on $(0,1)$.

\begin{proof}
For distinct positive $s,t$, use the explicit zero-start polynomial
path
\[
 \omega_{s,t}(r)=\frac{r(r-s)}{2t(t-s)}.
\]
It has $\omega_{s,t}(s)=0$, $\omega_{s,t}(t)=1/2$, so
$c_s(\omega_{s,t})=1$ and $c_t(\omega_{s,t})=-1$. The triangle
inequality supplies the reverse estimate; hence exactly
\[
 \|c_s-c_t\|_E=2\quad(s\ne t,\ s,t>0).
\]
A separable metric space contains at most countably many points
with pairwise distance two: balls of radius $1/2$ about those points
are disjoint, and each contains a distinct member of a fixed countable
dense subset. Every subset of a separable metric space is itself
separable, so the same countability holds when these characters lie
inside any separable subset of $E$.

If an $E$-valued map is almost everywhere a pointwise norm limit of
simple functions, then outside one null set its values lie in the
closure of the union of their finite ranges. That closure is separable.
Thus a strongly measurable modification of $c_t$ would put the
characters for almost every $t\in(0,1)$ inside a separable set.
The exact separation then makes that full-measure set of times
countable, which is impossible for Lebesgue measure. This proves the
assertion, including failure after any modification on a null set.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{An actual backward heat kernel with the same obstruction}
For the fixed heat order $p=2m$, define
\[
 a_m(t)=e^{-(1-t)(2\pi)^p},\qquad
 b_m(t,\omega)=a_m(t)c_t(\omega)\quad(0<t<1).
\]
The actual signed kernel identity, with the Fourier convention of
\eqref{eq:char}, is
\[
 b_m(t,\omega)=\int_\R e^{-2\pi i(\omega(t)+y)}k_{p,1-t}(y)\,dy.
\]
Indeed factor the phase into its current-coordinate and increment
factors, then apply the proved Fourier transform of the Schwartz
heat kernel. Absolute integrability follows from $|e^{-2\pi iy}|=1$
and the kernel's finite total variation. This is a genuine scalar
future-increment integral, not a prescribed formal multiplier.

The function $a_m$ is continuous and never zero. If $t\mapsto b_m(t)$
were almost everywhere strongly measurable in $E$, multiplication
by the continuous scalar $a_m(t)^{-1}$ would make $c_t$ strongly
measurable, contradicting the proof above. Scalar multiplication
preserves strong measurability by taking simple approximations to
both factors and intersecting the two full-measure convergence sets;
no boundedness assumption on the inverse is needed. Consequently,
for every $1\le r\le\infty$, no element of $L^r((0,1);E)$ agrees
almost everywhere with the $E$-valued backward heat map. In particular,
its uniform-norm bound by one alone does not give a Bochner integral.

On the other hand, $b_m$ is jointly continuous and $|b_m|\le1$.
For every finite positive Borel measure $\mu$ on $\X$, it therefore
belongs to the scalar space $L^r((0,1)\times\X,dt\,d\mu)$ for all
$1\le r\le\infty$. Scalar product-measure Fubini is valid. There is
no contradiction: scalar measurability on the product differs from
strong measurability with values in the uniform path-norm space.
This statement includes ordinary Gaussian heat $p=2$; the obstruction
is not caused by negativity of a higher-order kernel.

The necessary separable-range principle is classical; see the
Pettis criterion in Arora--Gl\"uck--Mui~\cite{AGM}, Theorem 4.A.5,
printed page 70. Our proof uses only its necessary direction and
proves that direction directly from simple functions. Joint continuity
of point evaluations is not asserted to give weak measurability
against every element of $E^*$. Nor do we substitute $E$ for the
original quotient $\A$ or infer that these arbitrary moving characters
belong to a fixed countable Fourier dictionary. The constructed
control-derivative time densities remain in their original spaces.

\section{Completed terminal observables and actual signed backward gains}
There is also an exact conditional pairing on the entire terminal
Fourier completion. It is a finite signed-kernel theorem, rather
than conditional probability under an infinite path law. This
distinction permits a useful backward representation without
contradicting the preceding variation and measurability obstructions.

Specialize the dictionary to terminal characters
\[
 e_{\xi_j}(\omega)=e^{-2\pi i\xi_j\omega(1)},\qquad j\geq1,
\]
and the constant character. Denote the corresponding quotient by
$\A_{\rm ter}$ and put
\[
 f_q(x)=\mathcal Jq(\ell_x),\qquad \ell_x(r)=xr.
\]
Each terminal character depends only on $\omega(1)$, so the entire
completed realization also does: this follows from uniform
convergence of every $\ell^1$ coefficient realization. In particular
$f_q\in C_b(\R;\C)$ and $\norm{f_q}_\infty\leq\norm q$.
Its coefficient representatives may be nonunique. The heat action
multiplies the $\xi_j$ coefficient by
$e^{-h(2\pi\xi_j)^p}$, consistently with the original Fourier
normalization in \eqref{eq:char}.

\begin{proposition}[Actual kernel on the completion]\label{prop:terminalkernel}
For $h>0$, $x\in\R$ and $q\in\A_{\rm ter}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:terminalkernel}
 f_{S_hq}(x)=\int_\R k_{p,h}(y)f_q(x+y)\,dy,
 \qquad |f_{S_hq}(x)|\leq\norm q.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On a single character, its multiplicative translation identity
and the exact kernel Fourier transform give
\[
 \int k_{p,h}(y)e^{-2\pi i\xi(x+y)}\,dy
       =e^{-h(2\pi\xi)^p}e^{-2\pi i\xi x}.
\]
The constant has kernel integral one. For fixed $h$, the integral
functional is continuous on the quotient: its absolute value is
at most $\norm{k_{p,h}}_1\norm q$. The heat/evaluation
functional is continuous with norm at most one. Equality on
the dense finite-character span therefore proves equality on
the completion. This proof is independent of a chosen
coefficient representative. The last bound follows from the
quotient heat contraction and evaluation bound, not positivity
of the signed kernel or a contraction in the physical supremum norm.
\end{proof}

Let $(\Omega,\mu)$ be sigma-finite, let $r:\Omega\to\R$
be measurable and integrable, and let $X:\Omega\to\R$ be
measurable. Think of $r\,d\mu$ as an actual finite signed past
law and $X$ as its past state. The fresh extension is the
finite signed density
\[
 d\nu_h(w,y)=r(w)k_{p,h}(y)\,d\mu(w)\,dy.
\]
For a measurable complex past test $G$ which is bounded, all
the integrals below are absolutely integrable against this
displayed density. More explicitly their product absolute
integral is bounded by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditionalmajorant}
 \norm q\,\norm{k_{p,h}}_1\int_\Omega|r(w)||G(w)|\,d\mu(w).
\end{equation}
Thus actual Fubini, followed by
Proposition~\ref{prop:terminalkernel}, proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:completedconditional}
 \int_{\Omega\times\R}G(w)f_q(X(w)+y)\,d\nu_h(w,y)
 =\int_\Omega r(w)G(w)f_{S_hq}(X(w))\,d\mu(w).
\end{equation}
The left side is the integral under the original signed
extension, not under its normalized absolute-density probability
law. The density makes its meaning completely explicit.

There is a simple HUMAN extension to an unbounded measurable
$G$ satisfying $\int|r||G|\,d\mu<\infty$. Define $G_n=G$
where $|G|\leq n$ and $G_n=nG/|G|$ elsewhere. These are bounded
measurable tests, $G_n\to G$ and $|G_n|\leq|G|$.
The majorant \eqref{eq:conditionalmajorant} is integrable for
this $G$. Dominated convergence on the actual product density
and on the past density passes \eqref{eq:completedconditional}
to $G$. This extension is not a stronger named formal
conditional-expectation theorem; it is a direct absolute-Fubini
consequence with the proof and exact weighted-$L^1$ hypothesis.

\subsection*{Backward fields and zero signed past pairing}
Fix $H>0$ and $q\in\A_{\rm ter}$, and for $0\leq t\leq H$ set
\[
 u_q(t,x)=f_{S_{H-t}q}(x).
\]
Strong continuity of the quotient semigroup makes
$t\mapsto\mathcal J(S_{H-t}q)$ continuous in uniform norm.
Joint evaluation at $(t,x)$ is therefore continuous:
\[
 |u_q(t,x)-u_q(s,z)|
 \leq\norm{\mathcal J(S_{H-t}q)-\mathcal J(S_{H-s}q)}_\infty
             +|f_{S_{H-s}q}(x)-f_{S_{H-s}q}(z)|.
\]
The first term tends to zero as $t\to s$, and the second as
$x\to z$. Also $|u_q(t,x)|\leq\norm q$,
$u_q(H,x)=f_q(x)$ and
$u_q(0,0)=\E_H(q)$, where $\E_H$ is the actual expectation
functional of the terminal dictionary.

For $0\leq s<t\leq H$ define the actual gain
\[
 g_{s,t}(x,y)=u_q(t,y)-u_q(s,x).
\]
It is bounded by $2\norm q$. Apply
\eqref{eq:completedconditional} at fresh duration $t-s$ to
the completed test $S_{H-t}q$. The semigroup identity gives
$S_{t-s}S_{H-t}=S_{H-s}$. The other term is constant in the
fresh increment, whose signed kernel mass is one. Subtracting
the two actual integrals proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:completedgainzero}
 \int_{\Omega\times\R}G(w)
       g_{s,t}(X(w),X(w)+y)\,d\nu_{t-s}(w,y)=0.
\end{equation}
Its absolute integral is at most
$2\norm q\norm{k_{p,t-s}}_1\int|r||G|$.
For bounded past tests this is the named completed signed-gain
theorem. For the weighted-$L^1$ tests it is the same printed
human clipping/Fubini extension. For a genuine finite heat past,
$r$ is the product of the original signed increment kernels
and $X$ their sum; hence the identity applies directly, with
the original cylindrical past intact. No positivity or
unconditional Gaussian law of this past is assumed.

\subsection*{Exact partition representation and its boundary}
For every finite grid $0=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_n=H$ and every
real coordinate list $x_0=0,x_1,\ldots,x_n$, ordinary telescoping
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:completedtelescope}
 \sum_{j=0}^{n-1}g_{t_j,t_{j+1}}(x_j,x_{j+1})
       = f_q(x_n)-\E_H(q).
\end{equation}
There is no limit or unproved cancellation in this identity:
every intermediate $u_q(t_j,x_j)$ occurs once with each sign.
In particular it holds on every continuous zero-start path
with $x_j=\omega(t_j)$. The gain sum is identical for any
two grids with these same endpoints on that same path.
Moreover $t\mapsto u_q(t,\omega(t))$ is continuous for every
such path. Joint continuity follows from the preceding
state continuity and continuous path evaluation, and the
map from the compact-open path space to continuous curves
is continuous. Thus these are genuine scalar path observables,
rather than merely assigned finite Fourier coefficients.

The telescope does not by itself produce a stochastic integral:
it simply names the sum of actual increments of this specified
backward observable. The zero-pairing statement
\eqref{eq:completedgainzero} additionally gives its precise
finite signed-past centering, but does not construct an infinite
signed path law, a positive martingale filtration, an It\^o
isometry or an arbitrary predictable-integrand completion.
Nor does joint scalar continuity contradict the uniform-path
Bochner obstruction in the preceding section: a fixed countable
quotient norm, scalar product integration and the full
$C_b(\X)$ norm are distinct spaces and topologies.

All mechanisms here are classical Fourier convolution,
completion, Fubini and telescoping. Nishioka's already compared
primary higher-order calculus remains an antecedent, and no
priority claim is added. The contribution of this section is
the exact whole-completion domain and the explicitly realized
signed past extension, with all integrability conditions.

\section{Antecedents, formal correspondence and limits of the claim}
