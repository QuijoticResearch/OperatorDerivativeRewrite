\begin{abstract}
We summarize verified stochastic constructions for the actual process
$Z_t=W(|B_t|)$ on the existing independent scenery--clock probability space.
Its uniform-grid quartic variation converges in probability to $3T$, whereas
quadratic variation diverges. Separately convergent raw first and second
Taylor channels already fail for the quadratic polynomial. Signed spatial
Simpson sums nevertheless converge for every global $C^3$ one-form, with
uniform control over grid prefixes, canonical bounded random horizons, and
deterministic $C^1$ time weights. An explicit clipped cosine construction
converges in observable $L^2$ and in the original intrinsic graph dual, while
its limit stays outside the strong joint derivative domain. These are
specified symmetric formulas, not completion of adapted physical-time
It\^o--Tanaka calculus.
\end{abstract}

\section{Process, prescription, and topology}\label{sec:model}
Let $W$ be the constructed continuous Brownian scenery on nonnegative spatial
time and $B$ the independent constructed Brownian clock. The process studied
here is precisely
\begin{equation}\label{eq:process}
 Z_t=W(|B_t|),\qquad t\geq0,
\end{equation}
under the original product probability law. This fixes the reflected-clock
convention; no equality with every other convention for iterated Brownian
motion is asserted. Its paths are continuous for every sample in the chosen
realization, and evaluation is jointly measurable. We retain $Z_0$ in finite
identities and targets rather than simplify arbitrary $L^2$ representatives.

Fix $T>0$ and $N=n+1$. Write $t_i=iT/N$, $Z_i=Z_{t_i}$ and
$\Delta_iZ=Z_{i+1}-Z_i$. Unless stated otherwise, stochastic limits are in
probability under this same original law, along all $n\to\infty$.
Samplewise identities are distinguished from probability limits.

\section{Variation and a concrete obstruction}\label{sec:variation}
\begin{theorem}[Actual uniform-grid variation]\label{thm:variation}
For each fixed positive $T$,
\[
 Q_{4,N}:=\sum_{i<N}(\Delta_iZ)^4\Pconv 3T,
 \qquad
 \mathbf P\!\left\{\sum_{i<N}(\Delta_iZ)^2\leq K\right\}\longrightarrow0
 \quad(K\in\R).
\]
The maximum absolute grid increment tends to zero on every sample.
\end{theorem}
\lean{IBMUniformQuarticVariation}{quartic_tendstoInMeasure}
\lean{IBMUniformFifthRemainder}{mesh_tendsto}
\lean{IBMActualQuadraticDivergence}{quadratic_lower_tail}
\begin{proof}[Verified mechanisms]
The quartic theorem uses the actual conditional Gaussian contractions,
overlap estimates and reflected-clock crossing penalty. It is not inferred
from its expectation alone. The deterministic inequality
\[
 Q_{4,N}\leq\left(\max_{i<N}|\Delta_iZ|\right)^2 Q_{2,N}
\]
then forces divergence of $Q_{2,N}$ in probability. The formal proof first
shows $(1+Q_{2,N})^{-1}\Pconv0$ and controls each lower-tail event by a
strictly positive reciprocal threshold. No almost-sure full-sequence
divergence or divergence rate is claimed.
\end{proof}

For $f(z)=z^2$, the two raw ranks satisfy, on every grid and sample,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cancellation}
 L_N+Q_{2,N}=Z_T^2-Z_0^2,
 \qquad L_N=\sum_{i<N}2Z_i\Delta_iZ.
\end{equation}
Neither $L_N$ nor $Q_{2,N}$ has any finite real-valued random probability
limit. Thus convergence of a combined channel expression does not license
separate unrenormalized integrals.
\lean{IBMActualQuadraticDivergence}{first_identity}
\lean{IBMActualQuadraticDivergence}{first_no_finite_limit}

\section{Combined Taylor formula and signed Simpson sums}\label{sec:simpson}
\begin{theorem}[Combined lower Taylor channels]\label{thm:taylor}
For every global $C^4$ real function $f$,
\begin{align*}
 \sum_{i<N}\left[f'(Z_i)\Delta_iZ
    +\frac12 f''(Z_i)(\Delta_iZ)^2
    +\frac16 f'''(Z_i)(\Delta_iZ)^3\right]
 \Pconv{}& f(Z_T)-f(Z_0)\\
 &-\frac18\int_0^T f^{(4)}(Z_t)\,dt.
\end{align*}
No fifth derivative or globally bounded derivative is assumed.
\end{theorem}
\lean{IBMActualTaylorChain}{channels_tendstoInMeasure}
The coefficient $1/8$ is $3/24$: the actual continuously weighted quartic
limit supplies $3\int f^{(4)}(Z_t)dt$, while the fourth Taylor coefficient is
$1/24$. Compact Peano control proves decay of the total absolute Taylor
error on the entire $C^4$ domain. The channels in Theorem~\ref{thm:taylor}
remain together.

Define a signed increment using only its integrand and spatial endpoints:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:simpson}
 S_g(x,y)=\frac{y-x}{6}
   \left[g(x)+4g\!\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)+g(y)\right].
\end{equation}
The middle argument is a \emph{spatial} midpoint, not $Z$ at a middle physical
time. The sign of $y-x$ is retained.
\begin{theorem}[Actual symmetric spatial integration]\label{thm:simpson}
For every global $C^3$ real function $g$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spatial-limit}
 \sum_{i<N}S_g(Z_i,Z_{i+1})\Pconv J_g(T),
 \qquad J_g(t)=\int_{Z_0}^{Z_t}g(z)\,dz.
\end{equation}
Each target is a genuine finite oriented Lebesgue integral. For continuous
$g$, $J_g$ is already a jointly measurable process with continuous paths and
a measurable realization in continuous-path space.
\end{theorem}
\lean{IBMActualSimpson}{sums_tendstoInMeasure}
\lean{IBMUniformSimpsonProcess}{integralPath_measurable}
\begin{proof}[Verified mechanism]
For $f\in C^4$, the Simpson error relative to $f(y)-f(x)$ is uniformly
$o(|y-x|^4)$ on compact spatial intervals. This follows from compact Peano
control for $f$ and for the third-order Taylor remainder of $f'$.
Every sample has a compact range and vanishing grid mesh. Total error is
therefore eventually bounded by $\varepsilon Q_{4,N}$ for each
$\varepsilon>0$. Quartic tightness and the subsequence criterion give
probability convergence. For general $g\in C^3$, the independently defined
ordinary primitive $f(x)=\int_0^xg$ is $C^4$ by FTC and has derivative $g$.
\end{proof}

This is convergence of an actual approximation, not an endpoint definition
of the finite sums. For example,
\[
 S_{5z^4}(x,y)-(y^5-x^5)=\frac{(y-x)^5}{24},
\]
which is generally nonzero. In contrast, $S_{4z^3}(x,y)=y^4-x^4$ exactly.
For $f(z)=z^4$, Theorem~\ref{thm:taylor} has correction $3T$, whereas the
Simpson rule for $f'$ telescopes exactly. These are distinct prescriptions,
consistent with the cancellation in~\eqref{eq:cancellation}.

\section{Uniform prefixes and bounded random horizons}\label{sec:horizons}
Let $P_{g,N}(m)=\sum_{i<m}S_g(Z_i,Z_{i+1})$ on the same $N$-cell grid.
\begin{theorem}[Uniform grid-prefix realization]\label{thm:prefix}
For every global $C^3$ one-form,
\[
 \max_{0\leq m\leq N}
   |P_{g,N}(m)-J_g(mT/N)|\Pconv0.
\]
\end{theorem}
\lean{IBMUniformSimpsonProcess}{maximum_tendstoInMeasure}
One compact modulus controls every prefix; its quartic prefix mass is at
most the full nonnegative quartic mass. No union bound growing with $N$ is
used. This is uniformity at \emph{grid prefixes}. No continuous interpolation
of the finite sums, or uniform real-time approximation theorem, is supplied.

\begin{theorem}[Canonical bounded random endpoint]\label{thm:random}
Let $\tau$ be any measurable nonnegative time with the pointwise bound
$\tau\leq T$. Put
\[
 k_N(\tau)=\left\lfloor\frac{N\tau}{T}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad \tau_N=\frac{T}{N}k_N(\tau).
\]
Then $k_N(\tau)\leq N$, $0\leq\tau-\tau_N<T/N$, and
$P_{g,N}(k_N(\tau))\Pconv J_g(\tau)$ for every global $C^3$ one-form.
The approximants and target are measurable.
\end{theorem}
\lean{IBMSimpsonRandomHorizon}{stopped_sums_tendstoInMeasure}
Uniform prefix control and every-sample continuity of $J_g$ prove the result.
The time need not be a stopping time; an example chooses $T$ or $T/2$
according to the sign of $Z_T$. This is not optional sampling of a martingale.

\section{Deterministic time weights and spacetime chains}\label{sec:time}
\begin{theorem}[Actual time-weighted formula]\label{thm:weighted}
For global $h\in C^1(\R)$ and $g\in C^3(\R)$,
\[
 \sum_{i<N}h(t_i)S_g(Z_i,Z_{i+1})
 \Pconv h(T)J_g(T)-\int_0^T h'(t)J_g(t)\,dt.
\]
The time correction is finite on every sample and measurable. No global
growth bound or expectation moment is assumed.
\end{theorem}
\lean{IBMTimeWeightedSimpson}{weighted_tendstoInMeasure}
\begin{proof}[Verified mechanism]
Finite Abel summation bounds the error in replacing Simpson increments by
actual increments of $J_g$ by $M_N(B+KT)$, where $M_N$ is the prefix maximum
from Theorem~\ref{thm:prefix}, $|h|\leq B$ on $[0,T]$, and $h$ has compact
Lipschitz bound $K$. These constants follow from $C^1$ regularity, not global
assumptions. Finite product telescoping identifies the remaining path sum.
FTC and uniform continuity of each continuous sample path give
$\sum J_g(t_{i+1})\Delta_i h\to\int h'J_g$ samplewise. The time integral is
proved directly through cell integrals, rather than assumed as a formal
Riemann--Stieltjes limit.
\end{proof}

In particular, for $f\in C^4$,
\begin{align*}
 \sum_{i<N}h(t_i)S_{f'}(Z_i,Z_{i+1})
 \Pconv{}& h(T)f(Z_T)-h(0)f(Z_0)\\
 &-\int_0^T h'(t)f(Z_t)\,dt.
\end{align*}
\lean{IBMTimeWeightedSimpson}{derivative_weighted_tendstoInMeasure}
This is a separable spacetime chain rule. Examples $tZ_t$, $tZ_t^2$, and
$t^2\sin(Z_t)$ are independently tested in Lean. General two-variable
$F(t,z)$ and random time coefficients remain separate obligations.

\section{A precise graph-dual connection and strong-domain limit}\label{sec:dual}
Let $D$ be the existing closed, densely defined intrinsic joint derivative
on the original observable $L^2$, and let $\delta=D^*$ denote its original
Hilbert adjoint. Its full adjoint-domain graph tests support the pre-existing
bounded graph-dual map $\mathfrak D$. Write $\operatorname{emb}$ for the
existing embedding of strong two-channel fields into that test dual.

Take the actual cosine Simpson sum $A_N=\sum S_{\cos}(Z_i,Z_{i+1})$ and define
\[
 C_N=\max(-1,\min(1,A_N)).
\]
Clipping is an explicit modification: already $S_{\cos}(0,2\pi)=-2\pi/3$.
It is $1$-Lipschitz and fixes the sine target, so it preserves convergence in
probability. The bounded actual functions $C_N$ are IBM observables.
\begin{theorem}[Bounded realization in the original graph dual]\label{thm:dual}
For every $T>0$, $C_N\to\sin(Z_T)$ strongly in observable $L^2$ and
\[
 \mathfrak D C_N\longrightarrow \mathfrak D\sin(Z_T)
 \quad\hbox{in the original graph-dual norm}.
\]
For every $v\in\operatorname{Dom}(\delta)$, the actual integrable pairings obey
\[
 \mathbf E[C_N\,\delta v]
   \longrightarrow (\mathfrak D\sin(Z_T))(\operatorname{testOf}v).
\]
\end{theorem}
\lean{IBMSimpsonGraphDual}{valueClass_tendsto}
\lean{IBMSimpsonGraphDual}{actual_pairing_tendsto}
Bounded dominated convergence on a.e. subsubsequences supplies strong $L^2$
convergence; continuity of the original graph-dual map and its evaluations
then supplies the stated derivative and pairing limits. No $L^2$ convergence
of the \emph{unclipped} sums is asserted.

The previously verified sine observation is nevertheless outside
$\operatorname{Dom}(D)$ for every positive $T$; its graph-dual derivative is
nonzero and is not $\operatorname{emb}(V)$ for any strong joint field $V$.
Any strong-domain sequence tracking $C_N$ in $L^2$ has embedded gradients
converging in the graph dual, but its strong gradient norms tend to infinity.
The tracking premise is explicit; the finite $C_N$ themselves are not asserted
to lie in the strong domain.
\lean{IBMSimpsonGraphDual}{limit_obstruction}
\lean{IBMSimpsonGraphDual}{tracked_graph_escape}

\section{What is established and what remains}\label{sec:scope}
The results construct specified signed quadratures, genuine process-valued
targets, grid-prefix and bounded random-horizon convergence, a deterministic
time-weighted chain formula, and an exact bounded graph-dual realization.
They do not identify the spatial one-form with a Skorohod integrand or the
Simpson integral with a causal divergence.

Existing predictable integration on the IBM observation filtration uses a
separately constructed terminal-observable martingale. Sharing a filtration
does not identify that integrator with physical $Z$. The earlier change-of-
integrator draft concerns that verified martingale calculus, and remains
valid on its own original domains.

Open repository obligations include general $F(t,z)$, controlled random
time-dependent coefficients, continuous finite-sum interpolation and its
real-time uniform topology, arbitrary partitions, required causal or adjoint
identifications, and nonsmooth physical-time It\^o--Tanaka formulas. Strong
domain exclusions cannot be repaired by silently assuming membership.
The verified stopping contract for the full IBM campaign has not been met.
