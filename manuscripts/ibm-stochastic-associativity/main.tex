\title{Observable martingales on Brownian-time paths:\\
predictable energy, associativity and uniform reconstruction}
\author{Project Stardust}
\date{Reviewed editorial draft, revision 3, October 29, 2026}
\begin{document}\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
On a constructed Brownian-time Brownian probability space, predictable
integrals against terminal-observable martingales admit an exact change of
energy measure. This gives a literal product isometry on the entire
output-energy $L^2$ space and associativity without boundedness of the
second integrand. We then retain all signed cross-channel covariances,
pass through their null quotient and completion, and identify every common
bounded predictable action with the original output-channel integral.
A fixed countable family of actual integrals reconstructs continuous
curves of observables uniformly on compact parameter sets, simultaneously
over all causal horizons. Original native graph tests identify the
family's scalar coefficients, with a quantitative graph-dual norm bound.
A bounded two-source Gaussian recovery transfers
this convergence to the original intrinsic divergence graph. The proofs
separate raw predictability, right-germ martingales and source-operator
domains. These are classical integration and Hilbert-space mechanisms
assembled as a formalization companion; they do not provide a temporal
It\^o or Clark--Ocone calculus for the uncorrected Brownian-time path.
\end{abstract}

\section{Probability space and the two filtrations}
Let $B$ and $W$ be independent standard Brownian motions constructed on
two independent countable Gaussian source spaces. The process considered
here is
\[
 Z_t=B_{|W_t|},\qquad t\geq0.
\]
The outer Brownian motion is one-sided and the clock is reflected.
Thus this paper concerns this particular Brownian-time Brownian motion;
terminology for two-sided versions of iterated Brownian motion does not
change the process used below. Write $(\Omega,\mathcal A,\Pp)$ for the
joint source space, $\HH=L^2(\Omega,\mathcal A,\Pp;\R)$, and
\[
 \mathcal F_t^Z=\sigma(Z_s:0\leq s\leq t),\qquad
 \mathcal F_\infty^Z=\sigma(Z_s:s\geq0),\qquad
 \OO=L^2(\mathcal F_\infty^Z)\subset\HH.
\]
All $L^2$ spaces consist of equivalence classes. Statements about
measurability of such a class mean that it has a representative with the
stated measurability.

The raw filtration $\mathcal F^Z$ is used for integrands. Let
$\PP$ be its predictable sigma algebra on
$S=[0,\infty)\times\Omega$. It is generated by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rect}
 (s,t]\times A,\quad 0\leq s\leq t<\infty,
 \quad A\in\mathcal F_s^Z,
 \qquad \{0\}\times A,\quad A\in\mathcal F_0^Z.
\end{equation}
For martingales use the right-continuous ambient-null augmentation
\[
 \mathcal G_t=\bigcap_{u>t}(\mathcal F_u^Z\vee\mathcal N),
 \qquad P_t f=\E[f\mid\mathcal G_t],
\]
where $\mathcal N$ denotes ambient measurable null sets and their ambient
measurable subsets. Passing to the completion of the probability space
when applying a classical regularization theorem leaves the $L^2$
objects unchanged. The frozen construction realizes $P_t$ as orthogonal
projection onto
\[
 \bigcap_{u>t}L^2(\mathcal F_u^Z).
\]
It proves the identification with $L^2(\mathcal G_t)$, rather than
assuming that an intersection of raw sigma algebras has the desired
representatives. In particular
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projections}
 P_sP_t=P_{\min(s,t)},\quad \norm{P_tf}\leq\norm f,
 \quad P_tf\in\OO,\quad
 P_nf\longrightarrow f\quad(f\in\OO).
\end{equation}
The last assertion follows also from increasing closed-subspace
projection convergence: every raw finite past is contained in its right
germ, and their union generates $\mathcal F_\infty^Z$ modulo null sets.

For a fixed $F\in\HH$, $M_t^F=P_tF$ is a square-integrable
$\mathcal G$-martingale. It has right-continuous $L^2$ dependence on
time. This fixed-terminal martingale differs from $Z$; no martingale
assumption on $Z$ enters any construction. Write
\[
 G_0=P_0,\qquad \OO_0=\OO\cap\ker G_0.
\]
We retain the whole initial germ in the statements, even where particular
observables have zero initial projection.

\section{The original predictable integral}
We first specify the energy measure and integral to which later
associativity statements refer. They are established objects, not
definitions imposed by the desired change-of-integrator identity.

\begin{proposition}[Finite predictable energy]\label{prop:measure}
For every $F\in\HH$ there is a unique finite measure $\mu_F$ on
$(S,\PP)$ satisfying
\begin{align}
 \mu_F((s,t]\times A)
   &=\E\one_A(M_t^F-M_s^F)^2,\quad A\in\mathcal F_s^Z,
       \label{eq:energyrect}\\
 \mu_F(\{0\}\times A)&=0,\qquad
 \mu_F(S)\leq\norm F^2.\label{eq:mass}
\end{align}
Finite linear combinations of rectangle indicators are dense in
$L^2(\PP,\mu_F)$, with origin indicators representing zero.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The existence assertion is the finite Dol\'eans-measure construction for
a square-integrable martingale, restricted to the raw predictable sigma
algebra. For precision, on the usual completion of $\mathcal G$ take a
c\`adl\`ag modification of $M^F$ and the predictable compensator $A^F$ of
$(M^F)^2$, normalized by $A_0^F=0$. The classical martingale
regularization and compensator theorems apply: $M^F$ is closed by $F$,
and its stopped squares are uniformly integrable, since they are
bounded by conditional expectations of the integrable variable $F^2$.
On each finite horizon $(M^F)^2-A^F$ is a martingale and
\[
 \E A_T^F=\norm{P_TF}^2-\norm{P_0F}^2\leq\norm F^2.
\]
The positive measure
$\E\int_{(0,\infty)}\one_E(r,\omega)\,dA_r^F(\omega)$,
restricted to $\PP$, is therefore finite. For
$A\in\mathcal F_s^Z\subset\mathcal G_s$, conditional martingale
identities give
\[
 \E\one_A(A_t^F-A_s^F)
 =\E\one_A((M_t^F)^2-(M_s^F)^2)
 =\E\one_A(M_t^F-M_s^F)^2.
\]
This verifies the required rectangle values. The compensator here
belongs only to the auxiliary martingale $M^F$.

The frozen proof instead constructs the raw rectangle content directly,
proves continuity at the empty set by compact-fiber approximation, and
extends that content. The classical construction just given has the same
values, so uniqueness identifies the measures. Indeed, rectangles and
origin sets form a generating semiring, and a countable increasing family
$(0,n]\times\Omega$, together with the origin, covers $S$.
Finite-measure uniqueness applies. The span of its indicators is
$L^2$ dense by the monotone class theorem and truncation; the origin has
zero mass. This also proves the asserted density for the directly
constructed formal measure. Classical measure and regularization
background can be found in~\cite{MP}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Isometric extension]\label{prop:integral}
There is a unique linear isometry
\[
 I_F:L^2(\PP,\mu_F)\longrightarrow\HH
\]
which on a raw predictable rectangle has value
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cellintegral}
 I_F(\one_{(s,t]\times A})=\one_A(P_t-P_s)F.
\end{equation}
Its range is contained in $\OO_0$. For $H\in L^2(\mu_F)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cutoff}
 I_F(\one_{(0,r]}H)=P_r I_F(H),\qquad r\geq0.
\end{equation}
Time indicators in this paper include the entire sample space.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Refine a finite collection of rectangles by all of their time endpoints.
The resulting process has the form
$H=\sum_j h_j\one_{(t_{j-1},t_j]}$, where each $h_j$ is a finite
linear combination of $\mathcal F_{t_{j-1}}^Z$ event indicators.
Assign the sum $\sum_j h_j(M_{t_j}^F-M_{t_{j-1}}^F)$.
Distinct time-cell outputs are orthogonal: the earlier one is
$\mathcal G_{t_{j-1}}$ measurable and the later increment has zero
conditional expectation there. Within one cell, partition the event
indicators into disjoint atoms. Formula~\eqref{eq:energyrect} then gives
\[
 \norm{\sum_j h_j(M_{t_j}^F-M_{t_{j-1}}^F)}^2
   =\int_S H^2\,d\mu_F.
\]
In particular null representatives have zero output. Refinement changes
neither process nor output, so this defines an isometry on the rectangle
span, and density gives its unique extension.

Each finite output is observable at some finite raw horizon strictly
larger than all the endpoints: $P_tF$ is measurable modulo null sets
at every raw time $u>t$. Its conditional expectation at $\mathcal G_0$
is zero by the martingale identity and raw adaptedness of $h_j$.
Closedness of $\OO$ and of $\ker G_0$ passes both properties to the
limit. Finally, conditional expectation of a single cell output at
$\mathcal G_r$ is zero for $r\leq s$, is
$\one_A(P_r-P_s)F$ for $s<r<t$, and is the whole output for
$r\geq t$. These are exactly the integrals of its time cutoff.
Linearity and the contractivity of cutoff and $P_r$ prove
\eqref{eq:cutoff} for every $H$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Raw rectangle pull-out]\label{lem:pullout}
If $Y=I_F(H)$ and $A\in\mathcal F_s^Z$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pullout}
 I_F(\one_{(s,t]\times A}H)=\one_A(P_t-P_s)Y.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First consider the future mask $(s,\infty)\times A$ and one input
cell $(a,b]\times C$. If $b\leq s$, its product is zero, as is
$\one_A(I-P_s)$ of the cell output. Otherwise replace its left endpoint
by $\ell=\max(a,s)$, and replace its event by $A\cap C$.
The event belongs to $\mathcal F_\ell^Z$, and the integral of the
masked cell is
\[
 \one_{A\cap C}(P_b-P_\ell)F
 =\one_A(I-P_s)\bigl(\one_C(P_b-P_a)F\bigr).
\]
The equality follows by splitting $s\leq a$ and $a<s<b$ and applying
conditional expectation. It extends from the cell span to all $H$:
both mask multiplication in $L^2(\mu_F)$ and the output operators
$\one_A(I-P_s)$ are bounded. Subtract the analogous future identity
at $t$, using $A\in\mathcal F_t^Z$. The result is
\eqref{eq:pullout}.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact change of energy and literal products}
The pull-out lemma compares the original integral with the original
energy measure of its actual output. It supplies the domain information
needed for an unbounded second integrand.

\begin{theorem}[Energy transformation]\label{thm:change}
For $F\in\HH$, $H\in L^2(\PP,\mu_F)$ and $Y=I_F(H)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:change}
 \mu_Y=H^2\mu_F
\end{equation}
as finite measures on the whole raw predictable sigma algebra.
Here and below products are formed from predictable measurable
representatives.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The right side is a finite measure of mass $\norm H_{L^2(\mu_F)}^2$.
For a raw rectangle $R=(s,t]\times A$, the isometry and
Lemma~\ref{lem:pullout} give
\[
 \int_R H^2\,d\mu_F
 =\norm{I_F(\one_RH)}^2
 =\E\one_A((P_t-P_s)Y)^2
 =\mu_Y(R).
\]
Both measures vanish on origin sets. The generating-semiring uniqueness
argument from Proposition~\ref{prop:measure} proves equality on $\PP$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Full-domain associativity]\label{thm:associativity}
With $F,H,Y$ as above, multiplication defines a linear isometry
\[
 T_H:L^2(\PP,\mu_Y)\longrightarrow L^2(\PP,\mu_F),
 \qquad T_HK=HK.
\]
For every $K$ in this entire output-energy space,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:assoc}
 \norm{T_HK}_{L^2(\mu_F)}=\norm K_{L^2(\mu_Y)},
 \qquad I_Y(K)=I_F(T_HK).
\end{equation}
There is no boundedness requirement on $H$ or $K$ beyond their stated
energy domains.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The density identity gives
\[
 \int H^2K^2\,d\mu_F=\int K^2\,d\mu_Y<\infty.
\]
It also handles the null ideals. If $K=L$ outside a $\mu_Y$-null
set $N$, then $\int_N H^2\,d\mu_F=0$, so $H=0$ for
$\mu_F$ almost every point of $N$. Hence $HK=HL$ in $L^2(\mu_F)$.
Changing the representative of $H$ changes neither the density measure
nor the product class. Additivity and homogeneity hold for measurable
representatives and descend to classes. The displayed equality proves
the norm identity.

For $K=\one_{(s,t]\times A}$, the cell formula for $I_Y$ and the
pull-out lemma show
\[
 I_Y(K)=\one_A(P_t-P_s)Y=I_F(\one_{(s,t]\times A}H).
\]
Origin indicators have zero class. Both $I_Y$ and $I_FT_H$ are continuous
isometries on the same $L^2(\mu_Y)$ space, and they agree on its dense
rectangle span. This proves~\eqref{eq:assoc}.
\end{proof}

The product theorem concerns two different energy measures. For example,
if $H$ vanishes on a set of positive $\mu_F$ mass, $\mu_Y$ forgets
that set, and multiplication by $H$ removes the ambiguity there. No
reverse absolute continuity is asserted. This is not an assertion that
products of arbitrary $L^2(\mu_F)$ functions remain in $L^2(\mu_F)$.
The associativity and domain-equivalence mechanism is classical; for a
continuous-martingale comparison see~\cite[Proposition 5.4]{Liao}.
Continuity of the auxiliary IBM-observable martingales is not assumed
here.

\section{Signed joint energy and common multipliers}
A family of channels must retain their cross covariances. Let
$\mathbf F=(F_i)_{i\in\mathcal I}$ be any family in $\HH$. Its raw
input space is the algebraic direct sum
\[
 \mathcal R_{\mathbf F}=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathcal I}L^2(\PP,\mu_{F_i}),
 \qquad J_{\rm raw}h=\sum_i I_{F_i}(h_i).
\]
Only finitely many components of a raw input are nonzero. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jointenergy}
 \inner h k_{\rm en}=\inner{J_{\rm raw}h}{J_{\rm raw}k}_{\HH},
 \qquad \mathcal N_{\mathbf F}=\ker J_{\rm raw}.
\end{equation}
Quotient by $\mathcal N_{\mathbf F}$ and complete in this inner
product. Denote the resulting Hilbert space by $\mathcal C_{\mathbf F}$.
The actual output extends uniquely to an isometry
$J_{\mathbf F}:\mathcal C_{\mathbf F}\to\HH$ with closed range in
$\OO_0$. This is not the direct sum norm obtained by discarding cross
terms.

For completeness those cross terms are genuine signed measures. Set
\[
 \nu_{FG}=\tfrac14(\mu_{F+G}-\mu_{F-G}).
\]
The linearity of $P_t$ and~\eqref{eq:energyrect} give
\[
 \nu_{FG}((s,t]\times A)
  =\E\one_A((P_t-P_s)F)((P_t-P_s)G).
\]
The same refinement argument as for the isometry proves the pairing
formula on rectangle-step inputs. Its extension is justified by the
following measure estimate.

\begin{lemma}[Cross-energy integrability]\label{lem:cross}
For $H\in L^2(\mu_F)$ and $K\in L^2(\mu_G)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cross}
 \int |HK|\,d|\nu_{FG}|\leq\norm H_{L^2(\mu_F)}
      \norm K_{L^2(\mu_G)},\qquad
 \inner{I_FH}{I_GK}=\int HK\,d\nu_{FG}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Rectangle values and finite signed-measure uniqueness first give
\[
 \mu_{aF+bG}=a^2\mu_F+2ab\nu_{FG}+b^2\mu_G
 \quad(a,b\in\R).
\]
In particular $\mu_F+\mu_G\pm2\nu_{FG}$ are positive. Consequently
$\nu_{FG}$, and hence its variation, is absolutely continuous with
respect to $\lambda=\mu_F+\mu_G$: on a $\lambda$-null set the two
positive-measure inequalities force its signed value on every subset
to vanish. Write $a=d\mu_F/d\lambda$, $b=d\mu_G/d\lambda$ and
$c=d\nu_{FG}/d\lambda$. Positivity of $\mu_{F+qG}$ says
$a+2qc+q^2b\geq0$ almost everywhere for each rational $q$.
A common exceptional set suffices for all rationals, and continuity
gives the inequality for all real $q$. Its discriminant yields
$c^2\leq ab$ almost everywhere. The variation density is $v=|c|$.
Thus
\[
 \int |HK|v\,d\lambda
 \leq\int |H|\sqrt a\,|K|\sqrt b\,d\lambda
 \leq\Big(\int H^2\,d\mu_F\Big)^{1/2}
       \Big(\int K^2\,d\mu_G\Big)^{1/2}.
\]
Approximate $H,K$ separately by rectangle-step functions in their own
energy norms. The bound just proved controls both product-error terms
in $L^1(|\nu_{FG}|)$. Their actual integral outputs converge by
isometry. Passing to the limit in the finite pairing identity proves
the second formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Bounded common action]\label{thm:multiplier}
Let $g:S\to\R$ be raw predictable and pointwise bounded by $C\geq0$.
Multiplication of every component by this same $g$ descends to a
bounded linear operator $M_g$ on $\mathcal C_{\mathbf F}$ with
$\norm{M_g}\leq C$. For all $z$ in that completion, setting
$Y=J_{\mathbf F}z$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:boundedassoc}
 J_{\mathbf F}(M_gz)=I_Y([g]_{\mu_Y}).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Put $g^\perp=(C^2-g^2)^{1/2}$, a bounded predictable function.
For raw $h$, Lemma~\ref{lem:cross} and
$g^2+(g^\perp)^2=C^2$ imply the exact partition identity
\[
 \norm{J_{\rm raw}(gh)}^2+
 \norm{J_{\rm raw}(g^\perp h)}^2
 =C^2\norm{J_{\rm raw}h}^2.
\]
Both squared norms on the left are nonnegative, including every signed
cross term. Hence $\norm{J_{\rm raw}(gh)}\leq
C\norm{J_{\rm raw}h}$. This proves preservation of the null relation
and extension to the completion.

We next give the single-channel representation needed to identify the
extension. For any $f\in\OO$, the rectangle formula yields
\[
 I_f(\one_{(0,n]})=P_nf-P_0f\longrightarrow f-P_0f.
\]
The isometric range of $I_f$ is closed, so some $H_f\in L^2(\mu_f)$
has output $f-P_0f$. Since $Y\in\OO_0$, this gives
$K\in L^2(\mu_Y)$ with $I_YK=Y$. This is a representation by a
channel indexed by the target itself; it asserts no predictable
representation property for a single fixed driving martingale.

Append the channel $Y$ to the family. The same common-multiplier bound
applied to a raw family input $h$ minus the appended input $K$ gives
\[
 \norm{J_{\rm raw}(gh)-I_Y(gK)}
 \leq C\norm{J_{\rm raw}h-Y}.
\]
Approximate $z$ by raw inputs and pass to the limit. It follows that
$J_{\mathbf F}(M_gz)=I_Y(gK)$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:associativity}, applied to $I_YK=Y$, now gives
$I_Y(gK)=I_Y([g]_{\mu_Y})$. Here the right-hand integral uses the
original energy measure of the output $Y$; $[g]$ is admissible because
that measure is finite. This proves~\eqref{eq:boundedassoc}.
\end{proof}

The hypothesis that $g$ is common to all channels is essential. If two
channels are identical, the raw pair $(H,-H)$ has zero joint energy.
Multiplying only its first component by one and its second by zero
usually gives a nonzero output. Thus unrelated channelwise multipliers
need not even descend to the quotient. This elementary example explains
why diagonal energy bounds alone would not prove the theorem.

\section{A fixed countable stochastic family}
The preceding target-indexed representation yields a fixed countable
family on the separable observable space. The joint Gaussian product
has separable $L^2$, and $\OO_0$ is a closed subspace. Choose a
countable Hilbert basis $(e_i)_{i\in I}$ of $\OO_0$, permitting a
finite or empty index set when appropriate. Regard each $e_i$ as a
terminal channel and choose $h_i\in L^2(\mu_{e_i})$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:basis}
 I_{e_i}(h_i)=e_i,\qquad \norm{h_i}=1.
\end{equation}
Existence follows from the self-representation argument because
$P_0e_i=0$. These are actual raw predictable integrands. Their
construction uses a Hilbert basis and closed ranges; no explicit causal
gradient formula is asserted.

For the family $\mathbf e=(e_i)$, $J=J_{\mathbf e}$ maps the completed
space $\mathcal C_{\mathbf e}$ isometrically onto $\OO_0$: its closed
range contains every $e_i$. For $f\in\OO$ set
\[
 r(f)=f-P_0f,\quad c_i(f)=\inner{e_i}{f},\quad
 L(f)=J^{-1}r(f),\quad
 Q_A(f)=\sum_{i\in A}[\iota_i(c_i(f)h_i)],
\]
where $A\subset I$ is finite and $\iota_i$ inserts a single raw
channel. The bracket means its image in the null quotient and
completion. Parseval gives both
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parseval}
 JQ_A(f)=\sum_{i\in A}c_i(f)e_i,\qquad
 \norm{Q_A(f)-L(f)}^2
 =\norm{r(f)}^2-\sum_{i\in A}|c_i(f)|^2.
\end{equation}
The maps $r$, $L$ and $c_i$ are continuous and linear.

Stopping is the contraction
\[
 S_r=J^{-1}P_rJ\quad\hbox{on }\mathcal C_{\mathbf e}.
\]
It is well-defined since $P_r$ preserves $\OO_0$. On a single input it
equals literal time cutoff, by~\eqref{eq:cutoff} and injectivity of
$J$. Define the finite actual output
\begin{equation}\label{eq:actualfinite}
 U_{A,r}(f)=\sum_{i\in A}I_{e_i}
     (\one_{(0,r]}c_i(f)h_i)=JS_rQ_A(f).
\end{equation}
Also $JS_rL(f)=P_rf-P_0f$. Since the entire initial germ is subtracted,
this identity holds without replacing $P_0f$ by a scalar mean.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform reconstruction]\label{thm:uniform}
Let $u:X\to\OO$ be norm continuous on a topological space $X$,
and let $K\subset X$ be compact. As $A$ runs through the directed
set of finite subsets of $I$ ordered by inclusion,
\begin{align}
 \sup_{x\in K}\norm{Q_A(u(x))-L(u(x))}&\longrightarrow0,
       \label{eq:uniforminput}\\
 \sup_{x\in K}\sup_{r\geq0}
 \norm{U_{A,r}(u(x))-(P_ru(x)-P_0u(x))}_{\HH}
       &\longrightarrow0.\label{eq:uniformoutput}
\end{align}
These are suprema of Hilbert norms, not samplewise suprema.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For fixed $A$ the squared error in~\eqref{eq:parseval} is a
continuous nonnegative function of $x$. It decreases when $A$ grows
and converges pointwise to zero by Parseval. For a direct compactness
proof, fix $\varepsilon>0$. For each $x\in K$ choose $A_x$ making
the squared error smaller than $\varepsilon^2$ at $x$.
Continuity gives a neighborhood with the same strict bound.
A finite subcover of $K$ and the union of its $A_x$ yield one finite
$A_0$ which works throughout $K$. Antitonicity gives the bound for
every $A\supset A_0$. This proves~\eqref{eq:uniforminput}; it is the
finite-subset form of Dini's argument used in the formal proof.

For every $r$ the contraction estimate is
\[
 \norm{S_rQ_A(f)-S_rL(f)}\leq\norm{Q_A(f)-L(f)}.
\]
The same bound is independent of $r$. Apply the isometry $J$ and
the exact identities~\eqref{eq:actualfinite} to obtain
\eqref{eq:uniformoutput}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The actual Brownian-time curve]\label{prop:actual}
For every finite $T$,
\[
 \sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\norm{U_{A,t}(Z_t)-Z_t}_{\HH}
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
If $\varphi$ is a bounded $C^1$ function with bounded continuous
derivative, the analogous diagonal convergence holds for
$\varphi(Z_t)-P_0\varphi(Z_t)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Condition on the clock and use the Brownian increment isometry:
\[
 \norm{Z_t-Z_s}_{\HH}^2
  =\E\bigl||W_t|-|W_s|\bigr|
  \leq\E|W_t-W_s|\leq|t-s|^{1/2}.
\]
Thus $t\mapsto Z_t$ is norm continuous. Raw measurability gives
$P_tZ_t=Z_t$. The frozen construction also proves $P_0Z_t=0$.
One direct proof of that particular initial-germ assertion is as
follows. Let $\mathcal K_\epsilon$ be generated by the whole clock
path and by the scenery up to the clock range
$R_\epsilon=\sup_{r\leq\epsilon}|W_r|$. Then
$\mathcal F_\epsilon^Z\subset\mathcal K_\epsilon$ and, by independence
and the deterministic Brownian prediction formula conditional on the
clock,
\[
 \E[Z_t\mid\mathcal K_\epsilon]
   =B_{\min(|W_t|,R_\epsilon)},\qquad
 \norm{\E[Z_t\mid\mathcal K_\epsilon]}^2
   =\E\min(|W_t|,R_\epsilon)\leq\E R_\epsilon.
\]
Brownian maximal bounds and scaling give
$\E R_\epsilon\to0$. The initial right-germ subspace lies inside
every $L^2(\mathcal F_\epsilon^Z)$, so its projection of $Z_t$ has
norm at most $(\E R_\epsilon)^{1/2}$ for every $\epsilon>0$ and
is zero. This does not claim that all initial-germ observables are
constant. Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform} with $r=t$ now proves the first
assertion. Finally,
$\norm{\varphi(Z_t)-\varphi(Z_s)}\leq
\norm{\varphi'}_\infty\norm{Z_t-Z_s}$, and the transformed
observable belongs to the raw past at $t$. Apply the same theorem;
its initial projection remains in the formula.
\end{proof}

\section{Recovery in the original intrinsic divergence graph}
The reconstruction so far occurs in predictable-energy and observable
$L^2$ spaces. To state the native graph limit we must specify a
different, closed source operator.

Let $H$ be the coefficient Hilbert space of either independent Gaussian
source. On the joint product, let $D_B$ and $D_W$ be the closed
Gaussian derivatives in the scenery and clock factors, with their
maximal closed graph domains in $\HH$. Let
\[
 \mathcal V=L^2(\Omega;H)\oplus L^2(\Omega;H),\qquad
 D_{\OO}f=(D_Bf,D_Wf),\quad
 \Dom D_{\OO}=\OO\cap\Dom D_B\cap\Dom D_W.
\]
The field norm is the square sum of the two source norms. The frozen
finite-average core establishes density of this domain in $\OO$;
closedness follows from closedness of the two ambient source graphs.
Define the original intrinsic divergence by the maximal adjoint
$\delta_{\OO}=D_{\OO}^*$: a field $V\in\mathcal V$ belongs to its
domain exactly when there is $g\in\OO$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adjoint}
 \inner V{D_{\OO}f}_{\mathcal V}=\inner g f_{\HH}
 \quad(f\in\Dom D_{\OO});
 \qquad \delta_{\OO}V=g.
\end{equation}
Density makes $g$ unique. This is a static two-source adjoint domain;
it is not a postulated derivative in physical time.

We recall the Gaussian recovery construction in enough detail to check
its domain. On one Gaussian factor, the Hermite chaos decomposition
$f=\sum_{q\geq0}f_q$ defines
$Rf=D\sum_{q\geq1}f_q/q$. Finite Hermite polynomials give
\[
 \norm{Rf}^2=\sum_{q\geq1}\norm{f_q}^2/q\leq\norm f^2,
 \qquad \delta Rf=f-\E f.
\]
The orthogonality of differentiated chaoses proves convergence of the
fields; convergence of their divergences and closedness of $D^*$
prove membership in the original maximal adjoint. Tensor simple
functions and closure give the same identities fiberwise on the joint
product.

Write $C_Wf=\E[f\mid B]$ for averaging out the clock factor and
$C_Bf=\E[f\mid W]$ for averaging out the scenery factor, and use
$R_B,R_W$ for the two fiberwise recovery maps. Independence implies
$C_BC_Wf=\E f$. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recovery}
 \mathfrak R f=(R_B(C_Wf),R_Wf).
\end{equation}
The ordering is important: its two ambient divergences sum to
\[
 (C_Wf-C_BC_Wf)+(f-C_Wf)=f-\E f.
\]
Consequently, for centered $f\in\OO$, adjointness against
$\Dom D_{\OO}$ proves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:native}
 \mathfrak R f\in\Dom\delta_{\OO},\quad
 \delta_{\OO}\mathfrak R f=f,\quad
 \norm{\mathfrak R f}_{\mathcal V}^2\leq2\norm f^2.
\end{equation}
The bound follows separately from the contraction of each fiber
recovery and of $C_W$. No equality between the full intrinsic adjoint
domain and the separate ambient adjoint domains is assumed.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform native graph reconstruction]\label{thm:native}
Set $Tz=\mathfrak R(Jz)$ on $\mathcal C_{\mathbf e}$. Then
$Tz\in\Dom\delta_{\OO}$ and $\delta_{\OO}Tz=Jz$.
For the graph metric
\[
 d_{\rm gr}(V,W)=\norm{V-W}_{\mathcal V}+
                     \norm{\delta_{\OO}V-\delta_{\OO}W}_{\HH},
\]
one has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:graphbound}
 d_{\rm gr}(Tz,Tw)\leq3\norm{z-w}.
\end{equation}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:uniform},
\[
 \sup_{x\in K}\sup_{r\geq0}
 d_{\rm gr}\bigl(TS_rQ_A(u(x)),TS_rL(u(x))\bigr)
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The output $Jz$ belongs to $\OO_0$ and is therefore centered, since
constant functions belong to the initial germ. Apply
\eqref{eq:native}. Linearity, the bound there and the isometry of
$J$ give
\[
 \norm{Tz-Tw}\leq\sqrt2\norm{z-w},\qquad
 \norm{\delta_{\OO}Tz-\delta_{\OO}Tw}=\norm{z-w}.
\]
Since $1+\sqrt2\leq3$, this proves~\eqref{eq:graphbound} with the
constant retained in the formal source. Combine it with the uniform
input error and contractivity of every $S_r$. Both field values and
their original divergences converge in the stated norms.
\end{proof}


\section{Fixed stochastic coefficients as native graph tests}
\label{sec:graphtests}
The fixed channels also identify every reconstruction coefficient by
an original adjoint-graph test. Let
\[
 \mathcal T=\{(V,\delta_{\OO}V):V\in\Dom\delta_{\OO}\}
       \subset\mathcal V\oplus\OO,
 \quad \norm{(V,g)}_{\mathcal T}^2=\norm V^2+\norm g^2.
\]
Closedness of the original adjoint makes this a Hilbert space. For
any $f\in\OO$ define the continuous functional
\[
 (\mathrm d f)(V,\delta_{\OO}V)=\inner f{\delta_{\OO}V}.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz gives $\norm{\mathrm d f}_{\mathcal T^*}\leq\norm f$.
If $f\in\Dom D_{\OO}$, original adjointness identifies it with
$V\mapsto\inner{D_{\OO}f}V$ on these graph tests. The functional
exists for every $f$; this is not an extension of the native derivative
domain.

Use the already constructed bounded linear recovery $\mathfrak R$
of \eqref{eq:recovery}. For the fixed basis $e_i$ in \eqref{eq:basis},
set $V_i=\mathfrak R e_i$ and $\tau_i=(V_i,e_i)\in\mathcal T$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:native} gives
\[
 \delta_{\OO}V_i=e_i,\qquad \norm{V_i}^2\leq2,
 \qquad \norm{\tau_i}_{\mathcal T}\leq\sqrt3.
\]
These are actual original-divergence domain elements, constructed
from the fixed predictable integral outputs, rather than arbitrary
formal test symbols.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:graphcoefficients}
For every $f\in\OO$,
\[
 c_i(f)=(\mathrm d f)(\tau_i),\qquad
 f-P_0f=\sum_{i\in I}I_{e_i}\bigl((\mathrm d f)(\tau_i)h_i\bigr),
 \qquad
 \sum_i |(\mathrm d f)(\tau_i)|^2=\norm{f-P_0f}^2.
\]
The stochastic series converges in the original observable $L^2$.
For $f\in\Dom D_{\OO}$ its coefficient can equivalently be written
$\inner{V_i}{D_{\OO}f}$. For arbitrary $f$ the graph-functional
formula is the one asserted.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By the definition and $\delta_{\OO}V_i=e_i$,
$(\mathrm d f)(\tau_i)=\inner f{e_i}=c_i(f)$.
Substitute this equality in the fixed-channel reconstruction and
Parseval identities \eqref{eq:parseval}. Each integral is linear
in its scalar coefficient and satisfies $I_{e_i}h_i=e_i$,
so these are exactly the same convergent Hilbert series, with the
same squared coefficient energy. For native-domain $f$, apply the
original adjoint relation \eqref{eq:adjoint} to $V_i$.
\end{proof}

There is also a useful quantitative human consequence of these same
constructions. For every $f\in\OO$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weaklower}
 \frac{\norm{f-P_0f}}{\sqrt3}
       \leq\norm{\mathrm d f}_{\mathcal T^*}\leq\norm f.
\end{equation}
To prove the lower bound, take finitely supported real coefficients
$\eta=(\eta_i)$ and $e_\eta=\sum_i\eta_ie_i$. Linearity and the
recovery estimate on the centered observable $e_\eta$ give
\[
 \tau_\eta:=\sum_i\eta_i\tau_i
       =(\mathfrak R e_\eta,e_\eta),\qquad
 \norm{\tau_\eta}_{\mathcal T}^2
       \leq3\norm{e_\eta}^2=3\sum_i\eta_i^2.
\]
Thus
$|\sum_i\eta_i c_i(f)|\leq\sqrt3\norm{\mathrm d f}\norm\eta_{\ell^2}$.
For a finite set $A$ choose $\eta_i=c_i(f)$ on $A$ and zero
elsewhere. Dividing by the finite coefficient norm when it is
nonzero gives
$(\sum_{i\in A}|c_i(f)|^2)^{1/2}\leq\sqrt3\norm{\mathrm d f}$;
the zero case is immediate. Taking increasing finite sets and
Parseval proves \eqref{eq:weaklower}. Individual bounds on
$\norm{V_i}$ alone would not justify this step; the bounded linear
recovery on their \emph{combinations} is what provides it.

For the actual point curve, the already proved $P_0Z_t=0$ and
Brownian conditioning give
$\norm{Z_t}^2=\E|W_t|=\sqrt{2t/\pi}$. Therefore
\[
 \frac1{\sqrt3}(2t/\pi)^{1/4}
 \leq\norm{\mathrm d Z_t}_{\mathcal T^*}
 \leq(2t/\pi)^{1/4}\qquad(t>0).
\]
At least one fixed coefficient of $Z_t$ is nonzero for every positive
$t$, since otherwise Parseval would make $Z_t=0$ in $L^2$.
These bounds are labelled human consequences, not newly named
formal theorems or sharp constants.

The graph tests are static source fields; the scalar coefficients
pair the whole target with their divergences. Their stochastic
integrands remain the already constructed target-indexed channels,
not a new physical-time conditional-gradient formula. In particular
\eqref{eq:weaklower} does not put the rough point target in
$\Dom D_{\OO}$, supply a common prescribed Brownian driver or prove
observed-filtration predictable representation. It quantifies the
existing generalized derivative using exact native tests.

\section{Scope, antecedents and formal correspondence}
The change-of-energy and associativity mechanisms belong to classical
martingale integration. M\'etivier--Pellaumail~\cite{MP} supplies the
general Dol\'eans-measure setting; Liao~\cite{Liao} gives a directly
comparable continuous-martingale product-domain theorem. The present
proof does not assume that the observed filtration is Brownian or that
its terminal martingales are continuous. Its purpose is to make the
verified raw predictable domains, signed-energy quotient, original
adjoint and actual Brownian-time application readable together.
No new associativity principle or priority claim is made.

The uniformity proved above is
$\sup_x\sup_r\norm{\mathrm{error}(x,r)}_{L^2}\to0$.
It does not place a random supremum inside expectation and does not
assert almost-sure uniform convergence or a c\`adl\`ag version of the
moving-terminal approximants. Their coefficients depend on the terminal
parameter, so the diagonal approximants are not automatically
martingales. The countable representation is built from many
target-indexed observable martingales. Neither a predictable
representation theorem for a prescribed driver nor a temporal
conditional-gradient Clark--Ocone formula follows. A native divergence
representation also does not put its output in $\Dom D_{\OO}$;
rough point observations can lie outside that derivative domain.
