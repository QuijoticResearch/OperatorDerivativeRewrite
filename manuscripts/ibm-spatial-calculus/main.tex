\begin{abstract}
Let $Z_t=W(|B_t|)$, where $W$ and $B$ are independent standard
Brownian motions. We prove convergence in probability of oriented
spatial Simpson sums for every global $C^3$ one-form. The limit
is its ordinary spatial primitive evaluated along $Z$. The proof
uses a compact fourth-order Peano remainder and a direct quartic
tightness estimate. Separately, an exact scenery-overlap bound and
Brownian clock localization prove the uniform-grid quartic limit
$3T$, quadratic-variation divergence and the need for compensation
between raw Taylor channels. Continuous spatial weights yield a combined
three-channel $C^4$ Taylor limit with correction $1/8$ times the
fourth-derivative time integral, without global derivative bounds.
A separate spatial-kernel concentration argument proves that a specific source-compensated grid has diverging $L^2$ norm even after projection onto the full actual IBM observable space.
Nevertheless, fixed quadratic and bounded $C^2$ terminal tests admit strong clock-source contraction limits, with explicit capped-occupation corrections. A faithful quotient Banach domain also supports clock-$L^1$-valued limits of the original source contractions.
We then prove a deterministic nonseparable $C^1$-time/$C^4$-space
chain formula, uniform control over prefixes of one common grid,
bounded random-horizon convergence for spatial one-forms, and an
explicit Bernstein realization converging in continuous-path
supremum norm. The time and space hypotheses, measurability, and
limiting topologies are stated separately. These prescribed
symmetric quadratures are not identified with a causal physical-time
divergence. The principal results have exact declarations in a
frozen Lean development; the exposition supplies full human proofs.
Novelty of this package is unresolved.
\end{abstract}

\section{The process and the quadrature prescription}
Work on a probability space carrying independent real standard
Brownian motions $B=(B_t)_{t\geq0}$ and $W=(W_s)_{s\geq0}$,
starting at zero, with continuous versions. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ibm}
 Z_t=W(|B_t|),\qquad t\geq0.
\end{equation}
Thus the clock is reflected and the scenery is indexed by
nonnegative spatial time. This convention is fixed throughout.
The product Wiener realization is equivalent for these statements
to the constructed independent Gaussian scenery--clock realization
used in the formal sources. The process is jointly measurable and
has continuous paths on a common full-measure set. All samplewise
arguments below are on that set.

For a real function $g$, define the oriented spatial Simpson
increment
\begin{equation}\label{eq:simpson}
 S_g(x,y)=\frac{y-x}{6}
 \left(g(x)+4g\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right)+g(y)\right).
\end{equation}
The sign of $y-x$ is retained. The midpoint is the spatial
midpoint of the two endpoints; it is not evaluation of $Z$ at
the midpoint of a physical time interval.

Fix $T>0$, use all uniform grids $t_i=iT/N$, $0\leq i\leq N$,
and abbreviate $Z_i=Z_{t_i}$. Define
\[
 P_{g,N}(k)=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}S_g(Z_i,Z_{i+1}),\qquad0\leq k\leq N.
\]
The finite sums are defined directly from the integrand and the
process values. The limiting primitive will be proved to agree
with them, rather than being substituted as their definition.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:spatial}
Let $g\in C^3(\R)$ and set
\[
 J_g(t)=\int_{Z_0}^{Z_t}g(x)\,dx,
\]
where the integral is oriented. Then $J_g$ has continuous paths,
is jointly measurable, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prefix}
 \max_{0\leq k\leq N}|P_{g,N}(k)-J_g(t_k)|\Pconv0.
\end{equation}
No global growth bound on $g$ or its derivatives is assumed.
\end{theorem}

The uniformity in \eqref{eq:prefix} is over prefixes of one
common grid. It is not an assertion that arbitrary partitions
or arbitrary adapted time-dependent integrands are covered.
The deterministic time--space formula and the continuous-path
realization will have their own definitions below.

Stochastic integration for iterated Brownian motion has classical
antecedents. Khoshnevisan and Lewis~\cite{kl} use a two-sided
scenery and a random partition induced by a spatial clock grid.
Our reflected-clock convention and deterministic physical-time
grids are different. Simpson-type stochastic sums have also been
studied for fractional Brownian motion~\cite{hn}; covariance or
scaling similarity does not identify those processes with
\eqref{eq:ibm}. The paper gives a verified, domain-explicit
exposition, not a claim of first construction of symmetric
integration for iterated Brownian motion.

\section{A direct quartic tightness estimate}
For an interval $[a,b]$ let $\Delta Z=Z_b-Z_a$. Conditional on
the complete clock path, the two scenery times are fixed and
$\Delta Z$ is a centered Gaussian variable of variance
\[
 L=\big||B_b|-|B_a|\big|.
\]
Independence of $W$ and $B$ is essential here. Reversal of the
order of the scenery times only changes the sign of a Brownian
increment and not its Gaussian fourth moment. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fourthmoment}
 \mathbb E[(\Delta Z)^4\mid B]=3L^2,
 \qquad\mathbb E[(\Delta Z)^4]\leq3(b-a),
\end{equation}
using $L\leq|B_b-B_a|$ and the Brownian increment variance.
These expectations can equally be computed by Fubini on the
independent scenery--clock product space, so no unspecified
conditional version is required.

Put
\[
 Q_{4,N}=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}|Z_{i+1}-Z_i|^4,\qquad
 M_N=\max_{i<N}|Z_{i+1}-Z_i|.
\]
Equation~\eqref{eq:fourthmoment} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tight}
 \mathbb E Q_{4,N}\leq3T,\qquad
 \sup_N\mathbb P\{Q_{4,N}>A\}\leq\frac{3T}{A}
 \quad(A>0).
\end{equation}
Thus the quartic sums are tight. No independence between different
$\Delta_iZ$ is needed when summing their expectations. Continuity
of each path on $[0,T]$ gives $M_N\to0$ almost surely.

The sharper assertion $Q_{4,N}\Pconv3T$ is proved separately in
Section~\ref{sec:variation}; the quadrature proofs need only tightness.
In particular it would be incorrect to infer that assertion from
\eqref{eq:tight} or the corresponding expectation calculation.
The present argument uses only the bound it has actually proved.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:product}
If $U_N\to0$ in probability and $V_N\geq0$ is tight, then
$U_NV_N\to0$ in probability.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $A>0$ and $\varepsilon>0$,
\[
 \mathbb P\{|U_NV_N|>\varepsilon\}
 \leq\mathbb P\{V_N>A\}
       +\mathbb P\{|U_N|>\varepsilon/A\}.
\]
First choose $A$ to make the first term uniformly small, then
let $N\to\infty$ in the second.
\end{proof}

This elementary transfer principle is why a local remainder
modulus is sufficient; neither an almost-sure variation bound
nor global bounds on the integrand are needed.

\section{Compact Simpson remainders}
We now establish the deterministic estimate which controls
every stochastic prefix simultaneously.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:peano}
Let $g\in C^3(\R)$ and $R>0$. There is a nonnegative modulus
$\omega_R(\delta)\to0$ as $\delta\downarrow0$ such that, for
$|x|,|y|\leq R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:peano}
 \left|S_g(x,y)-\int_x^y g(z)\,dz\right|
 \leq\frac13\omega_R(|y-x|)|y-x|^4.
\end{equation}
One may take $\omega_R$ to be the modulus of continuity of
$g'''$ on $[-R,R]$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $d=y-x$. The case $d=0$ is immediate. On the segment between
$x$ and $y$, Taylor's theorem in integral form gives
\[
 g(x+\theta d)=g(x)+g'(x)\theta d
 +\frac{g''(x)}2\theta^2d^2+\frac{g'''(x)}6\theta^3d^3+r(\theta)
\]
for $0\leq\theta\leq1$, with
$|r(\theta)|\leq\omega_R(|d|)|d|^3/6$.
Indeed, subtract the constant $g'''(x)$ inside the third-order
integral remainder and use its uniform continuity. This proof
works for either sign of $d$.

The Simpson functional on $[0,1]$, with weights $1/6,4/6,1/6$,
integrates polynomials of degree at most three exactly. Directly,
its values on $1,\theta,\theta^2,\theta^3$ are
$1,1/2,1/3,1/4$. Thus all displayed polynomial terms cancel
against the actual integral. The Simpson average of the remainder
and its integral each have absolute value at most
$\omega_R(|d|)|d|^3/6$. Multiplying their difference by $|d|$
proves \eqref{eq:peano}.
\end{proof}

The estimate is fourth order with a vanishing compact coefficient.
It requires $g'''$ continuous, not a fourth derivative of $g$.
Equivalently, when $g=f'$, it requires a $C^4$ primitive, not a
fifth derivative. This is the regularity boundary used in the
formal compact Peano result.

For a continuous path $z:[0,T]\to\R$ with range in $[-R,R]$,
sum \eqref{eq:peano} over any grid prefix. Since the exact
oriented integrals telescope through the ordinary primitive,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pathbound}
 \max_{k\leq N}\left|
 \sum_{i<k}S_g(z_i,z_{i+1})-\int_{z_0}^{z_k}g\right|
 \leq\frac13\omega_R(M_N(z))Q_{4,N}(z).
\end{equation}
There is one modulus and one full nonnegative quartic sum for
every prefix. No union bound with a factor growing in $N$ is
being applied.

\section{The spatial formula and measurable random horizons}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:spatial}]
The function $G(x)=\int_0^xg(z)\,dz$ is $C^4$ by the fundamental
theorem of calculus, so $J_g(t)=G(Z_t)-G(Z_0)$ is continuous in
$t$ and jointly measurable. Each finite Simpson sum is measurable
because it is a finite sum of continuous functions of measurable
coordinates.

The range of a continuous sample path is bounded, so for any
$\eta>0$ choose deterministic $R$ with
$\mathbb P\{\sup_{t\leq T}|Z_t|>R\}<\eta$.
On its complementary event \eqref{eq:pathbound} applies.
There $\omega_R(M_N)\to0$ almost surely. Quartic tightness
\eqref{eq:tight} and Lemma~\ref{lem:product} show that the
right side tends to zero in probability. The probability of the
excluded event is less than $\eta$, which is arbitrary. This
proves the uniform-prefix assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:random}
Let $\tau$ be any measurable random time with $0\leq\tau\leq T$.
Define
\[
 k_N=\left\lfloor\frac{N\tau}{T}\right\rfloor,\qquad
 \tau_N=\frac{T}{N}k_N.
\]
Then $P_{g,N}(k_N)\Pconv J_g(\tau)$ for $g\in C^3(\R)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The integer index is measurable, takes values in $\{0,\ldots,N\}$,
and obeys $0\leq\tau-\tau_N<T/N$. Evaluating the finite collection
of measurable prefix sums at that index is measurable. The error
to $J_g(\tau_N)$ is bounded by the prefix maximum in
\eqref{eq:prefix}. Pathwise continuity gives
$J_g(\tau_N)\to J_g(\tau)$ almost surely, proving the result.
\end{proof}

No stopping-time hypothesis is used. For example $\tau$ can equal
$T$ or $T/2$ according to the sign of $Z_T$. This conclusion
uses uniform approximation and continuity, not optional sampling
of a martingale.

\section{Deterministic time--space integrands}
Let $F:\R^2\to\R$ satisfy the following hypotheses: for every
fixed $t$, $F(t,\cdot)\in C^4(\R)$; for every fixed $x$,
$F(\cdot,x)\in C^1(\R)$; and the functions $\partial_tF$ and
$\partial_x^4F$ are jointly continuous. No mixed derivative or
global derivative bound is assumed. For the continuous-path
realization in Section~\ref{sec:bernstein}, we additionally state
joint continuity of $F$ explicitly, as in its formal declaration.

Define the time-frozen spatial sums
\begin{equation}\label{eq:timesums}
 P_{F,N}(k)=\sum_{i<k}
 S_{\partial_xF(t_i,\cdot)}(Z_i,Z_{i+1}),
\end{equation}
and the continuous target
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
 J_F(t)=F(t,Z_t)-F(0,Z_0)
       -\int_0^t\partial_tF(s,Z_s)\,ds.
\end{equation}
The target is continuous. In fact the stated hypotheses already
give joint continuity of $F$: near a fixed time $t_0$, write
$F(t,x)=F(t_0,x)+\int_{t_0}^t\partial_tF(s,x)\,ds$ and use
continuity and local boundedness of the joint time partial.
We keep the explicit hypothesis for the later source correspondence.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:time}
Under these time--space hypotheses,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:timeprefix}
 \max_{k\leq N}|P_{F,N}(k)-J_F(t_k)|\Pconv0.
\end{equation}
In particular the full sums converge in probability to
\eqref{eq:target} at $t=T$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
On a compact rectangle $[0,T]\times[-R,R]$, joint continuity
of $\partial_x^4F$ gives one horizontal modulus uniform in $t$.
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:peano} with $g=\partial_xF(t,\cdot)$.
The proof uses only differences of $\partial_x^4F$ at nearby
spatial points, so its modulus works for every frozen time.
Thus the maximum error in replacing \eqref{eq:timesums} by
\[
 V_{F,N}(k)=\sum_{i<k}
     \big(F(t_i,Z_{i+1})-F(t_i,Z_i)\big)
\]
tends to zero in probability, by exactly the same localization
and quartic-tightness argument as before.

It remains to identify $V_{F,N}$. The exact finite chain identity is
\begin{align}\label{eq:finitechain}
 F(t_k,Z_k)-F(0,Z_0)
 &=V_{F,N}(k)\notag\\
 &\quad+\sum_{i<k}
       \big(F(t_{i+1},Z_{i+1})-F(t_i,Z_{i+1})\big).
\end{align}
The time fundamental theorem of calculus identifies the second
sum as
\[
 \sum_{i<k}\int_{t_i}^{t_{i+1}}\partial_tF(s,Z_{i+1})\,ds.
\]
For each fixed continuous sample path, uniform continuity of
$\partial_tF$ on its compact time--space range shows that its
difference from $\int_0^{t_k}\partial_tF(s,Z_s)\,ds$ is bounded,
uniformly over $k$, by
\[
 T\,\nu_R\!\left(\sup_{|t-s|\leq T/N}|Z_t-Z_s|\right)
 \longrightarrow0.
\]
Here $\nu_R$ is a horizontal modulus of the time partial on
the compact rectangle. This is a pathwise limit. Combining it
with the spatial error and \eqref{eq:finitechain} proves
\eqref{eq:timeprefix}.
\end{proof}

The time correction is an ordinary finite Lebesgue integral on
each sample path. It is not an assumed stochastic correction
term. Joint measurability of $\partial_tF(s,Z_s)$ and integration
over a bounded interval make it a measurable random variable.
The same argument gives measurability of the finite sums, since
each frozen spatial slice has a continuous derivative.

\section{Weights and elementary checks on the prescription}
Taking $F(t,x)=h(t)G(x)$, where $h\in C^1(\R)$ and
$G'=g\in C^3(\R)$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted}
 \sum_{i<N}h(t_i)S_g(Z_i,Z_{i+1})\Pconv
 h(T)J_g(T)-\int_0^T h'(s)J_g(s)\,ds.
\end{equation}
The equality of this target with the one in
Theorem~\ref{thm:time} follows from cancellation of the constant
$G(Z_0)$ and $\int_0^T h'=h(T)-h(0)$.
Thus the separable time-weighted rule is contained in the full
nonseparable theorem. Random time coefficients are not included
by replacing $h$ with a random function without further work.

The spatial rule is exact for cubic one-forms. For example
\[
 S_{4z^3}(x,y)=y^4-x^4.
\]
It is not always an exact increment of a primitive: direct
polynomial expansion gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quintic}
 S_{5z^4}(x,y)-(y^5-x^5)=\frac{(y-x)^5}{24}.
\end{equation}
For a continuous path the sum of absolute fifth powers is at
most $M_NQ_{4,N}$, which tends to zero in probability here.
This example illustrates the finite prescription and its limiting
primitive without conflating them.

The sign in \eqref{eq:simpson} also matters. Replacing $y-x$
by $|y-x|$ would already destroy the oriented integral for a
constant one-form. Similarly, replacing the spatial midpoint
by $Z_{(t_i+t_{i+1})/2}$ changes the approximation scheme;
the deterministic Simpson polynomial cancellations no longer
apply to that expression.

\section{An explicit continuous-path realization}\label{sec:bernstein}
Under the time--space hypotheses, including explicit joint
continuity of $F$, define on $0\leq u\leq1$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bernstein}
 A_{F,N}(u)=\sum_{k=0}^N
 \binom Nk u^k(1-u)^{N-k}P_{F,N}(k).
\end{equation}
This is an actual random polynomial assembled from the prefixes
of the one common grid. Let $j_F(u)=J_F(Tu)$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:path}
As random elements of $C([0,1],\R)$ with supremum norm,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pathlimit}
 \norm{A_{F,N}-j_F}_\infty\Pconv0.
\end{equation}
The approximants and target are Borel measurable in this
continuous-path space.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The Bernstein basis is nonnegative and sums to one, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bernsteinbound}
 \norm{A_{F,N}-B_Nj_F}_\infty
 \leq\max_{k\leq N}|P_{F,N}(k)-j_F(k/N)|,
\end{equation}
where $B_N$ is the ordinary Bernstein approximation operator.
The right side tends to zero in probability by
Theorem~\ref{thm:time}.

For a fixed continuous $f$, Bernstein approximation gives
$\norm{B_Nf-f}_\infty\to0$. A direct
proof writes $(B_Nf)(u)=\mathbb E[f(K/N)]$ for a binomial
$K$ of parameters $N,u$. Split at $|K/N-u|\leq\delta$ and use
$\mathbb E(K/N-u)^2=u(1-u)/N\leq1/(4N)$ to obtain
\[
 \norm{B_Nf-f}_\infty
 \leq\omega_f(\delta)+\frac{2\norm f_\infty}{4N\delta^2}.
\]
First take $N\to\infty$ and then $\delta\downarrow0$.
Apply this pathwise to the continuous target $j_F$.
The resulting almost-sure Bernstein error tends to zero in
probability. Combine it with \eqref{eq:bernsteinbound}.

Each coefficient in \eqref{eq:bernstein} is measurable, hence
the finite polynomial is a measurable $C([0,1])$-valued map.
For the target, rational evaluation maps are measurable by
the measurability arguments following Theorem~\ref{thm:time}.
The Borel sigma algebra of the separable space $C([0,1])$ is
generated by these evaluations: suprema of continuous-path
distances equal suprema over a countable dense subset. Thus the
target path map is measurable as well.
\end{proof}

The approximation has $A_{F,N}(0)=0$ and
$A_{F,N}(1)=P_{F,N}(N)$, since the Bernstein basis selects the
first and last coefficients at the endpoints. At intermediate
times, all grid prefixes can enter \eqref{eq:bernstein}.
Consequently it is a polynomial smoothing, not a claimed causal
interpolation. No adaptedness or $L^p$ convergence follows merely
from \eqref{eq:pathlimit}.

\section{Exact uniform-grid quartic variation}\label{sec:variation}
The tightness estimate used above is deliberately weaker than the
library's full variation result. We now reconstruct that result
without changing the proof or domain of the Simpson formulas.
For $t_i=iT/N$, let
\[
 Q_N=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}(Z_{i+1}-Z_i)^4,
 \qquad C_N=3\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}(|B_{t_{i+1}}|-|B_{t_i}|)^2.
\]
The second quantity is the exact scenery-conditional mean of the
first, since each scenery increment has conditional variance
$\ell_i=\big||B_{t_{i+1}}|-|B_{t_i}|\big|$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:quartic}
For every $T>0$, along all positive integer uniform-grid sizes,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quarticlimit}
 Q_N\Pconv3T.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The scenery increments need not be independent after conditioning
on the clock. A later traversal may revisit a previous scenery
interval. We keep all such overlaps in the variance estimate.

\subsection*{The exact Gaussian overlap calculation}
Write $a_i=|B_{t_i}|$, $b_i=|B_{t_{i+1}}|$ and
$I_i=(\min(a_i,b_i),\max(a_i,b_i)]$. Let $o_{ij}$ be the signed
overlap of these oriented intervals; its absolute value is
$|I_i\cap I_j|$. Brownian scenery increments $G_i=W_{b_i}-W_{a_i}$
satisfy, for this fixed clock sample,
\[
 \mathbb E_WG_i^2=\ell_i,\qquad
 \mathbb E_W(G_iG_j)=o_{ij}.
\]
The second equality follows by writing each increment as the
oriented integral of an interval indicator against Brownian motion.
It also follows directly by expanding the covariance
$\mathbb E(W_uW_v)=\min(u,v)$.
For centered jointly Gaussian variables with variances $l,m$ and
covariance $o$, the pair-moment formula gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:wickquartic}
 \operatorname{Cov}(G^4,H^4)=72lm\,o^2+24o^4.
\end{equation}
To verify the constants, among pairings of four $G$'s and four
$H$'s, zero cross-pairs contribute $9l^2m^2$, two cross-pairs
contribute $\binom42^2 2!=72$ copies of $lm\,o^2$, and four
cross-pairs contribute $4!=24$ copies of $o^4$.
Subtract $\mathbb EG^4\mathbb EH^4=9l^2m^2$.
The pairing rule itself follows by expanding the Gaussian generating
function $\exp((lx^2+2oxy+my^2)/2)$ at $x^4y^4$.

If all $\ell_i\leq\delta$, then $|o_{ij}|\leq\delta$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditionalvariance}
 \mathbb E_W(Q_N-C_N)^2
 \leq96\delta^3\sum_{i,j}|o_{ij}|.
\end{equation}
Indeed, each summand in \eqref{eq:wickquartic} is bounded by its
coefficient times $\delta^3|o_{ij}|$. Every finite eighth moment
exists, so the double variance expansion is legitimate. Equivalently,
if $n(x)=\sum_i\mathbf1_{I_i}(x)$ is the return count, then
$\sum_{i,j}|o_{ij}|=\int n(x)^2dx$. Repeated visits are counted
quadratically, as required.

\subsection*{A Brownian endpoint envelope}
Define $\tau_\delta(x)=(2\delta-|x|)_+$ and
\[
 E_{N,\delta}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{N-1}
       \tau_\delta(|B_{t_i}|-|B_{t_j}|),\qquad
 p(s)=(2\pi s)^{-1/2}\quad(s>0).
\]
For two intervals starting at $a,c$ and of lengths at most
$\delta$, their overlap length is at most
$(2\delta-|a-c|)_+$. This follows by enclosing them in
$[a-\delta,a+\delta]$ and $[c-\delta,c+\delta]$ and intersecting
these two intervals. Consequently, on the event
\[
 \mathcal A_{N,\delta}
 =\{\max_i|B_{t_{i+1}}-B_{t_i}|<\delta\},
\]
the reverse triangle inequality gives $\ell_i\leq\delta$ and
$\sum_{i,j}|o_{ij}|\leq E_{N,\delta}$.

For $s<t$, condition on $B_s=x$. The later value is $x+G$, where
$G$ is independent normal with variance $t-s$. The folded variable
$|x+G|$ has density on $[0,\infty)$ bounded by $2p(t-s)$,
uniformly in $x$. The tent has height at most $2\delta$, support
length $4\delta$, and hence integral at most $8\delta^2$.
Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tentmean}
 \mathbb E\tau_\delta(|B_s|-|B_t|)
 \leq16\delta^2p(t-s).
\end{equation}
The diagonal is instead exactly $2\delta$; it is not evaluated
through a singular zero-variance density formula. Set $h=T/N$.
Since $p(kh)=p(h)/\sqrt k$ and
\[
 \sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac1{\sqrt k}\leq2\sqrt N,
\]
summing the ordered off-diagonal pairs gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endpointenergy}
 \mathbb EE_{N,\delta}
 \leq2N\delta+64\delta^2p(h)N\sqrt N
 =2N\delta+64p(T)N^2\delta^2.
\end{equation}
The inverse-square-root sum follows by telescoping
$1/\sqrt k\leq2(\sqrt k-\sqrt{k-1})$.

Let $m_{20}=\mathbb E|G_0|^{20}$ for a standard normal $G_0$;
this is a finite fixed constant. The union bound and Markov's
inequality give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:badclock}
 \mathbb P(\mathcal A_{N,\delta}^c)
 \leq N(T/N)^{10}m_{20}\delta^{-20}.
\end{equation}
Choose $\delta=\delta_N=N^{-4/9}$. The right side is
$T^{10}m_{20}N^{-1/9}\to0$, whereas
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weightedenergy}
 \delta_N^3\mathbb EE_{N,\delta_N}
 \leq2N^{-7/9}+64p(T)N^{-2/9}\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
For any $\varepsilon>0$, conditional Chebyshev on the good-clock
event, followed by Fubini, now yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quarticfluctuation}
 \mathbb P(|Q_N-C_N|\geq\varepsilon)
 \leq\mathbb P(\mathcal A_{N,\delta_N}^c)
     +\frac{96}{\varepsilon^2}\delta_N^3\mathbb EE_{N,\delta_N}
 \longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
On the bad event the conditional probability is bounded by one.
No global joint $L^2$ error bound is inferred by removing that event.

\subsection*{The reflected-clock correction}
For real $a,b$ define $r(a,b)=2(|ab|-ab)\geq0$. Direct expansion gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reflectionidentity}
 (|b|-|a|)^2=(b-a)^2-r(a,b).
\end{equation}
Let $R_N=\sum_i r(B_{t_i},B_{t_{i+1}})$.
If $|b-a|\leq\delta$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:penaltytent}
 r(a,b)\leq\delta\tau_\delta(a).
\end{equation}
When $ab\geq0$ the left side is zero. Otherwise the endpoints
straddle zero, so $|a|\leq\delta$ and
$r(a,b)=4|ab|\leq(b-a)^2\leq\delta^2$;
also $\tau_\delta(a)\geq\delta$. This proves the bound in both cases.
Therefore, on $\mathcal A_{N,\delta}$,
\[
 0\leq R_N\leq\delta\sum_i\tau_\delta(B_{t_i}).
\]
The initial term is bounded by $2\delta^2$. At positive grid times,
the ordinary Gaussian density and the same tent-mass bound give
$\mathbb E\tau_\delta(B_{t_i})\leq16\delta^2p(t_i)$.
Summing as before, with a harmless upper bound including one extra
positive time, gives
\[
 \mathbb E\left[\delta\sum_i\tau_\delta(B_{t_i})\right]
 \leq2\delta^2+32p(T)N\delta^3.
\]
For $\delta_N=N^{-4/9}$ this tends to zero. Markov's inequality,
with the bad-clock probability kept separate, implies $R_N\Pconv0$.

Finally $U_N=\sum_i(B_{t_{i+1}}-B_{t_i})^2$ has expectation $T$
and variance $2T^2/N$, by independent Brownian increments. Thus
$U_N\to T$ in $L^2$. Summing \eqref{eq:reflectionidentity} gives
$C_N=3U_N-3R_N\Pconv3T$. Combining this with
\eqref{eq:quarticfluctuation} proves Theorem~\ref{thm:quartic}.

\subsection*{Uniform prefixes on the same physical grid}
The preceding estimates also control prefixes of the original grid,
without remeshing a shorter time interval. Define
\[
 Q_N(m)=\sum_{i<m}(Z_{i+1}-Z_i)^4,\qquad
 M_N=\max_{0\leq m\leq N}|Q_N(m)-3Tm/N|.
\]
We claim $M_N\Pconv0$. First fix any deterministic sequence
$0\leq m_N\leq N$. Removing intervals only decreases
$\sum_{i,j}|o_{ij}|$, so the same good-clock conditional-variance
bound and bad-clock estimate prove that the scenery-centered
prefix error tends to zero in probability. The Brownian-clock
prefix quadratic sum has mean $m_NT/N$ and variance
$2m_N(T/N)^2\leq2T^2/N$. Its nonnegative reflection penalty is
at most the full penalty $R_N$, already proved to vanish in
probability. Therefore
\[
 Q_N(m_N)-3Tm_N/N\Pconv0.
\]
All selections in this argument are deterministic.

To obtain the maximum, fix $K\geq1$ and put
$L=\lfloor N/K\rfloor+1$, $r_j=\min(jL,N)$ for $0\leq j\leq K$.
These nodes cover every integer prefix, because $KL>N$.
The increasing function $m\mapsto Q_N(m)$ brackets each prefix
between its neighboring coarse values. Subtracting the linear
target gives the deterministic bound
\[
 M_N\leq\max_{0\leq j\leq K}|Q_N(r_j)-3Tr_j/N|
               +3T/K+3T/N.
\]
For fixed $K$, the finite maximum tends to zero in probability by
the prefix result and a union bound. Choose $K$ large first and
then let $N\to\infty$. This proves the claim. In particular this
is a maximum of partial errors, rather than an unjustified union
bound over an increasing number of prefixes.

\subsection*{Every continuous spatial weight}
For every continuous $\varphi:\R\to\R$, including globally
unbounded ones, we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weightedquartic}
 \sum_{i<N}\varphi(Z_i)(Z_{i+1}-Z_i)^4
       \Pconv3\int_0^T\varphi(Z_t)\,dt.
\end{equation}
Here the integral is a genuine finite pathwise time integral.
No moment or global bound on $\varphi$ is required.

We give the deterministic quadrature argument before applying it
to random weights. Suppose nonnegative masses $q_{N,i}$ on the
uniform grid obey
\[
 \max_{m\leq N}\left|\sum_{i<m}q_{N,i}-3Tm/N\right|
       =\eta_N\longrightarrow0.
\]
For a Lipschitz function $p$ on $[0,T]$ with
$|p|\leq B$ and Lipschitz constant $A$, Abel summation bounds
\[
 \left|\sum_{i<N}p(t_i)(q_{N,i}-3T/N)\right|
 \leq\eta_N\left(|p(t_{N-1})|
             +\sum_{i<N-1}|p(t_{i+1})-p(t_i)|\right)
 \leq\eta_N(B+AT).
\]
The left Riemann-sum error for $3\int_0^Tp$ is at most
$3AT^2/N$, by integrating $A|t-t_i|\leq AT/N$ on each cell.
For continuous $g$, choose a piecewise linear $p$ with
$\|g-p\|_\infty\leq\varepsilon$. Such a choice follows by linear
interpolation and uniform continuity on the compact interval.
The additional error is bounded by
\[
 \varepsilon\left(\sum_{i<N}q_{N,i}+3T\right).
\]
The mass sum tends to $3T$. First pass to the limit in $N$ with
the approximation fixed, then let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$.
Thus the quadrature converges for every continuous $g$.
The formal proof uses polynomial approximation in place of linear
interpolation; the deterministic estimate and positive-mass
mechanism are the same.

Now take any subsequence of grid sizes. Since $M_N\to0$ in
probability, it has a further subsequence on which $M_N\to0$
almost surely. This standard extraction follows by choosing
successive indices with probabilities
$\mathbb P(M_N>2^{-j})<2^{-j}$ and applying the summable-event
lemma. On each continuous sample path in this full-measure set,
apply the deterministic argument with
$q_{N,i}=(Z_{i+1}-Z_i)^4$ and $g(t)=\varphi(Z_t)$.
The resulting convergence is almost sure along that further
subsequence. The subsequence criterion proves
\eqref{eq:weightedquartic}: if probability convergence failed,
some subsequence would have a fixed positive tail probability,
which its almost-sure further subsequence contradicts. Finite
sums are measurable by continuity of $\varphi$ and the coordinate
maps; the time integral is measurable by joint measurability and
integration over $[0,T]$. No measurability of a selected samplewise
approximation or compact-path bound is needed.

\subsection*{The combined first three Taylor channels on the full $C^4$ domain}
For $f\in C^4(\R)$ define the actual finite sum
\[
 A_N(f)=\sum_{i<N}\left[f'(Z_i)\Delta Z_i
            +\tfrac12 f''(Z_i)(\Delta Z_i)^2
            +\tfrac16 f'''(Z_i)(\Delta Z_i)^3\right],
 \qquad\Delta Z_i=Z_{i+1}-Z_i.
\]
Then at each fixed $T>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:c4chain}
 A_N(f)\Pconv f(Z_T)-f(Z_0)-\tfrac18\int_0^T f^{(4)}(Z_t)\,dt.
\end{equation}
All derivatives may be globally unbounded. For a fixed continuous
sample path, choose $R$ containing its range. Taylor's theorem
and uniform continuity of $f^{(4)}$ on $[-R,R]$ give
\[
 f(y)-f(x)=f'(x)(y-x)+\tfrac12f''(x)(y-x)^2
  +\tfrac16f'''(x)(y-x)^3+\tfrac1{24}f^{(4)}(x)(y-x)^4+r(x,y),
\]
where $|r(x,y)|\leq\omega_R(|y-x|)|y-x|^4/24$ and
$\omega_R(a)\to0$ as $a\downarrow0$.
For example, the third-order Lagrange remainder is
$f^{(4)}(z)(y-x)^4/24$ for a point $z$ between $x$ and $y$;
subtracting the fourth coefficient proves this bound, also when
$x=y$. Since the path mesh $m_N\to0$ almost surely,
\[
 \sum_{i<N}|r(Z_i,Z_{i+1})|
       \leq\omega_R(m_N)Q_N/24\Pconv0.
\]
To justify the last passage without a random integrable dominator,
extract from any subsequence a further one on which $Q_N\to3T$
almost surely and use the pathwise bound. The same subsequence
criterion proves the stated probability convergence.

Finally telescope the exact Taylor identities. The sum tends by \eqref{eq:weightedquartic} to
$3\int_0^Tf^{(4)}(Z_t)dt$; the total signed remainder is bounded
by the vanishing total absolute remainder. Thus $3/24=1/8$
gives \eqref{eq:c4chain}. The three lower ranks remain grouped
in their genuine original grid sum. This is not an assertion of
their separate convergence, an $L^p$ limit, a simultaneous-in-time
version, or a classical observed-filtration stochastic integral.
It supplies the precise compensated change-of-variables statement
consistent with the raw-channel obstruction below.

\section{Divergent raw channels and finite compensated sums}
Let
\[
 V_N=\sum_i(Z_{i+1}-Z_i)^2,\qquad
 m_N=\max_i|Z_{i+1}-Z_i|.
\]
Path continuity on $[0,T]$ implies $m_N\to0$ almost surely, and
the elementary inequality
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quarticquadratic}
 Q_N\leq m_N^2V_N
\end{equation}
keeps the quartic and quadratic sums distinct.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:quadratic}
For $T>0$ and every fixed real $K$,
\[
 \mathbb P(V_N\leq K)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Neither $V_N$ nor the raw first Taylor channel
$F_N=\sum_i2Z_i(Z_{i+1}-Z_i)$ has a finite probability limit.
Nevertheless their sum is exactly $Z_T^2-Z_0^2$ for every grid.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For $K<0$ the event is empty. For $K\geq0$, on
$\{V_N\leq K\}$, \eqref{eq:quarticquadratic} gives
$Q_N\leq Km_N^2$. Since $Q_N\Pconv3T>0$ and
$Km_N^2\Pconv0$, the event has probability tending to zero.
If $V_N\Pconv Y$ for a finite random variable, choose $K$
with $\mathbb P(|Y|>K)<1/4$. For all sufficiently large $N$,
$\mathbb P(|V_N-Y|>1)<1/4$ would give
$\mathbb P(V_N\leq K+1)>1/2$, contradicting the lower-tail result.

Expanding $(Z_{i+1})^2-Z_i^2$ and telescoping gives
\[
 F_N+V_N=Z_T^2-Z_0^2.
\]
A finite probability limit for $F_N$ would therefore give one for
$V_N$ by subtraction from this fixed finite endpoint variable.
This is impossible. The compensation is essential even for the
polynomial $z^2$; it cannot be replaced by separately convergent
raw first and second channels.
\end{proof}

Burdzy's classical \emph{Variation of iterated Brownian motion}
\cite{burdzy} already establishes deterministic quartic variation
for two-sided-scenery IBM. Khoshnevisan--Lewis \cite{kl}, Theorem 4.3,
proves a stronger weighted quartic limit on its induced random
partition. These are explicit antecedents. Our source-mapped proof
retains the reflected-clock model, all uniform physical grids and
convergence in probability; it claims no priority for the quartic
phenomenon and no almost-sure or $L^2$ upgrade. The continuously weighted
uniform-grid limit above is proved in probability.


\section{Compensated grids still escape after observable projection}
The raw-channel divergence does not by itself exclude a compensated
$L^2$ limit. We now prove that a specific original compensated grid
also has diverging norm, even after conditioning on the entire IBM
observable sigma algebra. This assertion concerns a definite sequence;
it does not exclude other stochastic integral domains or prescriptions.

Write $r_i=|B_{t_i}|$, $h_i=\mathbf1_{(0,r_i]}\in L^2(\R_+)$,
$d_i=h_{i+1}-h_i$, and use the scenery isonormal notation
$X(h)=\int h\,dW$, so $Z_i=X(h_i)$. All these formulas can be
read conditionally on $B$, where $h_i,d_i$ are deterministic.
Define the actual finite random variables
\[
 R_N=\sum_{i<N}Z_i(Z_{i+1}-Z_i),\qquad
 C_N=\sum_{i<N}\langle d_i,h_i\rangle
 =\sum_{i<N}(\min(r_i,r_{i+1})-r_i),\qquad
 S_N=R_N-C_N.
\]
The conditional scenery mean of $S_N$ is zero. Gaussian moments of
the finitely many clock coordinates and conditional fourth moments
show that $R_N,C_N,S_N\in L^2$ for every $N$; for example
$\|d_i\|^2=|r_{i+1}-r_i|$ and $\|h_i\|^2=r_i$, and
the conditional second moment of each product is bounded by
$3\|d_i\|^2\|h_i\|^2$. Let
\[
 \mathcal O=\sigma(Z_t:t\geq0),\qquad
 I_N=\mathbb E[S_N\mid\mathcal O].
\]
This uses the full observable space, with no changing-past or
predictable identification.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:nativeescape}
For every fixed $T>0$ along all uniform physical grids,
\[
 a_N:=\mathbb E[S_N^2]\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(C_N)\leq\frac92T,
 \qquad \|I_N\|_2\longrightarrow\infty.
\]
More precisely,
\[
 a_N^2\leq\|I_N\|_2^2
       \bigl(a_N+\operatorname{Var}(C_N)\bigr).
\]
Thus this projected compensated sequence has no finite $L^2$ limit.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Folded clock mass and a concentrating spatial kernel}
Put $h=T/N$ and $U_N=\sum_{i<N}(B_{t_{i+1}}-B_{t_i})^2$.
Independent increments give $\mathbb E U_N=T$ and
$\operatorname{Var}(U_N)=2T^2/N$. We need the folded version
\[
 q_N=\sum_{i<N}(r_{i+1}-r_i)^2\longrightarrow T
 \quad\hbox{in }L^1.
\]
Here is a direct proof of that additional assertion. The loss
$(y-x)^2-(|y|-|x|)^2$ is nonnegative and at most $(y-x)^2$.
It vanishes when $x,y$ have the same sign. For any $\delta>0$,
a crossing with $|y-x|\leq\delta$ has $|x|\leq\delta$.
Consequently, writing $\Delta_iB=B_{t_{i+1}}-B_{t_i}$,
\[
 0\leq U_N-q_N\leq\sum_{i<N}(\Delta_iB)^2
 \left(\mathbf1_{\{|B_{t_i}|\leq\delta\}}+
       \mathbf1_{\{|\Delta_iB|>\delta\}}\right).
\]
The first summand at $i=0$ has expectation $h$. For $i\geq1$,
independence of $B_{t_i}$ and $\Delta_iB$ and the normal density bound
give a contribution at most
$h\sqrt{2/\pi}\,\delta/\sqrt{ih}$.
Since $\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}i^{-1/2}\leq2\sqrt N$, their sum is at
most $2\sqrt{2/\pi}\,\delta\sqrt T$.
The large-increment contribution is at most
$\sum_i\mathbb E(\Delta_iB)^4/\delta^2=3Th/\delta^2$.
For fixed $\delta$, let $N\to\infty$, and then $\delta\downarrow0$.
This proves $\mathbb E(U_N-q_N)\to0$ and hence the claimed $L^1$
limit. No pathwise quadratic-variation assertion has been substituted.

For a deterministic finite nonnegative radius sequence starting at
zero, define the spatial interval
\[ J_i=(\min(r_i,r_{i+1}),\max(r_i,r_{i+1})]. \]
Form the nonnegative spatial kernel
\[
 K_N(x,y)=\sum_{i<N}\mathbf1_{J_i}(x)\mathbf1_{J_i}(y).
\]
Its mass and energy are exactly
\[
 \int K_N=q_N,\qquad
 G_N:=\int K_N^2=
 \sum_{i,j<N}|J_i\cap J_j|^2
 =\sum_{i,j<N}\langle d_i,d_j\rangle^2.
\]
The last equality uses the signs of oriented indicator increments;
their signs disappear upon squaring. If $0\leq r_i\leq M$ and
$|r_{i+1}-r_i|\leq\eta$, the kernel is supported inside
$\{0\leq x\leq M,\ |y-x|\leq\eta\}$, of area $2M\eta$.
Ordinary integral Cauchy--Schwarz therefore gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nativeband}
 q_N^2\leq2M\eta G_N.
\end{equation}
All integrals here are Lebesgue integrals on $\R^2$, and all kernels
are finite sums of bounded finite-area indicators.

For fixed clock coordinates let $e_N(B)=\mathbb E_W S_N^2$.
Write $\mathcal W(u,v)=X(u)X(v)-\langle u,v\rangle$.
The Gaussian pairing identity gives
\[
 \mathbb E_W[\mathcal W(u,v)\mathcal W(a,b)]
 =\langle u,a\rangle\langle v,b\rangle
  +\langle u,b\rangle\langle v,a\rangle.
\]
This follows by the three pairings of four centered Gaussian
coordinates and cancellation of the product of means.
Since $S_N=\sum_i\mathcal W(d_i,h_i)$, telescoping the identity
\[
 2\mathcal W(d_i,h_i)
 =\mathcal W(h_{i+1},h_{i+1})-\mathcal W(h_i,h_i)
   -\mathcal W(d_i,d_i)
\]
and applying the pairing formula gives
\[
 e_N(B)=\frac12\left(r_N^2
      -2\sum_i\langle h_N,d_i\rangle^2+G_N\right).
\]
Indeed the endpoint square has energy $2r_N^2$, the sum of
increment squares has energy $2G_N$, and their cross term is
$2\sum_i\langle h_N,d_i\rangle^2$.
The map $r\mapsto\min(r_N,r)$ is 1-Lipschitz, so
$\sum_i\langle h_N,d_i\rangle^2\leq q_N\leq U_N$ for the
actual clock. Hence $G_N\leq2e_N(B)+2U_N$.
Combining with \eqref{eq:nativeband} yields the pathwise bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nativeconcentration}
 q_N^2\leq4M\eta\bigl(e_N(B)+U_N\bigr).
\end{equation}

We now prove $a_N=\mathbb E_B e_N(B)\to\infty$ without a
four-time lower bound. Suppose there is a subsequence with $a_N\leq A$.
The $L^1$ convergence of $q_N$ supplies a further subsequence with
$q_N\to T$ almost surely: select summable $L^1$ errors and apply
Tonelli. On the common continuous clock event, choose
$M=1+\sup_{t\leq T}|B_t|<\infty$ and
$\eta_N=N^{-1}+\max_i|r_{i+1}-r_i|\to0$.
Bound \eqref{eq:nativeconcentration} then forces
$e_N(B)+U_N\to\infty$ on this further subsequence.
Fatou applied to these nonnegative variables would give
\[
 \infty=\mathbb E\liminf(e_N(B)+U_N)
 \leq\liminf\mathbb E(e_N(B)+U_N)\leq A+T,
\]
a contradiction. Thus no bounded-energy subsequence exists,
which proves divergence to infinity along the entire sequence.

\subsection{A bounded centered clock correction}
The identity $2(\min(x,y)-x)=y-x-|y-x|$ gives
\[
 C_N=\frac12\left(r_N-\sum_i|r_{i+1}-r_i|\right).
\]
This uncentered quantity need not be bounded. Let instead
\[
 A_N=\sum_i\bigl(|\Delta_iB|-\mathbb E|\Delta_iB|\bigr),\quad
 L_N=\sum_i\bigl(|\Delta_iB|-|r_{i+1}-r_i|\bigr),\quad
 H_N=\sum_i\operatorname{sgn}(B_{t_i})\Delta_iB,
\]
where $\operatorname{sgn}(0)=0$, and put
$d_N=\sum_i\mathbb E|\Delta_iB|$.
Independent increments give $\mathbb E A_N^2\leq T$.
The sign coefficients are measurable at the left endpoints and bounded
by one; conditioning each cross term on its later left endpoint
gives zero and hence $\mathbb E H_N^2\leq T$.

The elementary discrete Tanaka inequalities are
\[
 0\leq |y-x|-\bigl||y|-|x|\bigr|
 \leq |y|-|x|-\operatorname{sgn}(x)(y-x).
\]
For equal signs the left difference vanishes and the upper expression
is zero; for opposite signs both inequalities follow by inserting
$x>0>y$ or $y>0>x$; at $x=0$ the left side is zero and the right
side is $|y|$. Summing yields $0\leq L_N\leq r_N-H_N$.
Therefore
$\mathbb E L_N^2\leq2\mathbb E r_N^2+2\mathbb E H_N^2\leq4T$.
The exact identity
\[
 2C_N+d_N=r_N-A_N+L_N
\]
and $(u+v+w)^2\leq3(u^2+v^2+w^2)$ give
$\mathbb E(2C_N+d_N)^2\leq18T$.
Since deterministic shifts leave variance unchanged,
$4\operatorname{Var}(C_N)\leq18T$, as claimed.
This argument works for any deterministic increasing partition from
zero, although only uniform grids are used in the escape theorem.

\subsection{A detecting observable survives projection}
The variable $R_N$ is $\mathcal O$-measurable. Conditional scenery
centering gives $\mathbb E S_N=0$ and
$\mathbb E[S_N(C_N-\mathbb E C_N)]=0$, with all products integrable
by Cauchy--Schwarz. Consequently
\[
 R_N-\mathbb E C_N=S_N+(C_N-\mathbb E C_N),\qquad
 \|R_N-\mathbb E C_N\|_2^2=a_N+\operatorname{Var}(C_N).
\]
By the defining orthogonal projection identity for conditional
expectation,
\[
 \langle I_N,R_N-\mathbb E C_N\rangle
 =\langle S_N,R_N-\mathbb E C_N\rangle=a_N.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz proves the quantitative inequality in
Theorem~\ref{thm:nativeescape}. Since $a_N\to\infty$ and the clock
variance is bounded, it forces $\|I_N\|_2\to\infty$.
This proves the theorem. In particular, contraction of the projection
does not remove the divergence, and no commutation of that projection
with a scenery-chaos projection has been assumed.

In the original source construction the finite field has scenery
component $\sum_i X(h_i)d_i$ and zero clock component.
Its ambient scenery divergence is $S_N$: Gaussian integration by
parts gives $\delta_W(X(h_i)d_i)=\mathcal W(d_i,h_i)$ for fixed
clock, and the finite $L^2$ bounds above allow fiberwise integration.
Restricting the two-source derivative to its original IBM observable
domain makes its adjoint value $I_N$. Explicitly, every test $F$ in
that original domain satisfies
\[
 \left\langle\sum_iX(h_i)d_i,D_WF\right\rangle_{L^2}
 =\langle S_N,F\rangle=\langle I_N,F\rangle.
\]
This is the precise finite-graph identification recorded by the
verified native-field declarations. The escape proof above was given
directly for actual conditional expectations, independently of any
enlargement of that test domain. It supplies neither a completed
physical-time integral nor a theorem against all possible
renormalizations or higher-channel constructions.


\subsection{Failure of weak reconstruction on the full observable space}
The norm escape has a stronger consequence than absence of a strong
limit. Let $E=L^2(\mathcal O)$, the real closed observable subspace
of the actual scenery--clock probability space. For the same original
projected native outputs $I_N\in E$ there exists a \emph{single}
$v\in E$ such that
\[
 \sup_{N\ge1}|\langle I_N,v\rangle|=\infty.
\]
Thus those pairings converge to no finite scalar. Moreover no
subsequence $I_{N_j}$ with strictly increasing indices converges
weakly to any $w\in E$.

Here is the complete functional-analytic step. If every $v\in E$
had bounded pairings, the closed sets
\[
 A_k=\{v\in E:|\langle I_N,v\rangle|\le k
                         \text{ for all }N\ge1\},\qquad k=1,2,\ldots,
\]
would cover $E$. It is a Hilbert space, hence complete. The Baire
category theorem would give a ball $B(v_0,r)\subset A_k$ for some
$k$ and $r>0$. For every $\|h\|\le1$, the two points
$v_0\pm(r/2)h$ belong to that ball. Subtract their pairings and use
the two bounds by $k$ to obtain
\[
 r|\langle I_N,h\rangle|\le2k\quad\text{for every }N.
\]
Taking the supremum over the unit ball gives
$\|I_N\|\le2k/r$, contrary to Theorem~\ref{thm:nativeescape}.
Negating the assumed pointwise boundedness produces the stated fixed
observable. This is the classical Banach--Steinhaus mechanism
\cite{BS}; its use introduces no new probabilistic assumption.

For every prescribed strictly increasing sequence $N_j$, the same
norm escape holds along $j$. Applying the argument to that subsequence
gives a detecting observable $v_{(N_j)}$ with unbounded pairings.
Weak convergence to any $w\in E$ would make every such pairing
convergent, hence bounded, a contradiction. The detecting observable
may depend on the subsequence; no one test detecting every possible
subsequence is asserted.

This is a full-observable $L^2$ weak-topology obstruction for exactly
the original finite-field adjoint outputs identified above. The
argument does not put its detecting observable in the original
closed derivative domain, nor make it smooth or bounded. Convergence
against a smaller graph-domain test class, a distributional topology,
a different integrand norm or a different compensated prescription
is not ruled out. In particular the spatial Simpson theorem and
its actual pathwise primitive remain intact.

\section{Strong source contractions without a full observable limit}
The preceding obstruction concerns the full outputs $I_N$. Certain
fixed tests nevertheless have strong limits after averaging only over
the scenery. We reconstruct these limits for actual quadratic
observations and bounded $C^2$ terminal tests. Throughout this section
the horizon is $T>0$, there are $N\geq1$ cells, $h=T/N$, and

\subsection{One common observation may anticipate the clock grid}
We first need a weighted Brownian bracket estimate that does not assume
predictability of the weights. For bounded Borel functions
$\phi_i:\R^2\to\R$, $|\phi_i|\leq1$, let
\[
 D_i=(B_{t_{i+1}}-B_{t_i})^2-h,\qquad
 a_i=\phi_i(B_{t_i},B_R),\qquad E_N=\sum_{i<N}D_i a_i.
\]
Then the exact source bound is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oneobsbracket}
 \mathbb E_B E_N^2\leq \frac{12T^2}{N}+\frac{16T^2}{\sqrt N}.
\end{equation}
The functions may vary with the cell and with $N$, and the same bound
even permits a different common observation time $R=R(N)$ for each grid.
It is not a theorem for arbitrary functions of the entire future path.

Here is a direct proof. For $s<t\leq R$, jointly Gaussian regression
onto $\mathcal F_s^B\vee\sigma(B_R)$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bridgecenteredsquare}
 \mathbb E_B[(B_t-B_s)^2-(t-s)\mid\mathcal F_s^B,B_R]
 =\left(\frac{t-s}{R-s}\right)^2
       \bigl((B_R-B_s)^2-(R-s)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Indeed $B_t-B_s$ is the sum of
$(t-s)(B_R-B_s)/(R-s)$ and a centered Gaussian residual independent
of the conditioning variables, of variance
$(t-s)-(t-s)^2/(R-s)$. Expanding the square proves the formula.
Its $L^2$ norm is $\sqrt2(t-s)^2/(R-s)$, at most twice that ratio.
If $R\leq s$, the corresponding conditional mean is zero by independent
increments. These statements use the actual clock and its original
Gaussian coupling.

\subsection{Capped random levels and a genuine mixed bracket}
For a nonnegative radius $r$ put $c_r(x)=\min(|x|,r)$. Define
\begin{align*}
 Q_N^{R,S}&=\sum_{i<N}
   [c_{|B_R|}(B_{t_{i+1}})-c_{|B_R|}(B_{t_i})]
   [c_{|B_S|}(B_{t_{i+1}})-c_{|B_S|}(B_{t_i})],\\
 O^{R,S}&=\int_0^T
   \mathbf1_{\{|B_t|<\min(|B_R|,|B_S|)\}}\,dt.
\end{align*}
These are actual functions of the original clock. We will prove
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixedcappedlimit}
 Q_N^{R,S}\longrightarrow O^{R,S}\quad\hbox{in }L^2(\mathbb P_B).
\end{equation}
Random radii here are not asserted to be stopping times.

First consider $R=S$. For each fixed $t\ne R$,
$\mathbb P_B(|B_t|=|B_R|)=0$: the event implies $B_t-B_R=0$ or
$B_t+B_R=0$, each a nondegenerate Gaussian equality. The single time
$t=R$ has zero Lebesgue measure. Fubini thus proves that the random
boundary $|B_t|=|B_R|$ has zero time occupation almost surely.
The origin $B_t=0$ also has zero time occupation by the same argument.
Continuity of each clock path now implies convergence of the bounded
left-node Riemann samples of
$\mathbf1_{\{|B_t|<|B_R|\}}$ to its time integral, and vanishing of
samples of shrinking bands about either boundary. To justify this
last statement, represent the sums by step functions on the cells.
At every time outside the null boundary their left nodes tend to that
time and the band indicators eventually vanish. Dominated convergence
first in time and then in clock probability proves the assertions in
$L^2$, since all sample sums lie between zero and $T$.

\subsection{Actual quadratic observations select a finite clock limit}
Retain the same compensated source grid $S_N$ as before. Conditionally
on the clock, Gaussian integration of a centered product gives
\[
 \mathbb E_W[(X(d_i)X(h_i)-\langle d_i,h_i\rangle)Z_RZ_S]
 =\langle d_i,h_R\rangle\langle h_i,h_S\rangle
  +\langle d_i,h_S\rangle\langle h_i,h_R\rangle.
\]
This is the two nonconstant pairings in the fourth Gaussian moment;
the constant pairing cancels the subtracted covariance. Since
$\langle h_i,h_R\rangle=\min(|B_{t_i}|,|B_R|)$, the elementary finite
product telescope yields the exact identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadraticsourcecontraction}
 \mathbb E_W[S_N Z_RZ_S]=M_N^{R,S}
 :=c_{|B_R|}(B_T)c_{|B_S|}(B_T)-Q_N^{R,S}.
\end{equation}
All finite products are integrable by Gaussian moments of the clock
and scenery. The endpoint product is in clock $L^2$, since it is
bounded in absolute value by $|B_T|^2$. It follows that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quadraticclocklimit}
 M_N^{R,S}\longrightarrow
 M^{R,S}:=c_{|B_R|}(B_T)c_{|B_S|}(B_T)-O^{R,S}
       \quad\hbox{in clock }L^2.

\[
 \mathbb E_B|a(M_N^{R,S}-M^{R,S})|
 \leq\|a\|_2\|M_N^{R,S}-M^{R,S}\|_2\longrightarrow0.
\]
This is an actual weighted $L^1$ conclusion, even for unbounded $a$.

\subsection{Bounded $C^2$ tests and the actual second-jet coefficient}

For the same fixed observation time $R$ define the actual coefficient
\[
 b_\varphi(B)=\frac12\mathbb E_W[\varphi''(Z_R)\mid B],
 \qquad |b_\varphi|\leq\tfrac12\|\varphi''\|_\infty.
\]
Here the notation means integration over the independent scenery with
the clock fixed; it is a genuine bounded clock $L^2$ class. There is
no assigned intrinsic derivative of $\varphi(Z_R)$ in this definition.

For deterministic Gaussian directions $d,h,k$, two scalar Gaussian
integrations by parts give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mixedStein}
 \mathbb E_W[(X(d)X(h)-\langle d,h\rangle)\varphi(X(k))]
 =\langle d,k\rangle\langle h,k\rangle
                   \mathbb E_W\varphi''(X(k)).
\end{equation}
For completeness, when $k\ne0$ decompose $d,h$ into their projections
onto $k$ and orthogonal residuals. The residuals are independent of
$X(k)$; their covariance cancels the residual part of
$\langle d,h\rangle$. The remaining term is
\[
 \frac{\langle d,k\rangle\langle h,k\rangle}{\|k\|^4}
 \mathbb E_W[(X(k)^2-\|k\|^2)\varphi(X(k))].
\]
Integrate twice against the scalar Gaussian density. Boundary terms
vanish because the function and its first derivative are bounded,
and the result is $\|k\|^4\mathbb E_W\varphi''(X(k))$.
All terms are integrable by the same bounds. If $k=0$, both sides
of \eqref{eq:mixedStein} vanish directly.
This also identifies the source second-chaos projection of
$\varphi(X(k))$ as
$\tfrac12\mathbb E_W\varphi''(X(k))(X(k)^2-\|k\|^2)$:
testing against every centered square verifies the identity, and
polarization makes their span dense in the second chaos.

\section{A faithful Banach domain for clock-valued source limits}
The fixed-test limits just proved do not give convergence against every
observable $L^2$ test. A separate completed test domain does support
continuous clock-$L^1$ limits of the original source contractions.
We construct that domain explicitly, including the norm and the
consistency needed to forget a test's spatial presentation.

\subsection{Spatial square kernels and their precise completed domain}
For $x,y\geq0$ let $J(x,y)=(\min(x,y),\max(x,y)]$, and for a bounded
continuous real function $F$ on $\R^2$ put
\[
 C_F(x,y)=\int_{J(x,y)^2}F(u,v)\,du\,dv.
\]
This rectangle is unoriented: its area is $(y-x)^2$ even when $y<x$.
Thus $|C_F(x,y)|\leq\|F\|_\infty(y-x)^2$. For Lipschitz $F$ with
constant $L$ in the maximum product metric,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spatialcellerror}
 |C_F(x,y)-F(x,x)(y-x)^2|\leq L|y-x|^3,
\end{equation}
since each point of the square is within $|y-x|$ of $(x,x)$.
Let $\mathcal C$ be the supremum-norm completion of the bounded
Lipschitz functions on $\R^2$. It embeds isometrically into
$C_b(\R^2)$: a norm-Cauchy sequence converges uniformly to a bounded
continuous function, with the same norm. This specifies the completed
domain used below; we make no identification with all of $C_b$.

The closed kernel $\mathcal K=\ker j$ can therefore be quotiented out.

\[
 \mathcal T=\mathcal G/\mathcal K,\qquad
 \|[p]\|_{\mathcal T}=\inf_{k\in\mathcal K}\|p+k\|_{\mathcal G}.
\]
It is Banach, and the induced realization
$J:\mathcal T\to\mathcal E$, $J[p]=jp$, is injective and satisfies
$\|Jx\|_2\leq\|x\|_{\mathcal T}$. Indeed $J[p]=0$ means
$p\in\mathcal K$, exactly the zero quotient class; the norm estimate

their specified quotient norm.

\section{What this integration result means}
The targets are genuine pathwise finite spatial or time integrals,
and the approximations converge under the actual positive
scenery--clock law. Here ``oriented'' refers to the signed spatial
increment $y-x$, not to a signed probability measure for $Z$.
This distinguishes the present setting from the higher-order
signed heat pseudoprocess calculus.

The proof is local in the spatial range. It admits unbounded
global $C^3$ one-forms and $C^4$ spatial slices because every
continuous sample path has a compact range. It provides
convergence in probability, not integrability of arbitrary
unbounded targets. Neither expectation convergence nor strong
$L^2$ convergence has been asserted without moment hypotheses.

It also does not construct a general physical-time adapted
stochastic integral. Its spatial one-form depends only on the
current spatial argument, and its time--space integrand comes
from a deterministic primitive $F$. The rule has not been
identified with the original intrinsic divergence or with an
operator acting on arbitrary predictable integrands. Bounded
random horizons are handled by approximation, not a martingale
stopping theorem. Nonsmooth It\^o--Tanaka formulas and arbitrary
partitions have their own proof obligations.

The native compensated escape concerns a specific projected finite-field sequence, not the symmetric Simpson prescription or every admissible integrand.

The variation and raw-channel results above are independent of the
Simpson proof, which needs only quartic tightness. Intrinsic
graph-dual obstructions remain separately scoped; no stochastic-domain
assertion is used to justify a deterministic quadrature estimate.
