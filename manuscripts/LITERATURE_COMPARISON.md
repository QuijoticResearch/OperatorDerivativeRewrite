## Arithmetic Fourier transforms

David A. Cardon, *Fourier transforms having only real zeros*, Proc. AMS 133 (2005), 1349–1356: the [author-hosted primary manuscript](https://mathdept.byu.edu/cardon/papers/FourierRealZeros.pdf) is now accessible. Proposition 1 and Theorem 1 were inspected directly, improving the earlier abstract-only comparison.

Proposition 1 is a universal-factor preservation result with an explicit Laguerre–Pólya premise. Theorem 1 instead uses normalized independent sign sums and a real entire multiplier of order below two. Neither statement may simply be invoked with the growing quartic arithmetic multiplier: its direct entire extension has order four and is outside those stated multiplier hypotheses. The sixth-order exponential extension likewise has order six. This rules out a particular unsupported theorem invocation; it does not establish that no other classical argument proves the arithmetic results. The nonintegral-order endpoint obstruction still needs direct comparison with the original Pólya papers, rather than the discovery survey alone.

Charles M. Newman, *Fourier transforms with only real zeros*, Proc. AMS 61 (1976), 245–251: [primary reprint](https://sites.math.northwestern.edu/~auffing/papers/Newman.pdf). Theorem 1 is quadratic Gaussian strip contraction; Theorem 2 classifies measures retaining real Fourier zeros after every negative quadratic tilt. A density containing a decaying quartic in that classification is not the growing quartic deformation of the arithmetic theta kernel. The sign, varying parameter and quantified class of measures differ. These primary results justify the manuscripts' insistence that quadratic propagation cannot be imported into an eventual quartic-start definition.

The original de Bruijn paper was located in the TU Delft repository, but the primary file fetch timed out. Its detailed theorem comparison therefore remains incomplete. No positive novelty finding or exhaustive negative literature claim follows from these comparisons.

## Nonlinear Gaussian and fractional prediction

McElroy and Das, *Nonlinear prediction via Hermite transformation*, Statistical Theory and Related Fields 5 (2021), 49–54: [primary article](https://doi.org/10.1080/24754269.2020.1856589). Its indexed primary introduction explicitly places Hermite predictor and mean-square-error formulas in existing Gaussian transformation prediction. Direct full-text access returned 403 and the alternate journal PDF timed out. The manuscript credits this mechanism without claiming a detailed theorem-number comparison.

Sottinen and Viitasaari, *Prediction law of fractional Brownian motion*, Statist. Probab. Lett. 129 (2017), 155–166: [primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.02900). Theorem 3.1 and Proposition 3.2 were inspected directly. Their conditional law and small-past covariance asymptotics already contain the two fractional power regimes. The Stardust companion reconstructs a weaker explicit lower bound, full terminal L2 rank transfer and inverse-number preservation. It does not advertise the exponents or conditional Gaussianity as new.

## Fluid diagnostics

Cantwell's [primary restricted-Euler article](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.858295) explicitly distinguishes the full velocity-gradient dynamics, strain and rotation. The exact self-similar matrix family is situated in that classical solution theory. Yang, Xu, Pumir and He's [primary 2024 pressure-Hessian article](https://doi.org/10.1017/jfm.2024.143), equations 1.1/1.2 and its deviatoric definition, fixes the pressure conventions. The diagnostic note does not allege that those equations contain the tested trace or sign error. Xin, Yu and Zlatoš's [primary ABC model statement](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.02724) supplies the established unit profile; no orbit theorem is used.
