## Conventions that must be fixed first

There are two different operators: the density PDE operator and its transpose on observables. If the density satisfies ∂t K = c ∂x^p K, integration by parts gives the observable generator **(-1)^p c ∂x^p**, not c ∂x^p at odd p. The manuscript drops this sign in `prop:obs-ito`. Its spectral moment convention E[X^(pn)] = (pn)! (c t)^n/n! instead treats c as the observable-generator coefficient. These conventions agree at even orders only. A repair must name coefficients separately, say b for observables and c=(-1)^p b for densities. The coefficient of (iξ)^p in a characteristic function also depends on the Fourier sign convention; fix this before using any Airy formula.

Even at p=2, the initial choice c2=1 produces variance2t, whereas later formulas silently switch to c2=1/2 and variance t. The displayed Airy expression also changes normalization later (c3=1/3). With b the observable coefficient, the algebraic cumulant is κp=p! b t; the extra i^p in the setup's cumulant sentence belongs to the characteristic-variable expansion, not to the real cumulant itself.

## Reconstruction repair under investigation

The algebra suggests a more precise replacement than simply discarding the depth-p term. If A_n(x,t) has generating series exp(λx−btλ^p), then a polynomial F has coefficients b_n=E[F^(n)(X_T)]/n! in its terminal Appell expansion. Under an actually constructed compatible chronological integral, conditioning the pth derivative and integrating p times should recover **only the Appell tail Σ_(n≥p) b_n A_n**, leaving all terms of degree1 through p−1 in addition to the mean. This explains why the advertised examples X_T^(pn) can succeed while the universal claim fails. For example, p=4 and F=x^5 leave the nonzero lower term 120bT x; p=2 and F=x^3 leave 6bT x.

This is a candidate algebraic repair, not a completed signed stochastic representation. Next steps are exact formal Appell-tail identities, independent checking of the conditional/integration recursion, and a legitimate topology and chronological operator on which the identities hold. In particular, a formal tail formula must not be labeled a completed construction of δ_p.
