## Objective and research method

Investigate autonomously whether the cumulative verified Stardust library
contains an unexpected mathematical principle capable of improving AI. Explore
connections to neural architectures, optimization, attention, memory,
generative modeling, inference and computational complexity. These are possible
directions, not a requirement to visit every branch or manufacture an AI use.
Prioritize substantive mathematical connections over incremental engineering.

For each promising direction:

1. Identify exact reusable verified declarations, their source modules,
   hypotheses, domains and limitations. An analogy alone is not theorem reuse.
2. Derive a concrete mathematical construction, algorithm or architecture.
3. Establish rigorous properties where feasible, formalizing useful results in
   Lean without weakening existing statements or adding unproved assumptions.
4. Audit relevant published research and compare against known AI methods
   before claiming novelty or advantage; record exact sources and comparisons.
5. State what computational advantage, if any, could follow, with explicit
   computational model, accuracy criterion, assumptions and resource costs.
6. Develop minimal computational experiments when they answer a mathematical
   question. Record reproducible settings and distinguish evidence from proof.
7. Attempt to disprove claimed advantages through counterexamples, competing
   baselines, limiting cases and failure tests before presenting discoveries.

Choose productive directions independently. Record failed directions and why
they failed; avoid repeated investigation without a substantive new reason.
Distinguish established mathematics, new formally verified theorems,
conjectures and experimentally observed improvements. A formal theorem about an
abstract construction does not establish empirical AI performance. A rigorous
obstruction or well-supported absence of advantage is a valid stopping result.
